    Lasswell's Communication Model

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Lasswell’s formula—Who says What, in Which Channel, to Whom, with What Effect—decomposes mass communication into five analyzable elements. It is a functional, linear framework that helps researchers and practitioners specify sources, messages, media, audiences, and outcomes in a way that is operationalizable and measurable. The model’s strengths are clarity and auditability; its limitations are the simplification of feedback, context, and meaning negotiation. Pairing Lasswell with feedback-oriented and production-oriented models (e.g., Osgood–Schramm, Westley–MacLean, Gatekeeping, Framing) increases explanatory power.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A quasi-theoretical framework that structures communication into five elements to analyze process → outcomes.
    • Scope: Advertising, PR, public information, political communication, health campaigns, and other mass/digital initiatives.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    • Who (Source): identity, credibility, authority, likability.
    • What (Message): claim, evidence, framing, tone/style, emotion, call-to-action.
    • Which Channel: TV/radio/print/digital/social/OOH; reach, frequency, richness.
    • To Whom (Audience): segments (demo/psycho/behavioral), involvement, prior attitudes.
    • With What Effect: knowledge, attitudes, intentions, behaviors, discourse change.

    Propositions¶

    • P1: Effects are conditional on interactions among source, message, channel, and audience characteristics.
    • P2: The more functionally aligned the message and channel are to the targeted effect, the more efficient the campaign.

    4) Model Diagram¶

    flowchart LR
  Who[Source / Who] --> What[Message / What]
  What --> Channel[Which Channel]
  Channel --> Whom[Audience / To Whom]
  Whom --> Effect[Outcomes / What Effect]

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    • Linearity: One-way transmission emphasis (feedback simplified).
    • Context reduction: Culture, power, and discourse structures are minimized.
    • Operationalizability: Each element must be measurable to enable empirical testing.

    6) Operationalization & Measurement¶

    Element Typical Operationalization Example Items/Indicators Notes
    Who (Source) Credibility (expertise, trust, attractiveness) “This source is trustworthy (1–7)”; aided/unaided awareness; follower counts Use Ohanian’s source credibility scales
    What (Message) Argument strength, evidence quality, framing, tone Manipulate argument quality (strong/weak); gain/loss framing; narrative vs. non-narrative Pair with ELM/framing designs
    Channel Reach, frequency, richness, shareability GRP/TRP, impressions, CTR, completion rate, share rate Consider media richness/synchronicity
    To Whom (Audience) Segments, involvement, prior attitudes Involvement scale, Need for Cognition, baseline attitude Define targeting variables clearly
    Effect Knowledge/attitude/intent/behavior/discourse Quiz accuracy, Likert attitude, purchase/vote intent, actual click/purchase/signup Tier KPIs: short/medium/long term
    Noise & Feedback Interference, misunderstanding, response Response time, correction rate, inquiries, comment sentiment Complement with Osgood–Schramm / Westley–MacLean

    Measurement Tips¶

    • Use pre–post designs for change scores (Δ).
    • Build a cascade of outcomes: cognition → affect → intention → behavior.
    • Combine exposure logs (impressions, reach) with behavioral logs (clicks, shares).

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Common designs¶

    • Content analysis + exposure-linked survey: Code message features (framing/emotion/evidence) and link to respondents’ exposure, attitudes, and behaviors.
    • Randomized experiments (A/B/n): Manipulate source, message, and channel; measure attitudes, intentions, memory, or behavior.
    • Time-series/panel: Track pre–during–post campaign effects; estimate decay or persistence.
    • Multilevel models: Individuals nested in media markets/regions over time.

    Analytic techniques¶

    • OLS/logit regression (with interactions), ANOVA/ANCOVA.
    • Mediation/moderation (PROCESS/SEM), ARIMA/ITS, difference-in-differences (DiD).
    • Media Mix Modeling (MMM) to estimate channel contribution.

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    • Conditional effects: Heterogeneity by involvement, prior attitudes, and segments.
    • Message–channel fit: High-involvement topics favor richer channels and strong arguments.
    • Source credibility: More influential under low involvement/knowledge.
    • Reach–frequency balance: Over-frequency risks fatigue; seek optimal exposures.
    • For high-cost behaviors, add prompts/reminders/social proof to reduce friction.

    9) Applications¶

    Campaign design checklist¶

    1. Define prioritized outcomes (knowledge/attitude/behavior).
    2. Who: choose sources (expert/peer/institution) and specify credibility proofs.
    3. What: claims, evidence, framing, CTA; include ethical guardrails.
    4. Channel: balance reach vs. precision; design cross-channel journeys.
    5. To Whom: specify personas/segments; incorporate language/accessibility.
    6. Effect: KPI matrix (short/medium/long); integrate logs + surveys in measurement.

    Domain hints¶

    • Advertising: High involvement → strong evidence/comparatives; low involvement → cues/simplicity.
    • PR/policy: Consistent, credible institutional voice; in crisis, be fast, factual, and empathetic.
    • Health: Combine with HBM/EPPM to balance risk and efficacy; remove practical barriers.

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Comparator Core Difference How It Complements Lasswell
    Shannon–Weaver Engineering focus on information/Noise/Capacity Adds noise/coding precision
    Osgood–Schramm Feedback and shared meaning Designs explicit feedback loops
    Westley–MacLean Environmental objects (X) and Gatekeepers (C) Captures mediated/algorithmic curation
    Two-Step Flow Interpersonal mediation via opinion leaders Adds channel + interpersonal pathways
    Agenda-Setting Salience shaping of public agendas Adds public agenda to effect metrics

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    • Linear/instrumental reduction: Over-simplifies meaning, culture, and power.
    • Feedback omission: Under-specifies interaction and network diffusion.
    • Effect bias: Emphasizes outcomes while minimizing production/reception power relations.
    • Platform era: Algorithms, recommender systems, and UGC create multi-path flows.

    Response: Use Lasswell as an audit/design checklist, but implement/interpret with feedback (Osgood–Schramm), gatekeeping (Westley–MacLean), and meaning/power frameworks (Framing, Political Economy).

    12) Cross-Cultural, Ethics, DEI¶

    • Cultural variation: The weight of authority/credibility cues differs by power distance/collectivism.
    • Language & accessibility: Multilingual/plain language; captions and alt text.
    • Representation: Avoid stereotyping; ensure diverse imagery/examples.
    • Ethics: Transparent sourcing; avoid manipulative practices (e.g., deepfakes, misinformation).

    13) Implementation Notes¶

    Pre-launch audit (Lasswell-aligned)¶

    1. Specify goals, audience, timeline, budget → build a KPI tree.
    2. Map Who–What–Channel–Whom (segments × channels × messages).
    3. Pre-research: involvement, prior attitudes, media use.
    4. Pretests: PME, cognitive load, comprehension, trust.
    5. Launch with logs/surveys/panels collected in parallel.
    6. Mid-course checks (frequency/reach/performance) → optimize message/channel.
    7. Post analysis and reporting (effect sizes, heterogeneity, lessons).

    14) Data & Code¶

    Example variables¶

    • source_cred (1–7), frame_gain (1 = gain; 0 = loss)
    • channel (TV/online/OOH), aud_involve (involvement)
    • att_post (attitude), intent (1–7), behavior (0/1)
    • exposure (impressions), controls (age, gender, etc.)

    R (logit for behavior)¶

    glm_fit <- glm(
  behavior ~ source_cred*frame_gain + channel + aud_involve + att_pre +
             exposure + age + gender,
  data = df, family = binomial()
)
summary(glm_fit)

    Python (OLS for attitude change)¶

    import statsmodels.formula.api as smf
model = smf.ols(
    "att_post ~ source_cred*frame_gain + C(channel) + aud_involve + att_pre + exposure + age + gender",
    data=df
).fit()
print(model.summary())

    • FRAME (gain/loss/fear/identity)
    • CTA (explicit/implicit/none)
    • TONE (informational/emotional)

    15) Teaching Aids¶

    10-minute activity¶

    • Map a current campaign onto Who–What–Channel–Whom–Effect; propose two improvements (one message, one channel).

    Discussion questions (5)¶

    1. Give a real case where channel selection shifts the effect of a constant message.
    2. Beyond surveys, how might you infer source credibility from behavior data?
    3. Which outcome (knowledge/attitude/behavior) is most ethical and practical to target, and why?
    4. Which feedback-based model best complements Lasswell in digital contexts?
    5. Where do cultural differences most strongly alter the five elements?

    Quiz (3 MCQ + 2 short answer)¶

    1. Order the five Lasswell elements correctly.
    2. Which element includes “source credibility,” and name one validated scale.
    3. Name three metrics to consider when choosing a channel.
    4. (Short) Contrast strategies for high- vs. low-involvement audiences.
    5. (Short) State two limitations of Lasswell and one complementary theory for each.
