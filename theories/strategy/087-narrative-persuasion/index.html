    Narrative Persuasion

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Narrative Persuasion Theory explains how stories can change attitudes and behaviors more effectively than traditional persuasive messages by engaging audiences through psychological transportation into narrative worlds. Developed by Green and Brock (2000), the theory identifies transportation as the key mechanism - a psychological state where individuals become fully absorbed in a story, losing awareness of their surroundings and experiencing the narrative as if they were part of it. When transported, people show reduced counterarguing, increased emotional engagement, and stronger identification with story characters, leading to attitude change consistent with story themes. The theory distinguishes between analytical processing (systematic evaluation of arguments) and experiential processing (emotional, immersive engagement with narratives). Transportation occurs through three main components: emotional engagement (feeling emotions consistent with story events), mental imagery (vivid visualization of story scenes), and attentional focus (complete absorption in the narrative). Effective narrative persuasion requires high-quality storytelling, relatable characters, realistic scenarios, and implicit rather than explicit persuasive messages. The approach is particularly powerful because it bypasses traditional persuasion knowledge and resistance mechanisms that audiences typically employ when encountering obvious persuasive attempts. Applications span health communication, social marketing, political campaigns, and corporate messaging, with research showing narratives can be more effective than statistical evidence or logical arguments for changing beliefs and behaviors across diverse audiences and topics.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theoretical framework explaining how narrative stories produce persuasion through transportation - a psychological state of absorption that reduces critical thinking and increases emotional engagement, leading to attitude and behavior change consistent with story themes.
    • Scope: All forms of story-based persuasive communication including written narratives, films, testimonials, case studies, advertising stories, health communication campaigns, and any persuasive message that uses narrative structure and characters to convey its central argument.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Transportation Mechanism¶

    Psychological Transportation¶

    Definition: A convergent process where all mental systems and capacities become focused on events occurring in the narrative

    Core Components: - Emotional Engagement: Experiencing emotions consistent with story events and character experiences - Mental Imagery: Vivid mental visualization of story scenes, settings, and character actions - Attentional Focus: Complete absorption in the narrative with reduced awareness of external environment - Empathetic Connection: Feeling concern, sympathy, or identification with story characters

    Transportation Process: 1. Narrative Exposure: Individual encounters story-based message 2. Initial Engagement: Story elements capture attention and interest 3. Immersion Development: Growing absorption in narrative world 4. Transportation State: Full psychological engagement with reduced critical evaluation 5. Attitude Integration: Story-consistent attitudes incorporated into belief system

    Measurement Indicators: - Loss of time awareness during story consumption - Emotional responses to character experiences - Vivid mental imagery of story events - Reduced attention to external stimuli - Feeling of being "inside" the story world

    Dual Processing Model¶

    Definition: Transportation represents experiential processing that operates alongside but can override analytical processing

    Analytical Processing: - Systematic Evaluation: Careful consideration of argument quality and evidence - Counterarguing: Generation of thoughts opposing persuasive messages - Source Evaluation: Assessment of communicator credibility and motives - Critical Thinking: Logical analysis of claims and supporting evidence

    Experiential Processing: - Emotional Responses: Immediate affective reactions to story elements - Vivid Imagery: Rich sensory mental representations of narrative events - Character Identification: Psychological merging with story protagonists - Immersive Experience: Feeling of presence within the story world

    Processing Competition: - Transportation reduces capacity for analytical processing - Immersive narrative experience inhibits counterargument generation - Emotional engagement overrides systematic evaluation - Story absorption leads to acceptance of embedded persuasive messages

    Character Identification¶

    Identification Process¶

    Definition: Psychological merging with story characters where audiences adopt character perspectives, goals, and emotional states

    Types of Identification: - Empathetic Identification: Feeling emotions consistent with character experiences - Cognitive Identification: Adopting character's thoughts, beliefs, and perspectives - Motivational Identification: Sharing character's goals and desired outcomes - Behavioral Identification: Wanting to act similar to admired characters

    Identification Factors: - Similarity: Perceived demographic, attitudinal, or experiential similarity to characters - Attractiveness: Physical, social, or personality appeal of characters - Positive Outcomes: Characters who achieve desirable goals or rewards - Moral Alignment: Characters whose values match audience moral frameworks

    Character Development Impact¶

    Protagonist Characteristics: - Relatability: Characters with familiar backgrounds, challenges, or aspirations - Competence: Characters who demonstrate skills, knowledge, or capability - Likability: Characters with positive personality traits and social appeal - Moral Standing: Characters who embody ethical principles valued by audience

    Character Arc Influence: - Transformation Narratives: Characters who change in story-consistent directions - Obstacle Overcoming: Characters who successfully navigate challenges - Learning Journey: Characters who gain insights or develop new perspectives - Relationship Development: Characters who form meaningful connections

    Narrative Structure Effects¶

    Story Elements¶

    Plot Development: - Conflict Introduction: Problems that create tension and maintain attention - Rising Action: Escalating challenges that increase emotional investment - Climax Resolution: Satisfying conclusion that reinforces key messages - Causal Coherence: Logical sequence of events that maintains believability

    Setting Characteristics: - Realistic Environments: Familiar or believable contexts that enhance immersion - Detailed Descriptions: Rich sensory information that promotes mental imagery - Cultural Relevance: Settings that resonate with target audience experiences - Temporal Framework: Time structure that supports narrative flow

    Dialogue and Voice: - Authentic Communication: Natural speech patterns and conversational style - Emotional Expression: Verbal communication that conveys character feelings - Perspective Consistency: Maintain narrative voice throughout story - Persuasive Integration: Subtle message incorporation through character speech

    Message Integration¶

    Implicit Persuasion: - Theme Development: Central messages emerge through story events rather than direct statements - Character Modeling: Persuasive behaviors demonstrated through character actions - Consequence Portrayal: Story outcomes that illustrate benefits of advocated behaviors - Value Integration: Persuasive messages embedded in character value systems

    Explicit vs. Implicit Approaches: - High Explicit: Direct persuasive appeals reduce transportation and increase resistance - Moderate Explicit: Some direct messaging balanced with narrative elements - Low Explicit: Persuasive messages primarily conveyed through story implications - Optimal Integration: Seamless blending of persuasive content with narrative structure

    Resistance Reduction¶

    Counterarguing Inhibition¶

    Mechanism: Transportation reduces cognitive capacity available for generating arguments against persuasive messages

    Reduced Critical Evaluation: - Attention Allocation: Mental resources focused on story rather than argument evaluation - Emotional Override: Affective responses prevent systematic message processing - Immersion Effects: Being "in" the story world reduces external perspective - Time Pressure: Story pace limits opportunity for reflection and analysis

    Resistance Mechanisms Bypassed: - Persuasion Knowledge: Transportation reduces awareness of persuasive intent - Source Derogation: Character focus reduces attention to message source credibility - Selective Exposure: Story engagement maintains attention despite challenging content - Defensive Processing: Emotional involvement reduces motivated rejection of information

    Individual Differences¶

    Transportation Propensity¶

    High Transportation Individuals: - Absorption Tendency: Natural ability to become immersed in experiences - Imagination Vividness: Strong capacity for mental imagery and visualization - Empathetic Sensitivity: High responsiveness to others' emotional experiences - Narrative Preference: Enjoyment of and attraction to story-based content

    Low Transportation Individuals: - Analytical Orientation: Preference for systematic, logical information processing - Skeptical Disposition: Tendency to question and critically evaluate messages - External Focus: Difficulty ignoring environmental distractions during story consumption - Narrative Resistance: Preference for factual rather than story-based information

    Demographic and Cultural Factors: - Age Effects: Younger audiences typically show higher transportation susceptibility - Cultural Variations: Different narrative traditions affect transportation patterns - Individual Differences: Personality traits moderate transportation and persuasion effects

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Transportation into narratives reduces counterarguing and increases persuasion

    P2: Emotional engagement, mental imagery, and attentional focus combine to create transportation

    P3: Character identification enhances transportation and attitude change effects

    P4: Implicit persuasive messages integrated into narratives are more effective than explicit appeals

    P5: Individual differences in transportation propensity moderate narrative persuasion effects

    P6: High-quality storytelling elements increase transportation and persuasion outcomes

    P7: Transportation effects persist over time and influence both attitudes and behaviors

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Cognitive Capacity: Individuals have limited mental resources that can be allocated to either analytical or experiential processing
    • Transportation Ability: People vary in their natural ability to become psychologically transported into narratives
    • Story Engagement: High-quality narratives with compelling characters and plots are necessary for transportation
    • Message Integration: Persuasive content must be meaningfully integrated into story structure rather than superficially added
    • Emotional Accessibility: Audiences must be capable of emotional responses to story content

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Extreme Attitudes: Strong pre-existing attitudes may resist narrative persuasion effects
    • High Expertise: Domain experts may maintain analytical processing despite transportation
    • Cultural Barriers: Narratives that violate cultural norms may fail to transport audiences
    • Attention Limitations: Severe distractions or cognitive load may prevent transportation
    • Story Quality Threshold: Poorly constructed narratives fail to achieve transportation effects
    • Relevance Requirements: Stories must have some connection to audience experiences or concerns

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental Design Framework¶

    Basic Transportation Manipulation: - Independent Variable: Story Quality (3 levels: High-quality narrative, Low-quality narrative, Non-narrative control) - Dependent Variables: Transportation scores, attitude change, behavioral intentions, counterarguing - Control Variables: Prior attitudes, transportation propensity, topic involvement

    Procedure: 1. Pre-test Assessment: Baseline attitude measures and individual difference assessments 2. Random Assignment: Participants assigned to narrative vs. control conditions 3. Message Exposure: Participants read story or non-narrative message 4. Transportation Measurement: Immediate assessment of transportation experience 5. Post-test Measures: Attitude change, behavioral intentions, thought listing

    Longitudinal Study Protocol¶

    Extended Effect Tracking: - Baseline (Week 0): Initial attitude and behavior measures - Intervention (Week 1): Narrative exposure with transportation assessment - Short-term Follow-up (Week 2): Initial attitude change persistence - Medium-term Follow-up (Month 1): Behavioral intention stability - Long-term Follow-up (Month 3): Actual behavior change assessment

    Data Collection Framework: 

    Pre-Exposure Measures:
- Baseline attitudes toward story topic
- Transportation propensity scale
- Demographic and individual difference measures
- Prior experience with story topic

Post-Exposure Measures:
- Transportation scale scores
- Attitude change measures
- Character identification assessments
- Counterarguing thought listings
- Behavioral intention measures

Follow-up Assessments:
- Attitude persistence measures
- Actual behavior tracking
- Memory for story content
- Attribution of attitude change

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Transportation Effects¶

    Transportation-Persuasion Relationship: - High Transportation: 40-60% greater attitude change compared to low transportation - Medium Transportation: 20-30% greater attitude change than control conditions - Low Transportation: Minimal attitude change, similar to non-narrative controls - Individual Variation: Effect sizes range from r = .25 to r = .65 depending on story quality

    Story Quality Impact: - Professional Narratives: Higher transportation and stronger persuasion effects - Personal Testimonials: Strong identification but variable story quality effects - Fictional vs. Non-fiction: Both effective when well-constructed, fiction may allow more creative freedom - Length Effects: Longer stories allow more transportation development but risk attention loss

    Character Identification Patterns¶

    Identification Predictors: - Demographic Similarity: 25-35% increase in identification when characters match audience demographics - Attitude Similarity: 30-40% stronger identification when characters share audience values - Aspirational Characters: 20-25% identification boost when characters represent audience goals - Character Likability: Strong positive correlation (r = .45-.65) between likability and identification

    Identification Consequences: - Attitude Adoption: Participants adopt attitudes consistent with identified characters - Behavioral Modeling: Increased likelihood of imitating character behaviors - Value Activation: Character values become more salient and accessible - Emotional Contagion: Audience experiences emotions similar to characters

    Resistance Reduction Mechanisms¶

    Counterarguing Suppression: - High Transportation: 50-70% reduction in counterarguing compared to analytical processing - Attention Focus: Mental resources allocated to story comprehension rather than evaluation - Emotional Engagement: Affective responses interfere with systematic message processing - Time Constraints: Story pace limits opportunity for critical evaluation

    Persuasion Knowledge Bypassing: - Reduced Skepticism: Transportation decreases awareness of persuasive intent - Source Credibility: Character focus shifts attention from message source evaluation - Defense Lowering: Immersion reduces motivation to resist persuasive content - Natural Acceptance: Story-embedded messages feel less like persuasion attempts

    Individual Difference Moderators¶

    Transportation Propensity Effects: - High Propensity: 40-50% stronger transportation and persuasion effects - Moderate Propensity: Standard transportation patterns with story quality dependence - Low Propensity: Reduced transportation but still some persuasion through other mechanisms - Measurement Stability: Transportation propensity shows good test-retest reliability

    Demographic Variations: - Age Effects: Younger adults (18-25) show 20-30% higher transportation than older adults - Gender Differences: Women show slightly higher transportation for relationship-focused stories - Education Impact: Higher education associated with more selective transportation - Cultural Background: Collectivistic cultures show stronger transportation for group-oriented narratives

    9) Applications¶

    Health Communication¶

    Disease Prevention Narratives: - Patient Stories: First-person accounts of health behavior change and outcomes - Family Impact: Stories showing how individual health affects loved ones - Community Testimonials: Narratives demonstrating social benefits of health behaviors - Provider Experiences: Healthcare worker perspectives on prevention importance

    Treatment Adherence Stories: - Success Journeys: Characters who benefit from following treatment recommendations - Obstacle Navigation: Realistic portrayals of adherence challenges and solutions - Support Systems: Stories highlighting importance of social support in treatment - Long-term Outcomes: Narratives showing extended benefits of adherence

    Implementation Strategies: 

    def design_health_narrative(health_behavior, target_demographic):
    """Framework for health communication narratives"""

    narrative_components = {
        'character_profile': {
            'demographics': f'Match target audience: age, gender, socioeconomic status',
            'health_status': f'Initial condition requiring {health_behavior}',
            'personality': 'Relatable traits that audience can identify with',
            'social_context': 'Family, work, and community relationships'
        },

        'plot_structure': {
            'health_challenge': f'Problem that motivates {health_behavior} adoption',
            'decision_process': 'Character reasoning through behavior change',
            'implementation': f'Realistic steps for adopting {health_behavior}',
            'outcomes': 'Positive consequences of health behavior change'

        'persuasive_elements': {
            'efficacy_modeling': f'Character successfully performs {health_behavior}',
            'outcome_expectations': 'Demonstrate benefits of behavior change',
            'barrier_management': 'Show how to overcome common obstacles',
            'social_support': 'Include supportive relationships and resources'
        }
    }

    return narrative_components

    Social Marketing Campaigns¶

    Environmental Behavior Change: - Individual Impact Stories: Personal narratives showing environmental action effects - Community Transformation: Stories of neighborhoods improving through collective action - Future Scenarios: Narratives depicting positive environmental outcomes - Youth Leadership: Stories of young people leading environmental change

    Social Issue Awareness: - Personal Experience: First-person accounts of social problems and solutions - Perspective Taking: Stories that help audiences understand different viewpoints - Action Modeling: Characters who take concrete steps to address social issues - Collective Efficacy: Narratives showing how individuals can create change

    Digital Media Applications¶

    Social Media Storytelling: - Micro-Narratives: Brief stories optimized for platform constraints - Visual Storytelling: Image and video-based narrative content - User-Generated Stories: Encouraging audiences to share their own narratives - Serial Narratives: Extended stories delivered across multiple posts

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Narrative Persuasion Key Differences
    Elaboration Likelihood Model Dual-route persuasion processing Both address different processing modes ELM focuses on central vs. peripheral routes; NP emphasizes experiential processing
    Social Cognitive Theory Observational learning and modeling Both use character modeling for behavior change SCT broader behavioral theory; NP specifically about story-based persuasion
    Persuasion Knowledge Model Audience awareness of persuasive intent NP helps bypass persuasion knowledge activation PKM about resistance; NP about overcoming resistance through transportation
    Inoculation Theory Building resistance to persuasion NP seeks to overcome resistance; Inoculation builds it Opposite goals: NP reduces critical thinking, Inoculation enhances it

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Limited Real-World Generalization: Most narrative persuasion research uses laboratory settings with controlled story exposure, raising questions about effects in natural media consumption environments where audiences may be distracted or skeptical

    Transportation Measurement Challenges: Self-report transportation measures may not capture the full psychological experience and could be influenced by social desirability or retrospective bias

    Individual Difference Complexity: Theory may oversimplify how personal characteristics, prior attitudes, and cultural backgrounds interact to influence transportation and persuasion outcomes

    Ethical Concerns: Narrative persuasion's ability to bypass critical thinking raises questions about manipulation, informed consent, and potential for misuse in persuasive communication

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Media Effects: - Attention Fragmentation: How do digital distractions affect transportation in online narrative environments? - Interactive Narratives: Do video games and interactive stories create different transportation mechanisms? - Social Media Stories: Can brief narrative formats on social platforms achieve meaningful transportation effects? - Algorithm Personalization: How does AI-driven content personalization affect narrative persuasion?

    Cultural and Cross-Cultural Issues: - Narrative Traditions: Do transportation effects vary across cultures with different storytelling traditions? - Representation Concerns: How do narrative choices about character demographics affect audience identification? - Global vs. Local: Should international campaigns use universal narratives or culturally-specific stories? - Digital Divide: How do technology access differences affect narrative persuasion opportunities?

    Ethical and Methodological Concerns: - Informed Consent: Should audiences be warned about persuasive intent in narrative messages? - Manipulation Boundaries: When does effective narrative persuasion cross ethical lines? - Long-term Effects: What are the sustained impacts of repeated narrative persuasion exposure? - Vulnerable Populations: Are certain groups more susceptible to narrative manipulation?

    Theoretical Developments¶

    Extended Transportation Models: Contemporary research examines how transportation operates across different media formats and interactive environments

    Cultural Adaptation Frameworks: Development of culturally-sensitive narrative persuasion approaches that respect diverse storytelling traditions

    Ethical Guidelines: Emerging standards for responsible use of narrative persuasion in health, political, and commercial communication

    Digital Integration Models: New frameworks incorporating social media, virtual reality, and interactive technologies into narrative persuasion theory

    12) Validated Survey Measures¶

    Transportation Scale (Green & Brock, 2000)¶

    Citation: Green, M. C., & Brock, T. C. (2000). The role of transportation in the persuasiveness of public narratives. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 79(5), 701-721. Reliability: α = .87

    Sample Items: 1. "I was mentally involved in the narrative while reading it" (1-7 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "I found myself thinking of ways the narrative could have turned out differently" (1-7) 3. "I found my mind wandering while reading the narrative" (1-7, reverse coded) 4. "The narrative affected me emotionally" (1-7) 5. "I had vivid images of the characters in the narrative" (1-7)

    Character Identification Scale (Cohen, 2001)¶

    Citation: Cohen, J. (2001). Defining identification: A theoretical look at the identification of audiences with media characters. Mass Communication and Society, 4(3), 245-264. Reliability: α = .91

    Sample Items: 1. "I felt I could really get inside [character's] head" (1-5) 2. "I was able to understand the events in the story through [character's] eyes" (1-5) 3. "I think I have a good understanding of [character]" (1-5) 4. "I felt as though I was friends with [character]" (1-5)

    Narrative Engagement Scale (Busselle & Bilandzic, 2009)¶

    Citation: Busselle, R., & Bilandzic, H. (2009). Measuring narrative engagement. Media Psychology, 12(4), 321-347. Reliability: α = .85

    Subscales: - Narrative Understanding: "I had a hard time recognizing the thread of the story" (reverse) - Attentional Focus: "My attention was fully captured by the story" - Emotional Engagement: "The story affected me emotionally" - Narrative Presence: "I felt as though I was part of the action"

    Transportation Propensity Scale (Dal Cin et al., 2004)¶

    Citation: Dal Cin, S., Zanna, M. P., & Fong, G. T. (2004). Narrative persuasion and overcoming resistance. In E. S. Knowles & J. A. Linn (Eds.), Resistance and persuasion (pp. 175-191). Lawrence Erlbaum. Reliability: α = .79

    Sample Items: 1. "I easily become involved in the stories I read or watch" (1-7) 2. "I find myself thinking about the stories I've read throughout the day" (1-7) 3. "Stories help me better understand life's complexities" (1-7) 4. "I prefer factual information to stories" (1-7, reverse coded)

    13) Implementation Notes¶

    Story Development Protocol¶

    Character Creation Framework: 

    def develop_persuasive_character(target_audience, persuasion_goal):
    """Systematic approach to character development for narrative persuasion"""

    character_profile = {
        'demographic_matching': {
            'age_range': 'Within 5-10 years of target audience median',
            'socioeconomic': 'Match or slightly aspirational to audience',
            'geographic': 'Familiar locations and cultural contexts',
            'occupation': 'Relatable or aspirational career paths'
        },

        'personality_traits': {
            'likability': 'Warm, friendly, and approachable characteristics',
            'competence': 'Demonstrate relevant skills and knowledge',
            'authenticity': 'Realistic flaws and genuine emotional responses',
            'moral_standing': 'Values that align with audience ethical frameworks'
        },

    import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

def analyze_narrative_persuasion(study_data):
    """
    Comprehensive analysis of narrative persuasion experimental data

    Parameters:
    study_data (DataFrame): Data with transportation, identification, and attitude measures

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results including effect sizes and recommendations
    """

    # Calculate key outcome measures
    study_data['attitude_change'] = (
        study_data['post_attitude'] - study_data['pre_attitude']
    )
    study_data['transportation_total'] = study_data['transportation_scale'].fillna(0)
    study_data['identification_score'] = study_data['character_identification'].fillna(0)

    # Group comparisons: narrative vs. control
    narrative_group = study_data[study_data['condition'] == 'narrative']
    control_group = study_data[study_data['condition'] == 'control']

    # Basic descriptive statistics
    analysis_results = {
        'sample_size': len(study_data),
        'narrative_n': len(narrative_group),
        'control_n': len(control_group)
    }

    # Transportation effects analysis
    if len(narrative_group) > 0:
        analysis_results['transportation_stats'] = {
            'mean_transportation': narrative_group['transportation_total'].mean(),
            'std_transportation': narrative_group['transportation_total'].std(),
            'high_transport_threshold': narrative_group['transportation_total'].quantile(0.75)
        }

        # Split narrative group by transportation level
        high_transport = narrative_group[
            narrative_group['transportation_total'] >= 
            analysis_results['transportation_stats']['high_transport_threshold']
        ]
        low_transport = narrative_group[
            narrative_group['transportation_total'] < 
            analysis_results['transportation_stats']['high_transport_threshold']
        ]

        # Attitude change comparisons
        attitude_comparisons = {
            'control_mean': control_group['attitude_change'].mean(),
            'narrative_mean': narrative_group['attitude_change'].mean(),
            'high_transport_mean': high_transport['attitude_change'].mean(),
            'low_transport_mean': low_transport['attitude_change'].mean()
        }

        # Statistical tests
        narrative_vs_control = stats.ttest_ind(
            narrative_group['attitude_change'].dropna(),
            control_group['attitude_change'].dropna()
        )

        high_vs_low_transport = stats.ttest_ind(
            high_transport['attitude_change'].dropna(),
            low_transport['attitude_change'].dropna()
        )

        analysis_results['statistical_tests'] = {
            'narrative_vs_control': {
                't_statistic': narrative_vs_control.statistic,
                'p_value': narrative_vs_control.pvalue,
                'effect_size': (attitude_comparisons['narrative_mean'] - 
                              attitude_comparisons['control_mean']) / 
                             narrative_group['attitude_change'].std()
            },
            'high_vs_low_transport': {
                't_statistic': high_vs_low_transport.statistic,
                'p_value': high_vs_low_transport.pvalue,
                'effect_size': (attitude_comparisons['high_transport_mean'] - 
                              attitude_comparisons['low_transport_mean']) / 
                             narrative_group['attitude_change'].std()
            }
        }

    # Mediation analysis: Transportation -> Identification -> Attitude Change
    if 'character_identification' in study_data.columns:
        # Simple mediation using regression
        X = narrative_group[['transportation_total']].fillna(
            narrative_group[['transportation_total']].mean()
        )
        M = narrative_group['identification_score'].fillna(
            narrative_group['identification_score'].mean()
        )
        Y = narrative_group['attitude_change'].fillna(
            narrative_group['attitude_change'].mean()
        )

        # Path a: Transportation -> Identification
        model_a = LinearRegression().fit(X, M)
        path_a = model_a.coef_[0]

        # Path b: Identification -> Attitude Change (controlling for Transportation)
        X_with_M = np.column_stack((X.values.flatten(), M))
        model_b = LinearRegression().fit(X_with_M, Y)
        path_b = model_b.coef_[1]  # Coefficient for identification

        # Path c: Transportation -> Attitude Change (total effect)
        model_c = LinearRegression().fit(X, Y)
        path_c = model_c.coef_[0]

        # Indirect effect (mediation)
        indirect_effect = path_a * path_b
        direct_effect = model_b.coef_[0]  # Transportation coefficient in full model

        analysis_results['mediation_analysis'] = {
            'path_a_transport_to_identification': path_a,
            'path_b_identification_to_attitude': path_b,
            'path_c_total_effect': path_c,
            'indirect_effect': indirect_effect,
            'direct_effect': direct_effect,
            'mediation_ratio': indirect_effect / path_c if path_c != 0 else 0
        }

    # Correlations between key variables
    correlation_vars = ['transportation_total', 'identification_score', 'attitude_change']
    available_vars = [var for var in correlation_vars if var in study_data.columns]

    if len(available_vars) > 1:
        corr_matrix = study_data[available_vars].corr()
        analysis_results['correlations'] = corr_matrix.to_dict()

    # Generate recommendations based on results
    recommendations = []

    if 'statistical_tests' in analysis_results:
        if analysis_results['statistical_tests']['narrative_vs_control']['p_value'] < 0.05:
            effect_size = analysis_results['statistical_tests']['narrative_vs_control']['effect_size']
            if effect_size > 0.5:
                recommendations.append("Strong narrative persuasion effect detected - consider scaling intervention")
            elif effect_size > 0.3:
                recommendations.append("Moderate narrative effect - optimize story quality for stronger impact")
            else:
                recommendations.append("Weak narrative effect - consider story improvements or audience targeting")
        else:
            recommendations.append("No significant narrative persuasion effect - major story revision needed")

    if 'mediation_analysis' in analysis_results:
        mediation_ratio = analysis_results['mediation_analysis']['mediation_ratio']
        if mediation_ratio > 0.5:
            recommendations.append("Strong mediation through identification - focus on character development")
        elif mediation_ratio > 0.2:
            recommendations.append("Partial mediation detected - both transportation and identification important")
        else:
            recommendations.append("Limited mediation - transportation may work through other mechanisms")

    analysis_results['recommendations'] = recommendations
    return analysis_results

def simulate_narrative_campaign(baseline_attitudes, story_quality, audience_characteristics):
    """
    Simulate narrative persuasion campaign outcomes based on story and audience factors

    Parameters:
    baseline_attitudes (array): Pre-campaign attitude scores (1-7 scale)
    story_quality (float): Story quality rating (0-1 scale)
    audience_characteristics (dict): Transportation propensity and demographic factors

    Returns:
    dict: Predicted campaign outcomes and optimization suggestions
    """

    np.random.seed(42)
    n_participants = len(baseline_attitudes)

    # Transportation generation based on story quality and individual propensity
    transport_propensity = audience_characteristics.get('transport_propensity', 0.5)
    base_transportation = story_quality * 0.7 + transport_propensity * 0.3

    transportation_scores = np.random.normal(
        base_transportation * 7,  # Scale to 1-7 range
        1.2,  # Standard deviation
        n_participants
    )
    transportation_scores = np.clip(transportation_scores, 1, 7)

    # Character identification based on transportation and similarity
    char_similarity = audience_characteristics.get('character_similarity', 0.5)
    identification_scores = (
        transportation_scores * 0.6 + 
        char_similarity * 7 * 0.4 + 
        np.random.normal(0, 0.8, n_participants)
    )
    identification_scores = np.clip(identification_scores, 1, 7)

    # Attitude change based on transportation and identification
    attitude_change = (
        (transportation_scores - 4) * 0.3 +  # Transportation effect
        (identification_scores - 4) * 0.25 +  # Identification effect
        np.random.normal(0, 0.5, n_participants)  # Random variation
    )

    post_attitudes = baseline_attitudes + attitude_change
    post_attitudes = np.clip(post_attitudes, 1, 7)

    # Calculate campaign metrics
    campaign_results = {
        'participation_metrics': {
            'mean_transportation': transportation_scores.mean(),
            'mean_identification': identification_scores.mean(),
            'transportation_rate': (transportation_scores > 4).mean(),  # Above midpoint
            'high_engagement_rate': (transportation_scores > 5.5).mean()
        },

        'persuasion_outcomes': {
            'mean_attitude_change': attitude_change.mean(),
            'positive_change_rate': (attitude_change > 0).mean(),
            'strong_change_rate': (attitude_change > 1).mean(),
            'effect_size': attitude_change.mean() / attitude_change.std()
        },

        'audience_segments': {
            'high_transport_high_id': ((transportation_scores > 5) & 
                                     (identification_scores > 5)).mean(),
            'high_transport_low_id': ((transportation_scores > 5) & 
                                    (identification_scores <= 5)).mean(),
            'low_transport_high_id': ((transportation_scores <= 5) & 
                                    (identification_scores > 5)).mean(),
            'low_transport_low_id': ((transportation_scores <= 5) & 
                                   (identification_scores <= 5)).mean()
        }
    }

    # Optimization recommendations
    optimization_suggestions = []

    if campaign_results['participation_metrics']['mean_transportation'] < 4:
        optimization_suggestions.append("Low transportation - improve story quality, pacing, and emotional engagement")

    if campaign_results['participation_metrics']['mean_identification'] < 4:
        optimization_suggestions.append("Poor character identification - increase character relatability and similarity")

    if campaign_results['persuasion_outcomes']['positive_change_rate'] < 0.6:
        optimization_suggestions.append("Low persuasion rate - strengthen message integration and story themes")

    if campaign_results['audience_segments']['low_transport_low_id'] > 0.3:
        optimization_suggestions.append("Large disengaged segment - consider audience segmentation and targeted narratives")

    campaign_results['optimization_suggestions'] = optimization_suggestions

    return campaign_results, {
        'transportation': transportation_scores,
        'identification': identification_scores,
        'attitude_change': attitude_change,
        'post_attitudes': post_attitudes
    }
