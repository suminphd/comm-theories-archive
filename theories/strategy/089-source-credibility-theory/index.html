    Source Credibility Theory

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Source Credibility Theory explains how characteristics of the message communicator fundamentally influence persuasive effectiveness and audience acceptance of information. Developed by Hovland, Janis, and Kelley (1953) at Yale University, the theory identifies expertise, trustworthiness, and attractiveness as the three primary dimensions of source credibility that determine persuasive impact. High-credibility sources produce significantly greater attitude change, increased message acceptance, and stronger behavioral compliance compared to low-credibility communicators. Expertise refers to the perceived knowledge, competence, and qualifications of the source regarding the topic being discussed. Trustworthiness encompasses honesty, integrity, and perceived motivation to communicate accurately rather than manipulate audiences. Attractiveness includes physical appeal, similarity to audience, and likability factors that create positive source evaluations. The theory operates through both peripheral processing (using source credibility as a decision shortcut) and central processing (credible sources receiving more attention and favorable evaluation of their arguments). Sleeper effects occur when initially low-credibility sources gain persuasive impact over time as audiences forget the source while retaining message content. Modern applications span celebrity endorsements, expert testimonials, corporate spokespersons, political campaigning, and crisis communication. Digital environments have created new credibility challenges including source verification difficulties, artificial credibility manipulation, and distributed credibility assessment through user ratings and social proof mechanisms.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theoretical framework explaining how audience perceptions of the communicator's expertise, trustworthiness, and attractiveness influence message acceptance, attitude change, and behavioral compliance in persuasive communication contexts.
    • Scope: All forms of persuasive communication where source characteristics are identifiable, including advertising, political messaging, health communication, corporate communication, interpersonal persuasion, and any context where communicator credibility affects message reception and impact.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Primary Credibility Dimensions¶

    Expertise¶

    Definition: Perceived knowledge, competence, and qualifications of the source regarding the communication topic

    Components of Expertise: - Domain Knowledge: Specialized understanding of subject matter relevant to message content - Professional Qualifications: Formal education, training, certifications, and credentials - Experience Indicators: Track record, years of practice, demonstrated success in relevant field - Recognition Status: Awards, peer acknowledgment, institutional affiliations, media coverage

    Expertise Cues: - Credentials Display: Titles, degrees, professional affiliations prominently featured - Technical Language: Appropriate use of specialized terminology and concepts - Depth of Detail: Comprehensive understanding demonstrated through nuanced analysis - Current Knowledge: References to latest developments, research, and industry trends

    Audience Assessment Factors: - Relevance Match: Alignment between source expertise area and message topic - Depth Perception: Audience evaluation of knowledge level and understanding - Comparison Standards: Relative expertise compared to other available sources - Verification Ability: Audience capacity to independently assess claimed expertise

    Trustworthiness¶

    Definition: Perceived honesty, integrity, and motivation to communicate accurately rather than manipulate audience

    Trust Components: - Honesty Perception: Belief that source tells the truth and avoids deception - Integrity Assessment: Consistency between stated values and observed behavior - Motivation Evaluation: Perceived reasons for communication and potential conflicts of interest - Reliability Expectation: Confidence that source will provide consistent, dependable information

    Trustworthiness Indicators: - Transparency Behaviors: Open acknowledgment of limitations, uncertainties, or conflicts - Consistency Pattern: Alignment between current and previous statements or positions - Independence Signals: Apparent freedom from undue influence or coercive pressures - Accountability Acceptance: Willingness to take responsibility for claims and recommendations

    Trust-Damaging Factors: - Conflict of Interest: Financial or personal motivations that compromise objectivity - Inconsistency History: Previous contradictory statements or position reversals - Deception Discovery: Evidence of past misleading or false communications - Bias Indicators: Obvious favoritism or prejudice affecting message content

    Attractiveness¶

    Definition: Physical appeal, similarity to audience, and likability factors that create positive source evaluations

    Attractiveness Elements: - Physical Appearance: Conventional beauty standards and visual appeal - Similarity Perception: Shared demographics, values, experiences, or characteristics with audience - Likability Factors: Personality traits, communication style, and interpersonal appeal - Familiarity Effects: Recognition, prior exposure, and comfort with source

    Similarity Dimensions: - Demographic Similarity: Age, gender, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, geographic location - Attitudinal Similarity: Shared values, beliefs, opinions, and worldview perspectives - Lifestyle Similarity: Common interests, activities, consumer preferences, life experiences - Aspirational Similarity: Representing desired future state or achievement goals

    Attractiveness Mechanisms: - Identification Process: Audience members psychologically connect with attractive sources - Halo Effect: Positive feelings toward source transfer to message content evaluation - Social Modeling: Attractive sources serve as behavioral and attitudinal role models - Approach Motivation: Positive source evaluation increases attention and message engagement

    Credibility Processing Mechanisms¶

    Peripheral Route Processing¶

    Definition: Using source credibility as a decision shortcut without extensive message analysis

    Heuristic Decision Rules: - Expert Rule: "Experts can be trusted, so I should accept their recommendations" - Likability Rule: "I agree with people I like and who are similar to me"
    - Authority Rule: "Legitimate authorities should be obeyed and followed" - Consensus Rule: "If credible sources agree, the position is probably correct"

    Processing Characteristics: - Low Elaboration: Minimal analysis of message arguments and evidence - Efficiency Focus: Quick decision-making with reduced cognitive effort - Contextual Dependence: Effectiveness varies based on motivation and ability to process - Temporary Effects: Attitude changes may be less stable than elaborated persuasion

    Central Route Enhancement¶

    Definition: High-credibility sources receiving more attention and favorable evaluation of their arguments

    Enhanced Processing: - Increased Attention: Credible sources capture and maintain audience attention more effectively - Positive Bias: Arguments from credible sources evaluated more favorably - Comprehension Aid: Credibility enhances understanding and retention of complex information - Elaboration Motivation: High credibility increases willingness to process message content

    Message-Source Integration: - Argument Enhancement: Source credibility strengthens perception of argument quality - Evidence Validation: Credible sources make supporting evidence more convincing - Counterargument Reduction: High credibility reduces critical evaluation and resistance - Memory Integration: Source and message information stored together in memory

    Credibility Development and Maintenance¶

    Initial Credibility¶

    Definition: Audience perceptions of source credibility before message exposure

    Pre-Communication Factors: - Reputation Indicators: Prior knowledge of source achievements, qualifications, and character - Institutional Affiliations: Associations with respected organizations, universities, or agencies - Media Coverage: Previous positive or negative publicity and public visibility - Peer Endorsements: Recommendations and testimonials from other credible sources

    First Impression Effects: - Appearance Factors: Professional presentation, appropriate dress, and visual credibility cues - Introduction Quality: How source is presented and contextualized to audience - Credential Highlighting: Effective communication of relevant qualifications and experience - Setting Appropriateness: Match between communication context and source credibility level

    Derived Credibility¶

    Definition: Credibility perceptions that develop during message presentation based on source performance

    Performance Indicators: - Communication Skill: Clarity, organization, and effectiveness of message delivery - Knowledge Demonstration: Evidence of deep understanding through detailed, accurate information - Responsiveness: Appropriate reactions to audience questions, concerns, or feedback - Authenticity Display: Genuine, natural presentation without obvious manipulation attempts

    Dynamic Credibility Factors: - Consistency Maintenance: Alignment between stated positions and demonstrated behavior - Adaptation Ability: Appropriate adjustment of message or style to audience needs - Confidence Level: Appropriate degree of certainty without arrogance or uncertainty - Error Handling: Graceful acknowledgment and correction of mistakes when they occur

    Terminal Credibility¶

    Definition: Audience credibility perceptions after message exposure and evaluation

    Post-Communication Assessment: - Overall Effectiveness: Evaluation of source performance throughout communication event - Goal Achievement: Degree to which source accomplished apparent communication objectives - Lasting Impression: Final thoughts and feelings about source competence and character - Future Expectations: Anticipated credibility in subsequent communication opportunities

    Individual and Contextual Moderators¶

    Audience Characteristics¶

    Education and Expertise: - High Expertise Audiences: More discriminating evaluation of source credentials and claims - Low Expertise Audiences: Greater reliance on surface credibility cues and heuristics - Domain Familiarity: Ability to independently evaluate source claims affects credibility impact - Critical Thinking Skills: Individual differences in analytical ability moderate credibility effects

    Motivation and Involvement: - High Involvement: Increased scrutiny of source qualifications and potential biases - Low Involvement: Greater susceptibility to peripheral credibility cues - Personal Relevance: Issues affecting audience personally receive more careful source evaluation - Decision Consequences: High-stakes decisions prompt more thorough credibility assessment

    Message and Context Factors¶

    Topic Complexity: - Technical Topics: Increased importance of demonstrable expertise and knowledge - Simple Topics: Greater role for attractiveness and likability factors - Controversial Issues: Enhanced significance of trustworthiness and perceived objectivity - Novel Information: Expertise becomes more crucial for unfamiliar or cutting-edge content

    Communication Channel: - Face-to-Face: Full range of credibility cues available for audience evaluation - Mediated Communication: Limited cue availability may emphasize certain credibility dimensions - Digital Platforms: New credibility indicators including follower counts and user ratings - Mass Media: Institutional credibility may supplement or substitute for individual source credibility

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: High-credibility sources produce greater attitude change than low-credibility sources

    P2: Source credibility effects operate through both peripheral and central processing routes

    P3: Expertise, trustworthiness, and attractiveness are independent but interactive credibility dimensions

    P4: Credibility effects are moderated by audience involvement and message complexity

    P5: Initial high credibility can overcome weak arguments; low credibility can undermine strong arguments

    P6: Credibility perceptions can change during communication based on source performance

    P7: Sleeper effects occur when source credibility impact diminishes over time while message content persists

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Conscious Processing: Audiences actively or passively evaluate source characteristics during communication
    • Credibility Relevance: Source characteristics are perceived as relevant to message evaluation
    • Cognitive Capacity: Audiences have sufficient ability to process credibility cues and their implications
    • Motivation Variability: Individual differences in motivation to evaluate sources and messages exist
    • Cultural Universality: Basic credibility dimensions operate across different cultural contexts (though specific indicators may vary)

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Anonymous Communication: Theory less applicable when source identity is unknown or irrelevant
    • Overriding Evidence: Extremely strong or weak evidence may override credibility effects
    • Institutional Contexts: Formal roles may provide credibility independent of individual characteristics
    • Crisis Situations: Emergency contexts may alter normal credibility evaluation processes
    • Automated Communication: AI and algorithmic sources present novel credibility evaluation challenges
    • Cultural Specificity: Credibility indicators and their importance may vary significantly across cultures

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental Design Framework¶

    Basic Credibility Manipulation: - Independent Variable: Source Credibility (2-3 levels: High vs. Low vs. Control) - Dependent Variables: Attitude change, message acceptance, behavioral intentions, source evaluations - Manipulation Methods: Credential presentation, biographical information, institutional affiliation - Control Variables: Message content, argument strength, presentation format

    Procedure: 1. Pre-test Measures: Baseline attitudes, demographic information, topic involvement 2. Source Introduction: Credibility manipulation through biographical information or credentials 3. Message Presentation: Identical content delivered by high vs. low credibility sources 4. Immediate Assessment: Source credibility ratings, attitude measures, persuasion indicators 5. Delayed Follow-up: Sleeper effect testing, attitude persistence, behavioral compliance

    Factorial Design Extensions¶

    2x2x2 Factorial Design: - Factor 1: Source Expertise (High vs. Low) - Factor 2: Source Trustworthiness (High vs. Low) - Factor 3: Audience Involvement (High vs. Low)

    Advanced Manipulations: 

    Credibility Conditions:
- High expertise, high trustworthiness, high attractiveness
- High expertise, high trustworthiness, low attractiveness
- High expertise, low trustworthiness, high attractiveness
- Low expertise, high trustworthiness, high attractiveness
- Control condition (minimal credibility information)

    Longitudinal Assessment Protocol¶

    Time-Series Credibility Tracking: - Baseline (Week 0): Initial source credibility perceptions, if any prior knowledge exists - Immediate (Day 1): Post-exposure credibility ratings and persuasive effects - Short-term (Week 1): Credibility persistence and attitude stability - Medium-term (Month 1): Sleeper effect assessment and behavioral tracking - Long-term (Month 3): Sustained credibility impact and relationship development

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Primary Credibility Effects¶

    Expertise Impact: - Attitude Change: High-expertise sources produce 30-50% greater attitude change than low-expertise sources - Message Acceptance: Expert sources achieve 60-80% message acceptance vs. 35-50% for non-experts - Behavioral Compliance: Expert recommendations followed 40-65% more often than non-expert advice - Information Processing: Expert sources receive 25-40% more attention and elaboration

    Trustworthiness Effects: - Credibility Ratings: High-trust sources rated 45-70% higher on overall credibility measures - Resistance Reduction: Trustworthy sources encounter 30-50% less counterarguing and resistance - Long-term Impact: Trust effects show greater persistence over time than expertise effects - Crisis Communication: Trustworthiness becomes primary credibility factor during organizational crises

    Attractiveness Influence: - Initial Attention: Attractive sources capture 20-35% more initial audience attention - Likability Transfer: Positive source feelings increase message favorability by 25-40% - Identification Effects: Similar sources produce 30-45% stronger audience identification - Peripheral Processing: Attractiveness shows strongest effects under low-involvement conditions

    Interaction Effects¶

    Expertise-Trustworthiness Interaction: - High Expertise + High Trust: Optimal credibility combination, maximum persuasive impact - High Expertise + Low Trust: Expertise effects diminished by trust concerns - Low Expertise + High Trust: Trust can partially compensate for limited expertise - Additive Effects: Both dimensions contribute independently to overall credibility

    Audience Involvement Moderations: - High Involvement: Expertise becomes more important, attractiveness less influential - Low Involvement: Attractiveness and simple credibility cues more impactful - Topic Relevance: Personal relevance increases scrutiny of source qualifications - Decision Stakes: Higher stakes increase emphasis on expertise and trustworthiness

    Temporal Patterns¶

    Immediate Effects: - Initial Impact: High credibility sources show immediate advantage in persuasive effectiveness - First Impressions: Initial credibility assessments strongly influence subsequent message processing - Attention Allocation: Credible sources receive more sustained attention throughout message - Processing Bias: High credibility creates favorable interpretation of ambiguous message content

    Sleeper Effects: - Timeline: Credibility effects typically begin declining 2-4 weeks post-exposure - Content Persistence: Message arguments may gain influence as source memory fades - Reinstatement: Reminding audiences of source identity can restore credibility effects - Individual Differences: Sleeper effects vary based on audience involvement and message complexity

    Channel and Context Effects¶

    Communication Channel Impact: - Face-to-Face: Full credibility cue availability, strongest overall effects - Video/Audio: Visual and vocal cues present, moderate credibility impact - Written Communication: Limited credibility cues, greater reliance on credentials and content - Digital Platforms: New credibility indicators including metrics, ratings, and social proof

    Competitive Contexts: - Multiple Sources: Comparative credibility becomes more important than absolute levels - Conflicting Messages: Source credibility crucial for resolving information inconsistencies - Expert Disagreement: Audience evaluation of relative expertise and trustworthiness - Media Environment: Institutional credibility may supplement individual source characteristics

    Cultural and Demographic Variations¶

    Cultural Differences: - Individualistic Cultures: Greater emphasis on individual expertise and achievement - Collectivistic Cultures: Increased importance of group affiliation and consensus - Power Distance: Variations in authority and status credibility indicators - Communication Styles: Direct vs. indirect culture effects on trustworthiness perception

    Demographic Moderators: - Age Effects: Older audiences may rely more heavily on traditional credibility cues - Education Impact: Higher education associated with more discriminating credibility evaluation - Gender Differences: Some variation in relative importance of different credibility dimensions - Socioeconomic Factors: Resource availability affects access to diverse credibility information

    9) Applications¶

    Celebrity Endorsements and Influencer Marketing¶

    Celebrity Credibility Assessment: - Expertise Matching: Aligning celebrity knowledge with product category (athlete endorsing sports equipment) - Trustworthiness Factors: Celebrity reputation, past endorsement history, perceived sincerity - Attractiveness Elements: Physical appeal, aspirational lifestyle, audience identification - Authenticity Concerns: Balancing commercial objectives with perceived genuine product use

    Influencer Credibility Strategies: - Niche Expertise: Building credibility through specialized knowledge and content quality - Transparency Practices: Disclosure of sponsorships and partnerships to maintain trust - Audience Engagement: Interactive communication to demonstrate authenticity and accessibility - Consistency Maintenance: Aligning endorsed products with established personal brand

    Corporate Communication and Leadership¶

    Executive Spokesperson Development: - Expertise Building: Highlighting relevant business experience, industry knowledge, and achievements - Trustworthiness Enhancement: Demonstrating transparency, ethical behavior, and stakeholder focus - Communication Skills: Developing clear, confident, and authentic presentation abilities - Crisis Preparedness: Training for high-pressure situations requiring credibility maintenance

    Organizational Credibility: - Institutional Reputation: Building corporate brand credibility through consistent performance - Third-Party Validation: Obtaining endorsements from credible external organizations - Transparency Initiatives: Open communication about operations, performance, and challenges - Stakeholder Engagement: Developing trusted relationships with key audience groups

    Political Communication¶

    Candidate Credibility Building: - Experience Highlighting: Emphasizing relevant political, professional, or life experience - Character Development: Demonstrating integrity, honesty, and commitment to public service - Competence Communication: Showing knowledge of issues and ability to govern effectively - Relatability Factors: Connecting with voter experiences and shared values

    Campaign Messaging: - Surrogate Deployment: Using credible third parties to validate candidate qualifications - Endorsement Strategy: Securing support from respected individuals and organizations - Debate Performance: Demonstrating knowledge, composure, and leadership qualities - Media Relations: Maintaining consistent, truthful communication with press and public

    Health Communication¶

    Medical Source Credibility: - Professional Qualifications: Highlighting medical degrees, board certifications, and specializations - Institution Affiliation: Association with respected hospitals, universities, or health organizations - Research Credentials: Publication history, clinical trial involvement, and scientific contributions - Patient Communication: Developing empathy, listening skills, and clear explanation abilities

    Public Health Campaigns: - Expert Spokespersons: Using medical professionals and researchers as primary communicators - Peer Messengers: Leveraging credible community members for targeted audience segments - Celebrity Health Advocates: Combining fame with authentic health experiences or commitments - Multi-Source Strategies: Coordinating messages across different credible source types

    Digital Era Credibility¶

    Online Source Verification: - Credential Checking: Verifying claimed qualifications and professional affiliations - Track Record Analysis: Reviewing past content quality, accuracy, and consistency - Third-Party Validation: Cross-referencing information with other credible sources - Transparency Assessment: Evaluating disclosure of conflicts of interest and funding sources

    Platform-Specific Credibility: - Social Media Metrics: Follower counts, engagement rates, and verification status as credibility indicators - Review Systems: User ratings and reviews as distributed credibility assessment mechanisms - Algorithm Influence: Understanding how platform algorithms affect credibility perception - Viral Content: Balancing popularity with accuracy in credibility evaluation

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Source Credibility Key Differences
    Elaboration Likelihood Model Dual-route persuasion processing Source credibility operates in both central and peripheral routes ELM broader framework; credibility theory focuses specifically on source effects
    Heuristic-Systematic Model Systematic vs. heuristic processing Credibility functions as heuristic cue in low-motivation contexts HSM emphasizes processing modes; credibility theory emphasizes source characteristics
    Social Judgment Theory Attitude change through judgment processes Credible sources can expand acceptance latitude SJT focuses on message-attitude discrepancy; credibility on source characteristics
    Attribution Theory Causal attributions for behavior Credibility affects attributions about source motives and message quality Attribution broader; credibility specifically about communicator evaluation

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Oversimplified Credibility Model: Critics argue that three-factor model (expertise, trustworthiness, attractiveness) may be too reductive for complex, multifaceted credibility perceptions in real-world contexts

    Cultural Bias: Theory developed primarily in Western contexts may not adequately capture credibility perception differences across diverse cultural and social groups

    Static Conceptualization: Traditional model may not account for dynamic, evolving credibility perceptions that change through ongoing interaction and relationship development

    Measurement Challenges: Difficulty in creating universal credibility measures that work across different domains, audiences, and communication contexts

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Credibility Assessment: - Algorithm Influence: How do recommendation algorithms and search rankings affect credibility perception? - Artificial Enhancement: What are the implications of bought followers, fake reviews, and manufactured credibility indicators? - Distributed Evaluation: How do crowd-sourced ratings and reviews change traditional credibility assessment? - Information Overload: How does the abundance of sources affect credibility evaluation processes?

    Authenticity vs. Expertise: - Relatability Trade-offs: Tension between perceived authenticity and professional credibility - Micro-Celebrity Influence: How do non-traditional influencers challenge expert authority? - Grassroots Credibility: Value of personal experience vs. professional qualification - Transparency Expectations: Increasing demands for disclosure and authentic communication

    Institutional vs. Individual Credibility: - Brand Association: How does organizational affiliation affect individual credibility? - Independence Questions: Impact of funding sources and institutional conflicts on credibility - Collective Credibility: Emergence of distributed, network-based credibility systems - Verification Challenges: Difficulty in authenticating credentials and claims in digital environments

    Theoretical Extensions¶

    import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

def analyze_source_credibility_experiment(study_data):
    """
    Comprehensive analysis of source credibility experimental data

    Parameters:
    study_data (DataFrame): Data with credibility manipulations and outcome measures

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results including dimension effects and interactions
    """

    # Calculate composite credibility scores
    study_data['expertise_score'] = study_data[['expert_rating', 'qualified_rating', 'experienced_rating']].mean(axis=1)
    study_data['trustworthiness_score'] = study_data[['honest_rating', 'trustworthy_rating', 'reliable_rating']].mean(axis=1)
    study_data['attractiveness_score'] = study_data[['likable_rating', 'attractive_rating', 'similar_rating']].mean(axis=1)
    study_data['overall_credibility'] = study_data[['expertise_score', 'trustworthiness_score', 'attractiveness_score']].mean(axis=1)

    # Attitude change calculation
    study_data['attitude_change'] = study_data['post_attitude'] - study_data['pre_attitude']

    analysis_results = {
        'sample_size': len(study_data),
        'descriptive_stats': {
            'expertise_mean': study_data['expertise_score'].mean(),
            'trustworthiness_mean': study_data['trustworthiness_score'].mean(),
            'attractiveness_mean': study_data['attractiveness_score'].mean(),
            'overall_credibility_mean': study_data['overall_credibility'].mean(),
            'attitude_change_mean': study_data['attitude_change'].mean()
        }
    }

    # Credibility condition comparisons
    if 'credibility_condition' in study_data.columns:
        condition_analysis = {}

        for condition in study_data['credibility_condition'].unique():
            if pd.notna(condition):
                condition_data = study_data[study_data['credibility_condition'] == condition]
                condition_analysis[condition] = {
                    'n': len(condition_data),
                    'expertise_mean': condition_data['expertise_score'].mean(),
                    'trustworthiness_mean': condition_data['trustworthiness_score'].mean(),
                    'attractiveness_mean': condition_data['attractiveness_score'].mean(),
                    'attitude_change_mean': condition_data['attitude_change'].mean(),
                    'message_acceptance_mean': condition_data.get('message_acceptance', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean()
                }

        analysis_results['condition_analysis'] = condition_analysis

        # Statistical comparisons between high and low credibility conditions
        if 'high_credibility' in condition_analysis and 'low_credibility' in condition_analysis:
            high_cred_data = study_data[study_data['credibility_condition'] == 'high_credibility']
            low_cred_data = study_data[study_data['credibility_condition'] == 'low_credibility']

            attitude_comparison = stats.ttest_ind(
                high_cred_data['attitude_change'].dropna(),
                low_cred_data['attitude_change'].dropna()
            )

            credibility_comparison = stats.ttest_ind(
                high_cred_data['overall_credibility'].dropna(),
                low_cred_data['overall_credibility'].dropna()
            )

            analysis_results['statistical_tests'] = {
                'attitude_difference': {
                    't_statistic': attitude_comparison.statistic,
                    'p_value': attitude_comparison.pvalue,
                    'effect_size': (condition_analysis['high_credibility']['attitude_change_mean'] - 
                                  condition_analysis['low_credibility']['attitude_change_mean']) / 
                                 study_data['attitude_change'].std()
                },
                'credibility_difference': {
                    't_statistic': credibility_comparison.statistic,
                    'p_value': credibility_comparison.pvalue,
                    'effect_size': (high_cred_data['overall_credibility'].mean() - 
                                  low_cred_data['overall_credibility'].mean()) / 
                                 study_data['overall_credibility'].std()
                }
            }

    # Correlation analysis between credibility dimensions and outcomes
    credibility_vars = ['expertise_score', 'trustworthiness_score', 'attractiveness_score', 'overall_credibility']
    outcome_vars = ['attitude_change']

    if 'message_acceptance' in study_data.columns:
        outcome_vars.append('message_acceptance')
    if 'behavioral_intention' in study_data.columns:
        outcome_vars.append('behavioral_intention')

    correlation_results = {}
    for cred_var in credibility_vars:
        correlation_results[cred_var] = {}
        for outcome_var in outcome_vars:
            if outcome_var in study_data.columns:
                correlation, p_value = stats.pearsonr(
                    study_data[cred_var].dropna(),
                    study_data[outcome_var].dropna()
                )
                correlation_results[cred_var][outcome_var] = {
                    'correlation': correlation,
                    'p_value': p_value
                }

    analysis_results['correlations'] = correlation_results

    # Regression analysis: Credibility dimensions predicting attitude change
    predictors = ['expertise_score', 'trustworthiness_score', 'attractiveness_score']
    available_predictors = [var for var in predictors if var in study_data.columns]

    if len(available_predictors) >= 2:
        X = study_data[available_predictors].fillna(study_data[available_predictors].mean())
        y = study_data['attitude_change'].fillna(study_data['attitude_change'].mean())

        model = LinearRegression().fit(X, y)

        analysis_results['regression_analysis'] = {
            'r_squared': model.score(X, y),
            'coefficients': dict(zip(available_predictors, model.coef_)),
            'intercept': model.intercept_,
            'predictors_used': available_predictors
        }

    # Involvement moderation analysis
    if 'involvement_level' in study_data.columns:
        involvement_moderation = {}

        for involvement in ['low', 'high']:
            involvement_data = study_data[study_data['involvement_level'] == involvement]
            if len(involvement_data) > 10:
                # Correlation between overall credibility and attitude change by involvement
                if len(involvement_data['overall_credibility'].dropna()) > 0:
                    correlation, p_value = stats.pearsonr(
                        involvement_data['overall_credibility'].dropna(),
                        involvement_data['attitude_change'].dropna()
                    )

                    involvement_moderation[involvement] = {
                        'credibility_attitude_correlation': correlation,
                        'p_value': p_value,
                        'sample_size': len(involvement_data)
                    }

        analysis_results['involvement_moderation'] = involvement_moderation

    # Generate recommendations based on results
    recommendations = []

    # Overall credibility effect
    if 'statistical_tests' in analysis_results:
        if analysis_results['statistical_tests']['attitude_difference']['p_value'] < 0.05:
            effect_size = analysis_results['statistical_tests']['attitude_difference']['effect_size']
            if effect_size > 0.5:
                recommendations.append("Strong credibility effect detected - prioritize high-credibility sources")
            elif effect_size > 0.3:
                recommendations.append("Moderate credibility effect - source selection important but not critical")
            else:
                recommendations.append("Weak credibility effect - focus on message content over source characteristics")
        else:
            recommendations.append("No significant credibility effect - consider audience and context factors")

    # Dimension-specific recommendations
    if 'correlations' in analysis_results:
        expertise_corr = analysis_results['correlations'].get('expertise_score', {}).get('attitude_change', {})
        trust_corr = analysis_results['correlations'].get('trustworthiness_score', {}).get('attitude_change', {})
        attract_corr = analysis_results['correlations'].get('attractiveness_score', {}).get('attitude_change', {})

        if expertise_corr.get('correlation', 0) > 0.3 and expertise_corr.get('p_value', 1) < 0.05:
            recommendations.append("Expertise shows strong effect - emphasize source qualifications and knowledge")

        if trust_corr.get('correlation', 0) > 0.3 and trust_corr.get('p_value', 1) < 0.05:
            recommendations.append("Trustworthiness crucial - focus on honesty, integrity, and transparency")

        if attract_corr.get('correlation', 0) > 0.3 and attract_corr.get('p_value', 1) < 0.05:
            recommendations.append("Attractiveness matters - consider likability and audience similarity")

    # Involvement moderation recommendations
    if 'involvement_moderation' in analysis_results:
        high_involve = analysis_results['involvement_moderation'].get('high', {})
        low_involve = analysis_results['involvement_moderation'].get('low', {})

        if (high_involve.get('credibility_attitude_correlation', 0) > 
            low_involve.get('credibility_attitude_correlation', 0)):
            recommendations.append("Credibility more important for high-involvement audiences - tailor approach accordingly")
        else:
            recommendations.append("Credibility effects consistent across involvement levels - general approach suitable")

    analysis_results['recommendations'] = recommendations
    return analysis_results

def simulate_credibility_campaign(source_profiles, audience_segments):
    """
    Simulate source credibility campaign outcomes across different sources and audiences

    Parameters:
    source_profiles (dict): Different source types with credibility characteristics
    audience_segments (dict): Audience groups with varying involvement and characteristics

    Returns:
    dict: Predicted campaign outcomes and source optimization recommendations
    """

    np.random.seed(42)
    campaign_results = {}

    for segment_name, segment_chars in audience_segments.items():
        segment_size = segment_chars.get('size', 100)
        involvement_level = segment_chars.get('involvement', 'medium')
        segment_results = {}

        for source_name, source_profile in source_profiles.items():
            # Base credibility scores
            expertise = source_profile.get('expertise', 4.0)
            trustworthiness = source_profile.get('trustworthiness', 4.0)
            attractiveness = source_profile.get('attractiveness', 4.0)

            # Involvement moderation effects
            if involvement_level == 'high':
                expertise_weight = 0.5
                trust_weight = 0.4
                attract_weight = 0.1
            elif involvement_level == 'low':
                expertise_weight = 0.2
                trust_weight = 0.3
                attract_weight = 0.5
            else:  # medium
                expertise_weight = 0.4
                trust_weight = 0.4
                attract_weight = 0.2

            # Calculate overall credibility with weights
            overall_credibility = (
                expertise * expertise_weight +
                trustworthiness * trust_weight +
                attractiveness * attract_weight
            )

            # Generate attitude change based on credibility
            base_attitude_change = (overall_credibility - 4.0) * 0.4  # Scale factor

            attitude_changes = np.random.normal(
                base_attitude_change,
                0.6,
                segment_size
            )
            attitude_changes = np.clip(attitude_changes, -2, 3)

            # Calculate additional outcomes
            message_acceptance_rate = 0.5 + (overall_credibility - 4.0) * 0.1
            message_acceptance_rate = np.clip(message_acceptance_rate, 0.1, 0.9)

            behavioral_compliance = np.random.binomial(
                1,
                message_acceptance_rate,
                segment_size
            )

            segment_results[source_name] = {
                'overall_credibility': overall_credibility,
                'expertise_contribution': expertise * expertise_weight,
                'trust_contribution': trustworthiness * trust_weight,
                'attractiveness_contribution': attractiveness * attract_weight,
                'mean_attitude_change': attitude_changes.mean(),
                'attitude_change_std': attitude_changes.std(),
                'message_acceptance_rate': message_acceptance_rate,
                'behavioral_compliance_rate': behavioral_compliance.mean(),
                'positive_change_rate': (attitude_changes > 0).mean()
            }

        # Identify optimal source for segment
        best_source = max(segment_results.keys(), 
                         key=lambda x: segment_results[x]['mean_attitude_change'])
        segment_results['optimal_source'] = best_source
        segment_results['segment_characteristics'] = segment_chars

        campaign_results[segment_name] = segment_results

    return campaign_results
