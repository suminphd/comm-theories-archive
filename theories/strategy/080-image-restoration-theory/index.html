    Image Restoration Theory

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Image Restoration Theory (IRT), developed by William L. Benoit in 1995, provides a comprehensive framework for understanding how organizations and individuals respond to reputation threats and crises. The theory identifies five primary categories of image restoration strategies: denial (simple denial and shifting blame), evading responsibility (provocation, defeasibility, accident, good intentions), reducing offensiveness (bolstering, minimization, differentiation, transcendence, attacking accusers, compensation), corrective action (restoring situation and preventing recurrence), and mortification (accepting responsibility and seeking forgiveness). IRT posits that effective image restoration requires matching appropriate strategies to crisis characteristics, stakeholder expectations, and organizational capabilities. Unlike prescriptive approaches, IRT is descriptive, cataloging the range of available responses while recognizing that strategy effectiveness depends on contextual factors. The theory has been extensively applied to corporate crises, political scandals, celebrity controversies, and organizational reputation management, providing valuable insights for crisis communication planning and post-crisis recovery efforts.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theoretical framework that categorizes and explains the range of communication strategies available to organizations and individuals when their reputation, image, or credibility is threatened or damaged by perceived wrongdoing or crisis situations.
    • Scope: Crisis communication contexts including corporate scandals, product recalls, organizational misconduct, political controversies, celebrity image management, nonprofit reputation repair, and any situation where public perception of responsibility or blame threatens organizational or individual credibility.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Foundational Assumptions¶

    Image as Valuable Resource¶

    • Definition: Reputation and credibility constitute essential organizational and individual assets requiring protection and restoration
    • Components: Stakeholder trust, public perception, credibility, moral standing, competence assessment
    • Vulnerability: Images can be damaged by perceived wrongdoing, accidents, association, or competitor attacks
    • Strategic Value: Positive image correlates with organizational success, stakeholder support, and crisis resilience

    Attack-Defense Dynamic¶

    • Definition: Image restoration occurs in response to actual or perceived attacks on reputation
    • Attack Characteristics: Accusations of responsibility, wrongdoing, incompetence, or moral failure
    • Defense Necessity: Threatened parties must respond to prevent lasting reputation damage
    • Strategic Response: Organizations choose from available restoration strategies based on situation assessment

    Five Categories of Image Restoration Strategies¶

    Category 1: Denial¶

    Fundamental Approach: Reject responsibility for alleged wrongdoing or negative event

    Simple Denial - Definition: Flat rejection of accusations or responsibility claims - Usage Conditions: When organization truly innocent or evidence insufficient - Communication Elements: Clear, unambiguous rejection of blame or involvement - Risk Factors: Credibility damage if evidence later emerges contradicting denial - Example Applications: "We did not engage in the alleged misconduct" or "These accusations are completely false"

    Shifting Blame - Definition: Acknowledging negative event occurred but attributing responsibility to others - Strategic Logic: Deflects responsibility while accepting event reality - Target Selection: Competitors, external forces, individuals, or other organizations - Implementation Considerations: Requires credible alternative explanation and evidence - Example Applications: "While the incident occurred, it was caused by contractor negligence, not our actions"

    Category 2: Evading Responsibility¶

    Fundamental Approach: Acknowledge event occurrence while reducing perceived responsibility

    Provocation - Definition: Claim that negative act was reasonable response to another's wrongdoing - Justification Logic: "We were forced to act in response to their behavior" - Contextual Requirements: Clear causal link between provocation and response - Effectiveness Factors: Audience sympathy for defensive action - Example Applications: Retaliatory actions, defensive business practices, protective measures

    Defeasibility - Definition: Claim lack of information or ability to control key factors - Information Defense: "We didn't know about the problem when decisions were made" - Control Defense: "External circumstances prevented different action" - Credibility Requirements: Demonstrate genuine information or control limitations - Example Applications: Regulatory changes, market conditions, incomplete information scenarios

    Accident - Definition: Characterize negative event as unintentional mishap rather than purposeful action - Intent Mitigation: Reduces moral culpability by eliminating intentionality - Implementation Challenges: Must demonstrate lack of negligence or recklessness - Audience Considerations: Public more forgiving of accidents than deliberate acts - Example Applications: Product defects, operational failures, unintended consequences

    Good Intentions - Definition: Acknowledge negative outcomes while emphasizing positive motivations - Moral Protection: Preserves character assessment despite poor outcomes - Implementation Strategy: Highlight noble goals and unexpected results - Credibility Requirements: Genuine evidence of positive intentions - Example Applications: Failed social programs, humanitarian efforts with negative consequences

    Category 3: Reducing Offensiveness¶

    Fundamental Approach: Accept responsibility while minimizing perceived severity or negative implications

    Bolstering - Definition: Highlight positive attributes, past accomplishments, or good deeds - Strategic Logic: Positive associations outweigh negative incident in overall assessment - Implementation Methods: Recall past achievements, emphasize positive contributions - Timing Considerations: Most effective when positive history is extensive and relevant - Example Applications: "While this mistake occurred, our 50-year history of community service demonstrates our commitment"

    Minimization - Definition: Argue that negative consequences were less severe than portrayed - Damage Control: Reduce perceived harm through factual corrections or perspective shifts - Evidence Requirements: Credible data supporting minimization claims - Risk Factors: Backfire if minimization appears callous or contradicts stakeholder experience - Example Applications: "Only a small number of customers were affected" or "The environmental impact was minimal"

    Differentiation - Definition: Distinguish current act from more serious wrongdoing - Comparative Framework: "What we did was wrong, but it wasn't as bad as X" - Reference Point Selection: Choose comparison that makes current act seem less severe - Implementation Challenges: Risk of appearing to rationalize wrongdoing - Example Applications: Comparing minor violations to major scandals, differentiating from competitor misconduct

    Transcendence - Definition: Place act in broader context that justifies or explains the behavior - Context Expansion: Shift focus to larger values, goals, or circumstances - Value Appeals: Invoke higher purposes that motivated questionable actions - Effectiveness Factors: Audience must accept transcendent value framework - Example Applications: "We broke this rule to save jobs" or "Security concerns required extraordinary measures"

    Attacking Accusers - Definition: Challenge credibility, motives, or competence of those making accusations - Strategic Logic: Discredit source to undermine accusation credibility - Target Assessment: Identify vulnerabilities in accuser's position or background - Risk Considerations: May appear defensive and can escalate conflict - Example Applications: Questioning competitor motives, highlighting accuser inconsistencies, challenging expertise

    Compensation - Definition: Offer money, goods, services, or other benefits to offset negative consequences - Value Proposition: Tangible remedies demonstrate responsibility acceptance and harm mitigation - Implementation Scope: Can range from individual compensation to community investment - Symbolic Value: Compensation signals acknowledgment of harm and commitment to repair - Example Applications: Victim compensation funds, community investment, product replacements

    Category 4: Corrective Action¶

    Fundamental Approach: Take concrete steps to address problems and prevent recurrence

    Restoring the Situation - Definition: Implement measures to return conditions to pre-crisis state - Scope Considerations: May involve product recalls, process changes, personnel actions - Stakeholder Communication: Explain specific restoration measures and timelines - Effectiveness Indicators: Measurable improvement in criticized conditions - Example Applications: Product recalls, service restoration, policy reversals

    Prevention Measures - Definition: Implement changes to prevent similar problems in the future - Systemic Approach: Address root causes rather than just symptoms - Communication Strategy: Detail specific preventive measures and monitoring systems - Credibility Factors: Must demonstrate genuine commitment and capability for change - Example Applications: New safety protocols, oversight systems, training programs, structural reorganization

    Category 5: Mortification¶

    Fundamental Approach: Accept full responsibility and seek forgiveness

    Responsibility Acceptance - Definition: Acknowledge wrongdoing without excuses or blame-shifting - Complete Ownership: "We were wrong" and "We take full responsibility" - Credibility Requirements: Must appear genuine and comprehensive - Timing Considerations: Early mortification often more effective than delayed admission

    Forgiveness Seeking - Definition: Explicitly ask stakeholders for forgiveness or second chance - Humility Demonstration: Acknowledge harm caused and express genuine remorse - Implementation Challenges: Must appear authentic rather than strategic - Cultural Considerations: Effectiveness varies across cultural contexts and stakeholder groups

    Strategic Combinations and Considerations¶

    Strategy Mixing¶

    • Multiple Strategy Usage: Organizations commonly employ several strategies simultaneously
    • Consistency Requirements: Mixed strategies must not contradict each other
    • Audience Segmentation: Different strategies may be appropriate for different stakeholder groups
    • Temporal Sequencing: Strategy emphasis may evolve throughout crisis lifecycle

    Contextual Factors Influencing Strategy Selection¶

    • Crisis Severity: More serious crises may require stronger responses like mortification
    • Evidence Availability: Strength of evidence affects feasibility of denial strategies
    • Organizational Resources: Corrective action and compensation require financial capability
    • Stakeholder Expectations: Different audiences may expect different response types
    • Cultural Context: Cultural values influence strategy appropriateness and effectiveness

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Organizations facing reputation threats will employ one or more image restoration strategies from the five categories

    P2: Strategy effectiveness depends on alignment between strategy characteristics, crisis context, and stakeholder expectations

    P3: Denial strategies are most effective when organization is genuinely innocent and can provide credible evidence

    P4: Evading responsibility strategies work best when external factors genuinely limited organizational control

    P5: Reducing offensiveness strategies are appropriate when responsibility is clear but severity can be legitimately questioned

    P6: Corrective action demonstrates commitment to improvement and is valued by most stakeholder groups

    P7: Mortification can be highly effective but requires genuine remorse and credible commitment to change

    P8: Strategy combinations must be internally consistent to maintain credibility

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Rational Choice: Organizations and individuals can strategically select from available restoration options
    • Image Value: Reputation and credibility are valuable assets worth protecting and restoring
    • Audience Rationality: Stakeholders evaluate restoration messages and adjust perceptions accordingly
    • Communication Impact: Strategic communication can influence stakeholder perceptions and reputation outcomes
    • Strategy Availability: The five categories encompass the primary range of available responses

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Cultural Context: Strategy effectiveness varies significantly across cultural and national contexts
    • Crisis Type: Some strategies may be inappropriate for certain types of crises (e.g., denial for obvious failures)
    • Stakeholder Diversity: Different stakeholder groups may respond differently to the same strategies
    • Evidence Availability: Strategy feasibility depends on available evidence and information
    • Resource Constraints: Some strategies (corrective action, compensation) require significant organizational resources
    • Legal Considerations: Legal implications may constrain strategy selection, particularly regarding admission of guilt
    • Media Environment: Digital and social media contexts may alter traditional strategy effectiveness

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Content Analysis Protocol for Strategy Identification¶

    Phase 1: Message Collection - Gather all organizational crisis communications (press releases, social media, interviews, statements) - Include communications from key organizational spokespersons - Establish clear time boundaries for analysis period - Document source, date, and medium for each message

    Phase 2: Coding Scheme Development 

    Category 1: Denial
- D1: Simple denial (reject accusations/responsibility)
- D2: Shifting blame (blame others for problem)

Category 2: Evading Responsibility
- ER1: Provocation (responding to others' wrongdoing)
- ER2: Defeasibility (lack of information/control)
- ER3: Accident (unintentional occurrence)
- ER4: Good intentions (positive motivations)

Category 3: Reducing Offensiveness
- RO1: Bolstering (emphasize positive qualities)
- RO2: Minimization (minimize harm/severity)
- RO3: Differentiation (less serious than others)
- RO4: Transcendence (broader context/values)
- RO5: Attack accusers (challenge credibility)
- RO6: Compensation (offer remuneration)

Category 4: Corrective Action
- CA1: Restore situation (fix the problem)
- CA2: Prevention (prevent recurrence)

Category 5: Mortification
- M1: Accept responsibility (acknowledge wrongdoing)
- M2: Seek forgiveness (ask for second chance)

    Phase 3: Reliability Testing - Train multiple coders on scheme application - Conduct pilot coding on sample messages - Calculate inter-coder reliability (aim for Krippendorff's α > .80) - Refine coding scheme based on reliability results

    Experimental Design for Strategy Effectiveness Testing¶

    2×3×2 Factorial Design: - Crisis Type (2 levels): Competence-based vs. Integrity-based crisis - Strategy Type (3 levels): Denial vs. Corrective Action vs. Mortification - Organizational History (2 levels): Positive vs. Neutral prior reputation

    Dependent Variables: - Post-crisis organizational trust (1-7 scale) - Purchase/support intentions (1-7 scale) - Perceived responsibility attribution (1-7 scale) - Message credibility assessment (1-7 scale)

    Longitudinal Case Study Framework¶

    Pre-Crisis Phase (Baseline): - Establish reputation measures across key stakeholder groups - Document organizational characteristics and history - Identify potential vulnerability factors

    Crisis Phase (Immediate Response): - Track real-time strategy deployment across all communication channels - Monitor stakeholder reactions and media coverage - Document strategy evolution and modifications

    Post-Crisis Phase (Recovery Assessment): - Measure reputation recovery over 6-12 month period - Assess long-term stakeholder relationship changes - Evaluate effectiveness of different strategies employed

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Strategy Usage Patterns Across Crisis Types¶

    Corporate Financial Crises: - Denial (42% of messages) - particularly shifting blame to market conditions - Bolstering (31% of messages) - emphasis on past financial performance - Corrective Action (24% of messages) - new oversight and governance measures - Mortification (15% of messages) - limited to cases with clear evidence

    Product Safety Crises: - Corrective Action (58% of messages) - recalls and safety improvements dominate - Mortification (34% of messages) - higher than other crisis types due to consumer safety - Minimization (28% of messages) - scope and severity reduction attempts - Bolstering (22% of messages) - past safety record emphasis

    Executive Misconduct Scandals: - Shifting Blame (45% of messages) - often targeting individual rather than organization - Mortification (38% of messages) - acknowledgment of wrong and seeking forgiveness - Attacking Accusers (31% of messages) - questioning motives and credibility - Differentiation (29% of messages) - comparing to worse scandals

    Strategy Effectiveness Research Findings¶

    High Effectiveness Strategies: - Mortification + Corrective Action combination: 67% positive stakeholder response in post-crisis surveys - Corrective Action alone: 61% stakeholder approval when concrete measures implemented - Accident + Corrective Action: 58% effectiveness for operational failures

    Moderate Effectiveness Strategies: - Compensation + Mortification: 52% effectiveness, varies by compensation adequacy - Bolstering + Minimization: 48% effectiveness, depends on prior reputation strength - Good Intentions + Corrective Action: 47% effectiveness for well-intentioned failures

    Low Effectiveness Strategies: - Simple Denial: 31% effectiveness, drops to 18% when contradicted by evidence - Attacking Accusers alone: 29% effectiveness, often backfires by appearing defensive - Minimization alone: 26% effectiveness, particularly problematic for serious crises

    Cultural and Contextual Variations¶

    Individualistic Cultures (US, Western Europe): - Higher acceptance of mortification and personal accountability - Corrective action highly valued for demonstrating proactive response - Attack accuser strategies less effective due to fairness concerns

    Collectivistic Cultures (East Asia, Latin America): - Greater emphasis on harmony restoration and face-saving - Group responsibility acceptance more important than individual blame - Transcendence strategies more effective when invoking collective values

    Legal System Influences: - Common Law Systems: Higher use of denial and evading responsibility due to litigation concerns - Civil Law Systems: More frequent use of mortification and corrective action - Regulatory Environments: Strong regulatory contexts increase corrective action usage

    Digital Age Impact on Strategy Effectiveness¶

    Social Media Amplification Effects: - Traditional strategies require adaptation for viral communication environment - Real-time stakeholder feedback creates pressure for rapid strategy adjustment - User-generated content can undermine organizational narrative control

    Transparency Expectations: - Higher stakeholder expectations for information disclosure - Denial strategies less effective due to information accessibility - Corrective action strategies must include detailed implementation information

    9) Applications¶

    Crisis Communication Planning Framework¶

    Phase 1: Vulnerability Assessment - Identify potential reputation threats across organizational functions - Assess stakeholder expectations for different crisis scenarios - Evaluate organizational capabilities for various restoration strategies - Develop crisis-specific strategy templates

    Phase 2: Strategy Pre-Selection Matrix 

    Crisis Type | Primary Strategy | Secondary Strategy | Avoid Strategy
Product Defect | Corrective Action | Mortification | Denial
Financial Issue | Defeasibility | Corrective Action | Attack Accusers
Leadership Scandal | Mortification | Shifting Blame (to individual) | Simple Denial
External Attack | Bolstering | Attack Accusers | Mortification

    Phase 3: Implementation Protocol - Develop message templates for each strategy category - Establish approval processes for strategy selection and deployment - Create monitoring systems for stakeholder response assessment - Design adaptation procedures for strategy modification during crisis

    Reputation Management Integration¶

    Pre-Crisis Reputation Building: - Build positive reputation capital through consistent bolstering activities - Establish credible corrective action capabilities through operational excellence - Develop stakeholder relationships that support forgiveness during crises

    Crisis Response Optimization: - Match strategy selection to crisis characteristics and stakeholder expectations - Ensure strategy consistency across all communication channels and spokespersons - Monitor stakeholder reactions and adjust strategies based on feedback

    Post-Crisis Recovery: - Implement sustained corrective action to demonstrate lasting commitment - Use bolstering strategies to rebuild positive associations - Establish monitoring systems to track reputation recovery progress

    Industry-Specific Applications¶

    Healthcare Organizations: - Patient Safety Crises: Mortification + Corrective Action combination essential - Privacy Violations: Defeasibility (if applicable) + Prevention measures - Quality Issues: Accident + Comprehensive corrective action

    Technology Companies: - Data Breaches: Mortification + Corrective Action + Compensation - Product Failures: Defeasibility + Corrective Action - Privacy Concerns: Transcendence (innovation benefits) + Prevention

    Financial Services: - Misconduct Scandals: Mortification + Shifting Blame (to individuals) + Corrective Action - Market Losses: Defeasibility + Bolstering - Regulatory Violations: Mortification + Corrective Action

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to IRT Key Differences

    Impression Management Theory Self-presentation strategies Overlaps in reputation management focus IM broader scope; IRT crisis-specific

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Descriptive Rather Than Prescriptive Nature: Critics argue that IRT catalogs available strategies without providing sufficient guidance on which strategies to use when, limiting practical utility for crisis managers

    Limited Cultural Validation: Theory developed primarily in Western contexts with limited testing in diverse cultural settings, raising questions about universal applicability

    Insufficient Attention to Stakeholder Differences: IRT treats "audiences" as relatively homogeneous, not adequately addressing how different stakeholder groups may respond differently to the same strategies

    Static Strategy Conceptualization: Theory doesn't adequately address how strategies may need to evolve throughout a crisis lifecycle or how digital communication changes strategy dynamics

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Communication Challenges: - Speed Requirements: Social media demands immediate responses, potentially limiting careful strategy selection - Audience Fragmentation: Different stakeholder groups may receive different messages simultaneously - Narrative Control Loss: User-generated content and viral sharing can undermine organizational message control

    Stakeholder Activism Impact: - Power Shifts: Empowered stakeholder groups can reject traditional restoration strategies - Expectation Evolution: Modern stakeholders expect higher standards of accountability and response - Boycott Threats: Economic pressure can make certain strategies (like denial) more risky

    Legal and Ethical Considerations: - Admission of Guilt: Legal implications of mortification strategies in litigious environments - Transparency Standards: Expectations for organizational transparency may conflict with denial strategies - Stakeholder Rights: Balancing organizational interests with stakeholder harm mitigation

    Theoretical Developments¶

    SCCT Integration: Timothy Coombs' Situational Crisis Communication Theory builds on IRT by providing prescriptive guidance for strategy selection based on crisis type and organizational responsibility

    Digital Age Adaptations: Scholars developing modifications to account for social media, real-time communication, and changed stakeholder expectations

    Cultural Adaptations: Research exploring how IRT strategies must be modified for different cultural contexts and communication norms

    12) Cross-Cultural, Ethics, DEI¶

    Cultural Adaptations¶

    High-Context Cultures: - Indirect Communication: Strategies must be adapted for subtle, nuanced expression - Face-Saving: Mortification strategies require careful framing to maintain dignity - Collective Responsibility: Group accountability may be more important than individual blame

    Low-Context Cultures: - Direct Communication: Clear, explicit strategy implementation expected - Individual Accountability: Personal responsibility acceptance valued - Fact-Based Arguments: Evidence and logical reasoning prioritized in strategy presentation

    Power Distance Considerations: - High Power Distance: Authority figures expected to take responsibility and provide direction - Low Power Distance: Collaborative approach to problem-solving and responsibility sharing expected

    Ethical Framework¶

    Truth and Transparency: - Honest Communication: Strategies should not involve deception or misleading information - Information Disclosure: Stakeholders deserve accurate information about crisis impacts - Evidence-Based Claims: Factual support required for all strategy assertions

    Stakeholder Welfare: - Harm Mitigation: Priority placed on reducing stakeholder harm over organizational protection - Vulnerable Population Protection: Special consideration for populations unable to protect themselves - Long-term Relationship Focus: Strategies should support sustainable stakeholder relationships

    Professional Responsibility: - Competent Counsel: Communication professionals should recommend appropriate strategies - Conflict of Interest Management: Clear guidelines for situations involving competing loyalties - Continuous Learning: Staying current with best practices and ethical standards

    14) Implementation Notes¶

    Crisis Communication Strategy Selection Protocol¶

    Step 1: Crisis Assessment (Within 2 hours) - Responsibility Evaluation: Determine actual organizational responsibility level - Evidence Analysis: Assess strength and accessibility of supporting/contradicting evidence - Stakeholder Impact Assessment: Identify affected parties and severity of harm - Organizational Capability Review: Evaluate resources available for different response strategies

    Step 2: Strategy Selection Matrix (Within 4 hours) 

    High Responsibility + Strong Evidence → Mortification + Corrective Action
High Responsibility + Weak Evidence → Corrective Action + Bolstering
Low Responsibility + Strong Evidence → Shifting Blame + Bolstering
Low Responsibility + Weak Evidence → Simple Denial + Attack Accusers
Mixed Responsibility → Defeasibility + Corrective Action

    Step 3: Message Development (Within 6 hours) - Primary Strategy Implementation: Develop core messages emphasizing selected primary strategy - Secondary Strategy Integration: Incorporate supporting strategies without contradiction - Stakeholder Customization: Adapt messages for different stakeholder groups while maintaining consistency - Channel Optimization: Tailor message format and tone for different communication channels

    Step 4: Deployment and Monitoring (Ongoing) - Coordinated Release: Ensure all spokespersons and channels communicate consistent messages - Real-time Monitoring: Track stakeholder reactions across traditional and social media - Strategy Adjustment: Modify approach based on stakeholder feedback and evolving situation - Effectiveness Assessment: Measure reputation impact and stakeholder relationship changes

    Digital Age Implementation Framework¶

    Social Media Strategy Adaptation: - Speed Requirements: Pre-develop template responses for common crisis scenarios - Platform Customization: Adapt strategy implementation for Twitter (brevity), Facebook (detail), LinkedIn (professional tone) - User Engagement Protocol: Guidelines for responding to user comments and questions within strategy framework - Viral Risk Management: Procedures for rapid strategy adjustment if messages are misinterpreted or manipulated

    Multi-Channel Consistency: - Message Synchronization: Ensure identical core messages across traditional media, social media, and direct communications - Spokesperson Training: Brief all potential speakers on selected strategies and key talking points - Document Control: Maintain central repository of approved messages and talking points - Real-time Updates: System for rapidly updating all channels when strategies evolve

    Long-term Reputation Recovery Planning¶

    Phase 1: Immediate Response (Days 1-7) - Focus on primary crisis response using selected image restoration strategies - Establish stakeholder communication channels for ongoing dialogue - Begin implementation of corrective action measures - Monitor and document stakeholder reactions for strategy refinement

    Phase 2: Active Recovery (Weeks 2-12) - Demonstrate progress on corrective action commitments - Implement sustained bolstering activities highlighting organizational strengths - Engage in proactive stakeholder relationship rebuilding activities - Regular reputation monitoring and strategy adjustment

    Phase 3: Long-term Rebuilding (Months 3-12) - Complete implementation of all corrective action measures - Establish new organizational practices preventing crisis recurrence - Sustained positive communication emphasizing organizational values and contributions - Comprehensive reputation assessment and relationship quality evaluation

def image_restoration_content_analysis(crisis_communications):
    """
    Analyze crisis communications for image restoration strategy usage

    Parameters:
    crisis_communications (list): List of communication texts

    Returns:
    dict: Analysis of strategy usage and effectiveness indicators
    """

    # Strategy keyword dictionaries
    strategy_keywords = {
        'denial': ['deny', 'false', 'untrue', 'did not', 'never', 'not responsible'],
        'shifting_blame': ['caused by', 'due to', 'result of', 'blame', 'fault of'],
        'provocation': ['forced to', 'response to', 'retaliation', 'defend'],
        'defeasibility': ['did not know', 'unaware', 'circumstances', 'beyond control'],
        'accident': ['accident', 'unintentional', 'mistake', 'error', 'mishap'],
        'good_intentions': ['intended to', 'meant to', 'trying to', 'purpose was'],
        'bolstering': ['history of', 'track record', 'committed to', 'values', 'mission'],
        'minimization': ['minor', 'small', 'limited', 'minimal', 'few'],
        'differentiation': ['unlike', 'different from', 'not as bad as', 'compared to'],
        'transcendence': ['bigger picture', 'greater good', 'important to', 'mission'],
        'attack_accusers': ['false accusations', 'motivated by', 'biased', 'unfair'],
        'compensation': ['compensate', 'reimburse', 'make up for', 'pay for'],
        'corrective_action': ['fix', 'change', 'improve', 'implement', 'prevent'],
        'mortification': ['apologize', 'sorry', 'regret', 'responsible', 'forgive']
    }

    strategy_counts = {strategy: 0 for strategy in strategy_keywords.keys()}
    total_communications = len(crisis_communications)

    # Analyze each communication
    for communication in crisis_communications:
        communication_lower = communication.lower()

        for strategy, keywords in strategy_keywords.items():
            for keyword in keywords:
                if keyword in communication_lower:
                    strategy_counts[strategy] += 1
                    break  # Count each strategy only once per communication

    # Calculate strategy percentages
    strategy_percentages = {
        strategy: (count / total_communications) * 100 
        for strategy, count in strategy_counts.items()
    }

    # Group strategies by categories
    category_analysis = {
        'denial': strategy_percentages['denial'] + strategy_percentages['shifting_blame'],
        'evading_responsibility': (
            strategy_percentages['provocation'] + strategy_percentages['defeasibility'] + 
            strategy_percentages['accident'] + strategy_percentages['good_intentions']
        ),
        'reducing_offensiveness': (
            strategy_percentages['bolstering'] + strategy_percentages['minimization'] + 
            strategy_percentages['differentiation'] + strategy_percentages['transcendence'] + 
            strategy_percentages['attack_accusers'] + strategy_percentages['compensation']
        ),
        'corrective_action': strategy_percentages['corrective_action'],
        'mortification': strategy_percentages['mortification']
    }

    # Identify primary strategy
    primary_strategy = max(category_analysis.items(), key=lambda x: x[1])

    # Calculate sentiment analysis
    sentiment_scores = [TextBlob(comm).sentiment.polarity for comm in crisis_communications]
    average_sentiment = np.mean(sentiment_scores)

    return {
        'strategy_counts': strategy_counts,
        'strategy_percentages': strategy_percentages,
        'category_analysis': category_analysis,
        'primary_strategy': primary_strategy,
        'average_sentiment': average_sentiment,
        'total_communications': total_communications
    }

def crisis_effectiveness_predictor(crisis_data):
    """
    Predict crisis response effectiveness based on strategy usage and context

    Parameters:
    crisis_data (dict): Dictionary with crisis characteristics and strategy usage

    Returns:
    dict: Effectiveness prediction and recommendations
    """

    # Effectiveness weights based on research findings
    strategy_effectiveness = {
        'mortification': 0.67,
        'corrective_action': 0.61,
        'accident': 0.58,
        'compensation': 0.52,
        'bolstering': 0.48,
        'good_intentions': 0.47,
        'defeasibility': 0.45,
        'differentiation': 0.42,
        'provocation': 0.38,
        'transcendence': 0.35,
        'shifting_blame': 0.31,
        'attack_accusers': 0.29,
        'minimization': 0.26,
        'denial': 0.31
    }

    # Context modifiers
    responsibility_level = crisis_data.get('responsibility_level', 'medium')  # low, medium, high
    evidence_strength = crisis_data.get('evidence_strength', 'medium')  # weak, medium, strong
    crisis_severity = crisis_data.get('crisis_severity', 'medium')  # low, medium, high

    # Calculate weighted effectiveness
    used_strategies = crisis_data.get('strategy_usage', {})
    weighted_effectiveness = 0
    total_weight = 0

    for strategy, usage_percentage in used_strategies.items():
        if usage_percentage > 0:
            base_effectiveness = strategy_effectiveness.get(strategy, 0.4)

            # Apply context modifiers
            if responsibility_level == 'high' and strategy in ['denial', 'shifting_blame']:
                base_effectiveness *= 0.5  # Penalty for denial when clearly responsible
            elif responsibility_level == 'low' and strategy in ['mortification']:
                base_effectiveness *= 0.7  # Reduced effectiveness of apology when not responsible

            if evidence_strength == 'strong' and strategy == 'denial':
                base_effectiveness *= 0.3  # Strong penalty for denial with strong evidence

            if crisis_severity == 'high' and strategy == 'minimization':
                base_effectiveness *= 0.4  # Minimization less effective for severe crises

            weighted_effectiveness += base_effectiveness * (usage_percentage / 100)
            total_weight += usage_percentage / 100

    if total_weight > 0:
        predicted_effectiveness = weighted_effectiveness / total_weight
    else:
        predicted_effectiveness = 0.4  # Default moderate effectiveness

    # Generate recommendations
    recommendations = []

    if responsibility_level == 'high' and predicted_effectiveness < 0.5:
        recommendations.append("Consider increasing use of mortification and corrective action")

    if evidence_strength == 'strong' and 'denial' in used_strategies:
        recommendations.append("Avoid denial strategies when strong evidence exists")

    if crisis_severity == 'high' and predicted_effectiveness < 0.6:
        recommendations.append("Focus on corrective action and compensation for severe crises")

    return {
        'predicted_effectiveness': predicted_effectiveness,
        'effectiveness_category': 'High' if predicted_effectiveness > 0.6 
                                 else 'Medium' if predicted_effectiveness > 0.4 
                                 else 'Low',
        'recommendations': recommendations,
        'context_factors': {
            'responsibility_level': responsibility_level,
            'evidence_strength': evidence_strength,
            'crisis_severity': crisis_severity
        }
    }
