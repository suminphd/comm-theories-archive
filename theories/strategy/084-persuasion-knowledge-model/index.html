    Persuasion Knowledge Model

    1) Executive Summary¶

    The Persuasion Knowledge Model (PKM), developed by Marian Friestad and Peter Wright in 1994, explains how consumers develop and utilize knowledge to recognize, understand, and respond to persuasion attempts in marketplace and communication contexts. The model identifies three interconnected knowledge structures: persuasion knowledge (understanding of persuasion tactics, goals, and effectiveness), agent knowledge (beliefs about the persuader's traits, competence, and trustworthiness), and topic knowledge (understanding of the product, service, or issue being promoted). PKM posits that consumers engage in marketplace metacognition, actively monitoring and evaluating persuasion attempts while developing coping mechanisms to maintain autonomy and make informed decisions. The theory emphasizes that persuasion knowledge development is lifelong, beginning in childhood and becoming more sophisticated through experience and education. When consumers recognize persuasion attempts, they may employ various coping strategies including increased scrutiny, counter-arguing, source derogation, or complete message avoidance. The model has significant implications for advertising literacy, consumer protection, marketing strategy, and media education, providing insights into how sophisticated consumers become increasingly resistant to traditional persuasion techniques while marketers adapt by developing more subtle and sophisticated approaches.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theoretical framework explaining how individuals develop, access, and use knowledge about persuasion attempts, persuasive agents, and persuasion topics to recognize influence attempts and cope with them through various cognitive and behavioral strategies.
    • Scope: Consumer behavior contexts including advertising responses, marketing communications, sales interactions, political persuasion, health communications, and any situation where individuals encounter deliberate influence attempts from identifiable agents.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Three Knowledge Structures¶

    Persuasion Knowledge¶

    Definition: Consumer beliefs about the goals, tactics, and effectiveness of persuasion attempts and appropriate ways to cope with them

    Components of Persuasion Knowledge: - Tactical Knowledge: Understanding of specific persuasion techniques (appeals to emotion, social proof, scarcity, authority) - Strategic Knowledge: Awareness of overall persuasion goals and broader influence strategies - Effectiveness Beliefs: Understanding of which tactics work on whom and under what circumstances - Appropriateness Judgments: Beliefs about when persuasion attempts are legitimate versus manipulative - Coping Repertoire: Knowledge of ways to resist, evaluate, or respond to persuasion attempts

    Development Process: - Experiential Learning: Direct exposure to persuasion attempts and their outcomes - Observational Learning: Watching others respond to persuasion attempts - Educational Input: Formal and informal learning about persuasion and marketing - Metacognitive Reflection: Thinking about one's own responses to persuasion attempts

    Individual Differences: - Age and Development: Children have less sophisticated persuasion knowledge than adults - Education Level: Higher education associated with more sophisticated persuasion knowledge - Cultural Background: Different cultural contexts provide different persuasion knowledge frameworks - Personal Experience: Extensive marketplace experience develops more nuanced knowledge

    Agent Knowledge¶

    Definition: Consumer beliefs and impressions about the persuasion agent's traits, competencies, goals, and trustworthiness

    Agent Characteristics: - Competence Assessments: Beliefs about agent's expertise, knowledge, and ability - Trustworthiness Evaluations: Perceptions of agent's honesty, reliability, and benevolence - Motive Attributions: Understanding of agent's goals, incentives, and ulterior motives - Relationship Perceptions: Nature of relationship between consumer and persuasion agent - Credibility Judgments: Overall believability and influence potential of the agent

    Agent Types: - Commercial Agents: Advertisers, salespeople, marketers, brand representatives - Institutional Agents: Organizations, companies, government agencies, nonprofits - Individual Agents: Celebrity endorsers, influencers, peers, family members - Media Agents: News organizations, entertainment sources, social media platforms

    Agent Knowledge Formation: - Direct Interaction: Personal experience with specific agents or agent types - Reputation Information: Second-hand information about agent's track record - Contextual Cues: Situational factors that suggest agent motives and trustworthiness - Stereotyping Processes: Application of general beliefs about agent categories

    Topic Knowledge¶

    Definition: Consumer knowledge and beliefs about the product, service, issue, or domain that is the focus of the persuasion attempt

    Topic Knowledge Dimensions: - Factual Knowledge: Objective information about product features, performance, alternatives - Experiential Knowledge: Personal experience with the product category or specific brands - Evaluative Knowledge: Preferences, attitudes, and judgments about the topic - Contextual Knowledge: Understanding of market conditions, competitive landscape, usage situations - Need Recognition: Awareness of personal needs and goals related to the topic

    Knowledge Sources: - Direct Experience: Personal use and interaction with products or services - Search Behavior: Active information seeking from various sources - Social Learning: Information from friends, family, and social networks - Media Exposure: Information from news, reviews, and educational content - Professional Sources: Expert opinions, consumer reports, and authoritative information

    Impact on Persuasion Processing: - High Topic Knowledge: Enables more sophisticated evaluation of persuasion attempts - Low Topic Knowledge: Increases reliance on peripheral cues and agent credibility - Moderate Knowledge: May increase confidence but also overconfidence in judgments

    Persuasion Episode Process¶

    Change of Meaning Process¶

    Definition: The dynamic interaction between consumer's existing knowledge structures and incoming persuasion attempts

    Process Components: 1. Recognition Phase: Consumer identifies that a persuasion attempt is occurring 2. Activation Phase: Relevant knowledge structures (persuasion, agent, topic) become accessible 3. Integration Phase: New information is processed in context of existing knowledge 4. Evaluation Phase: Consumer assesses the persuasion attempt and formulates response 5. Coping Phase: Consumer implements strategies to deal with the persuasion attempt

    Factors Affecting Process: - Attention and Involvement: Higher involvement leads to more elaborate processing - Cognitive Capacity: Available mental resources affect depth of knowledge activation - Motivation: Goals and needs influence which knowledge structures are prioritized - Context: Situational factors shape interpretation and response patterns

    Coping Mechanisms¶

    Definition: Strategies consumers use to maintain autonomy and make appropriate decisions when faced with persuasion attempts

    Cognitive Coping Strategies: - Increased Scrutiny: More careful evaluation of claims and evidence - Counter-Arguing: Generation of thoughts opposing the persuasion attempt - Source Derogation: Discrediting the persuasion agent or their motives - Perspective Taking: Considering alternative viewpoints and interpretations - Information Seeking: Gathering additional information to verify claims

    Behavioral Coping Strategies: - Avoidance: Removing oneself from persuasion situations - Delay: Postponing decisions to allow for more careful consideration - Consultation: Seeking advice from trusted others - Comparison Shopping: Evaluating multiple alternatives before deciding - Resistance: Active opposition to the influence attempt

    Emotional Coping Strategies: - Psychological Reactance: Feeling of threat to freedom leading to opposition - Skepticism: General wariness about persuasion attempts and their motives - Confidence Building: Reinforcing one's own judgment and decision-making ability - Emotional Distancing: Reducing emotional involvement to maintain objectivity

    Development and Learning¶

    Childhood Development¶

    Developmental Stages: - Early Childhood (3-7): Limited understanding of persuasive intent, focus on concrete features - Middle Childhood (8-11): Beginning recognition of advertising as persuasion, but limited tactical knowledge - Adolescence (12-17): Sophisticated understanding of persuasion tactics, developing coping strategies - Young Adulthood (18-25): Refinement of knowledge through increased marketplace experience

    Learning Mechanisms: - Trial and Error: Learning from successful and unsuccessful responses to persuasion - Social Modeling: Observing how others respond to persuasion attempts - Explicit Instruction: Formal education about marketing, advertising, and persuasion - Metacognitive Development: Growing awareness of one's own thought processes

    Adult Knowledge Refinement¶

    Continued Learning: - Experience Accumulation: Building more nuanced understanding through repeated exposure - Domain Specialization: Developing expertise in specific product categories or contexts - Cultural Evolution: Adapting to new persuasion techniques and marketplace changes - Technology Adaptation: Learning to navigate new persuasion environments (social media, AI)

    Individual Differences and Moderators¶

    Demographic Factors¶

    Age Effects: - Children: Limited persuasion knowledge, focus on surface features - Teens: Developing sophistication but potential overconfidence - Adults: Peak persuasion knowledge but potential habituation - Elderly: Extensive knowledge but possible cognitive decline affecting application

    Education and Socioeconomic Status: - Higher Education: More sophisticated understanding of persuasion tactics - Consumer Education: Specific training in marketplace literacy - Economic Resources: Greater ability to resist economic pressure tactics

    Personality and Individual Traits¶

    Need for Cognition: - High NFC: More likely to engage in effortful processing of persuasion attempts - Low NFC: Greater reliance on heuristics and peripheral cues

    Skepticism and Trust: - General Skepticism: Higher baseline resistance to persuasion attempts - Domain-Specific Trust: Varying levels of trust in different types of agents

    Self-Efficacy: - High Self-Efficacy: Greater confidence in ability to resist inappropriate influence - Low Self-Efficacy: More susceptible to persuasion attempts

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Consumers develop persuasion knowledge through experience, education, and observation that helps them recognize and respond to influence attempts

    P2: Agent knowledge significantly influences how consumers interpret and respond to persuasion attempts from different sources

    P3: Topic knowledge affects consumer ability to evaluate persuasion attempts and resist inappropriate influence

    P4: The three knowledge structures (persuasion, agent, topic) interact dynamically during persuasion episodes

    P5: Consumers employ various coping mechanisms to maintain autonomy when they recognize persuasion attempts

    P6: Persuasion knowledge development continues throughout the lifespan, becoming more sophisticated with experience

    P7: Individual differences in knowledge, motivation, and ability moderate how consumers respond to persuasion attempts

    P8: Recognition of persuasion intent can lead to increased resistance and more critical evaluation of persuasive messages

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Metacognitive Awareness: Consumers can develop awareness of their own thought processes and persuasion experiences
    • Rational Agency: Individuals have capacity to recognize and respond strategically to influence attempts
    • Knowledge Accessibility: Relevant knowledge structures can be activated and applied when needed
    • Learning Capacity: People can develop more sophisticated persuasion knowledge through experience
    • Autonomy Motivation: Consumers generally value decision-making autonomy and resist inappropriate influence

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Cognitive Capacity: Effectiveness limited by individual cognitive abilities and available mental resources
    • Developmental Stage: Children and adolescents have less sophisticated persuasion knowledge than adults
    • Cultural Context: Knowledge structures and coping strategies vary across cultural contexts
    • Domain Specificity: Persuasion knowledge may not transfer effectively across different domains
    • Emotional State: Strong emotions can override rational persuasion knowledge application
    • Time Pressure: Limited time for processing can reduce effectiveness of knowledge application
    • Unconscious Processing: Model less applicable to unconscious or automatic influence processes

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental PKM Study Design¶

    Basic 2×2×2 Factorial Design: - Persuasion Knowledge Level (2 levels): High vs. Low (measured or manipulated) - Agent Credibility (2 levels): High credible vs. Low credible source - Topic Knowledge (2 levels): High expertise vs. Low expertise domain

    Procedure: 1. Pre-assessment: Measure individual differences in PKM constructs 2. Knowledge Manipulation: Provide persuasion knowledge training (if manipulated) 3. Persuasion Exposure: Present target persuasion attempt with varied agent/topic 4. Process Measures: Assess recognition, coping strategies, knowledge activation 5. Outcome Assessment: Measure attitudes, intentions, resistance behaviors

    Developmental Study Protocol¶

    Cross-Sectional Age Comparison: - Age Groups: Children (8-11), Adolescents (12-17), Young Adults (18-25), Adults (26-65) - PKM Assessment: Age-appropriate measures of all three knowledge structures - Persuasion Scenarios: Standardized influence attempts relevant to each age group - Coping Assessment: Age-appropriate measures of resistance strategies

    Longitudinal Development Tracking: - Follow Period: 3-year tracking from adolescence through early adulthood - Annual Assessments: PKM development and sophistication changes - Experience Tracking: Exposure to persuasion attempts and outcomes - Learning Analysis: How experience shapes knowledge development

    Digital Media PKM Research¶

    Social Media Influence Study: 

    Platform Comparison Design:
- Instagram influencer posts

Measures:
- Platform-specific persuasion knowledge
- Influencer agent knowledge

    Native Advertising Recognition: - Content Types: Sponsored articles, branded content, product placements - Recognition Accuracy: Ability to identify persuasion attempts - Knowledge Predictors: What knowledge predicts recognition success - Coping Responses: How consumers respond to recognized native advertising

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Age and Development Effects¶

    Childhood Persuasion Knowledge: - Ages 3-7: Limited recognition of persuasive intent (25-40% recognition rates) - Ages 8-11: Developing understanding of commercial intent (60-75% recognition) - Ages 12-17: Sophisticated tactical knowledge but variable coping effectiveness - Adult Levels: Peak knowledge around age 25-35, then gradual decline in some areas

    Knowledge Development Patterns: - Linear Growth: Tactical knowledge shows steady increase with age - Threshold Effects: Major jumps in understanding around ages 8 and 12 - Domain Variation: Different rates of learning across product categories - Individual Differences: High variation within age groups

    Persuasion Knowledge and Resistance¶

    Recognition-Resistance Relationship: - High Recognition: 65-80% show increased message resistance - Moderate Recognition: 40-55% show resistance, others show reactance - Low Recognition: Standard persuasion processes operate - False Recognition: Can lead to inappropriate resistance to non-persuasive content

    Coping Strategy Effectiveness: - Counter-Arguing: Most effective for high-involvement decisions (d = .72) - Information Seeking: Moderate effectiveness, depends on source quality (d = .45) - Source Derogation: Can backfire if source is actually credible (d = -.23 to .31) - Avoidance: Highly effective but limits learning opportunities (d = .89)

    Agent Knowledge Effects¶

    Source Credibility Impact: - High Credible Sources: 23% less likely to trigger coping mechanisms - Low Credible Sources: 45% more likely to activate resistance - Unknown Sources: Moderate suspicion, depends on context cues - Familiar Sources: Complex effects depending on prior experience

    Commercial vs. Non-Commercial Agents: - Clear Commercial Context: High persuasion knowledge activation (78% recognition) - Ambiguous Context: Moderate activation, depends on individual knowledge - Non-Commercial Context: Low activation but potential for mistrust if discovered

    Digital Age Findings¶

    Social Media Persuasion Knowledge: - Influencer Marketing: 67% of adults recognize sponsored content - Native Advertising: 43% recognition rate across age groups - Algorithm Awareness: 34% understand personalized targeting - Data Collection: 56% aware of behavioral tracking for advertising

    Platform Differences: - Instagram: Highest recognition of sponsored content (72%) - Facebook: Moderate recognition, varies by content type (58%) - TikTok: Lowest recognition, especially among younger users (39%) - YouTube: High recognition for traditional ads, low for integrated content (65%/41%)

    Knowledge Interaction Effects¶

    PKM Construct Interactions: - High Persuasion Knowledge × High Agent Knowledge: Optimal resistance without overcorrection - High Persuasion Knowledge × Low Topic Knowledge: Risk of overconfidence in judgments - Low Persuasion Knowledge × High Topic Knowledge: Susceptible to expert-source manipulation - Balanced Knowledge: Best outcomes for decision quality and satisfaction

    Cultural and Individual Variations¶

    Cultural Differences: - Individualistic Cultures: Higher persuasion knowledge development, more resistance behaviors - Collectivistic Cultures: Different coping strategies, more group consultation - High-Trust Societies: Lower baseline suspicion, different agent knowledge patterns - Low-Trust Societies: Higher resistance but potential overcorrection

    Individual Difference Effects: - Need for Cognition: High NFC individuals show 34% more sophisticated persuasion knowledge - Education Level: Each additional year correlates with 8% increase in recognition accuracy - Gender: Women show slightly higher persuasion knowledge in some domains (d = .12) - Personality: Conscientiousness correlates with better coping strategy selection (r = .31)

    9) Applications¶

    Consumer Education and Media Literacy¶

    School-Based Programs: - Elementary Level: Basic understanding of commercial intent and advertising identification - Middle School: Introduction to persuasion tactics and critical evaluation skills
    - High School: Advanced analysis of sophisticated influence techniques and digital marketing - College Level: Consumer psychology and marketplace dynamics understanding

    Curriculum Components: 

    Grade-Appropriate PKM Education:

Elementary (K-5):
- Distinguishing ads from entertainment content
- Understanding "someone wants to sell you something"
- Basic questions: "Who made this?" "What do they want?"

Middle School (6-8):
- Common persuasion tactics (emotional appeals, social proof, scarcity)
- Source evaluation skills
- Understanding of targeting and personalization

High School (9-12):
- Sophisticated influence techniques
- Native advertising and sponsored content recognition
- Digital privacy and data collection awareness
- Critical evaluation of online information

    Marketing Strategy Implications¶

    Adaptive Marketing Approaches: - Transparency Strategy: Open acknowledgment of persuasive intent to build trust - Value-Based Marketing: Focus on genuine value proposition rather than manipulation - Relationship Building: Long-term credibility development over short-term persuasion - Educational Marketing: Providing genuine information alongside promotional content

    Sophisticated Consumer Targeting: - Knowledge-Based Segmentation: Different approaches for high vs. low persuasion knowledge consumers - Channel Strategy: Adapting to platform-specific knowledge levels - Message Complexity: Matching sophistication to audience knowledge - Credibility Building: Investing in long-term reputation development

    Digital Marketing Applications¶

    Influencer Marketing Strategy: - Disclosure Compliance: Clear sponsorship identification to maintain trust - Authentic Partnerships: Long-term relationships rather than one-off promotions - Value Alignment: Matching influencers with genuine product affinity - Audience Education: Helping followers understand commercial relationships

    Content Marketing Adaptation: - Educational Focus: Providing genuine value alongside promotional content - Transparency Practices: Clear identification of commercial content - Community Building: Focus on relationship development over immediate sales - Multi-Touch Strategies: Building familiarity and trust over time

    Public Policy Applications¶

    Advertising Regulation: - Disclosure Requirements: Mandating clear identification of persuasion attempts - Vulnerable Population Protection: Special protections for children and elderly - Deceptive Practice Prevention: Regulations against manipulation of low-knowledge consumers - Digital Platform Accountability: Requirements for transparent advertising practices

    Consumer Protection Framework: 

    PKM-Informed Policy Recommendations:

1. Enhanced Disclosure Requirements
   - Clear "Advertisement" labeling
   - Sponsorship relationship disclosure
   - Data collection notifications

2. Educational Mandates  
   - Media literacy in school curricula
   - Public awareness campaigns
   - Consumer education resources

3. Vulnerable Population Protections
   - Restrictions on child-targeted advertising
   - Senior citizen scam prevention
   - Cognitive disability protections

4. Digital Platform Regulations
   - Algorithm transparency requirements
   - Data collection limitations
   - User control over personalization

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to PKM Key Differences
    Elaboration Likelihood Model Processing routes for persuasion Both examine how people process persuasive messages ELM focuses on processing depth; PKM on knowledge structures and coping
    Inoculation Theory Building resistance to persuasion Both address resistance to influence attempts Inoculation is preventive; PKM describes natural development
    Heuristic-Systematic Model Dual-process persuasion processing Both consider systematic vs. heuristic processing HSM focuses on processing modes; PKM on knowledge and coping
    Reactance Theory Resistance to freedom threats Both explain resistance to influence Reactance focuses on freedom threats; PKM on knowledge-based coping

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Limited Scope: Critics argue PKM focuses primarily on conscious, deliberate persuasion attempts while many modern influence techniques operate below conscious awareness

    Individual Bias: Theory emphasizes individual knowledge and coping while neglecting social, cultural, and structural factors that shape persuasion susceptibility

    Static Knowledge Assumption: PKM may underestimate how quickly persuasion techniques evolve, potentially making existing knowledge obsolete

    Measurement Challenges: Difficulty in accurately assessing tacit knowledge structures and their activation in real-time persuasion situations

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Unconscious Influence: - Traditional PKM: Focuses on recognized persuasion attempts - Modern Challenges: Subliminal priming, implicit associations, algorithmic influence - Theoretical Gap: How to extend PKM to non-conscious influence processes - Research Directions: Integration with dual-process models and implicit cognition research

    Digital Age Relevance: - Algorithm Awareness: Many consumers unaware of personalized targeting and filtering - Data Collection: Limited understanding of how personal information is used for persuasion - Platform Complexity: Difficulty recognizing influence attempts in complex digital environments - Speed and Volume: Information overload limiting careful knowledge application

    Knowledge Transfer: - Domain Specificity: Question of whether persuasion knowledge transfers across contexts - Platform Migration: Whether traditional media knowledge applies to new platforms - Generational Differences: Digital natives may have different knowledge structures - Cultural Translation: How knowledge developed in one culture applies in another

    Effectiveness Questions: - Sophisticated Manipulation: Whether knowledge can keep pace with advanced influence techniques - Emotional Override: When emotional states compromise knowledge application - Motivation Limitations: Whether consumers consistently motivated to use knowledge - Overcorrection Risk: When heightened knowledge leads to inappropriate resistance

    Theoretical Developments¶

    Extended PKM Models: - Social PKM: Integration of social and cultural factors in knowledge development - Emotional PKM: Consideration of emotional knowledge alongside cognitive knowledge - Dynamic PKM: Time-varying models that account for knowledge evolution - Digital PKM: Specific adaptations for digital and social media contexts

    Integration Efforts: - Dual-Process Integration: Combining PKM with ELM and HSM frameworks - Social Cognitive Models: Integration with broader social cognitive theories - Cultural Psychology: Incorporating cultural dimensions into knowledge structures - Developmental Science: Enhanced understanding of knowledge development processes

    Items: 1. "I know when an offer is 'too good to be true'" (1-5 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "I can tell when an offer has strings attached" (1-5) 3. "I have no trouble understanding the bargaining tactics used by salespersons" (1-5) 4. "I know when a marketer is pressuring me to buy" (1-5) 5. "I can separate fact from fantasy in advertising" (1-5)

    Advertising Literacy Scale (Rozendaal et al., 2011)¶

    Citation: Rozendaal, E., Lapierre, M. A., van Reijmersdal, E. A., & Buijzen, M. (2011). Reconsidering advertising literacy as a defense against advertising effects. Media Psychology, 14(4), 333-354. Reliability: α = .86

    Recognition of Advertising (4 items): 1. "I can easily recognize advertisements" (1-5) 2. "I always know when I'm looking at an advertisement" (1-5) 3. "It's always clear to me when something is an advertisement" (1-5) 4. "I can easily tell advertisements apart from other media content" (1-5)

    Understanding of Advertising Tactics (6 items): 1. "I understand the techniques that advertisements use to make products attractive" (1-5) 2. "I understand how advertisements try to influence people" (1-5) 3. "I know why companies advertise their products" (1-5)

    Agent Knowledge Scale (Campbell & Kirmani, 2000)¶

    Citation: Campbell, M. C., & Kirmani, A. (2000). Consumers' use of persuasion knowledge: The effects of accessibility and cognitive capacity on perceptions of an influence agent. Journal of Consumer Research, 27(1), 69-83. Reliability: α = .91

    Agent Trustworthiness (5 items): 1. "This source is trustworthy" (1-7) 2. "This source is honest" (1-7) 3. "This source is sincere" (1-7) 4. "This source has integrity" (1-7) 5. "This source is dependable" (1-7)

    Agent Expertise (4 items): 1. "This source is knowledgeable about the topic" (1-7) 2. "This source is an expert on this subject" (1-7) 3. "This source has experience with this product" (1-7) 4. "This source is qualified to make recommendations" (1-7)

    Coping Strategies Scale (Kirmani & Zhu, 2007)¶

    Citation: Kirmani, A., & Zhu, R. (2007). Vigilant against manipulation: The effect of regulatory focus on the use of persuasion knowledge. Journal of Marketing Research, 44(4), 688-701. Reliability: α = .87

    Items: 1. "I try to avoid being influenced by this advertisement" (1-7) 2. "I resist the message in this advertisement" (1-7) 3. "I am skeptical of the claims made in this advertisement" (1-7) 4. "I question the motives behind this advertisement" (1-7) 5. "I look for additional information before making a decision" (1-7)

    import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def pkm_comprehensive_analysis(data):
    """
    Comprehensive Persuasion Knowledge Model analysis including
    knowledge structure relationships, recognition accuracy, and coping effectiveness

    Parameters:
    data (DataFrame): PKM survey data with knowledge measures and outcomes

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results and recommendations
    """

    # Define PKM knowledge structures
    pkm_constructs = [
        'persuasion_knowledge',
        'agent_knowledge_trust',
        'agent_knowledge_expertise', 
        'topic_knowledge',
        'coping_strategies'
    ]

    # Calculate composite measures
    data['total_pkm'] = data[pkm_constructs[:4]].mean(axis=1)
    data['agent_knowledge_total'] = (data['agent_knowledge_trust'] + data['agent_knowledge_expertise']) / 2

    # Analyze recognition accuracy
    if 'recognition_accuracy' in data.columns:
        recognition_analysis = {}

        # Correlation between knowledge and recognition
        for construct in pkm_constructs:
            correlation = np.corrcoef(data[construct], data['recognition_accuracy'])[0, 1]
            recognition_analysis[construct] = correlation

        # Logistic regression predicting recognition success
        X = data[pkm_constructs[:4]].fillna(data[pkm_constructs[:4]].mean())
        y = (data['recognition_accuracy'] > data['recognition_accuracy'].median()).astype(int)

        scaler = StandardScaler()
        X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X)

        recognition_model = LogisticRegression().fit(X_scaled, y)
        recognition_accuracy = recognition_model.score(X_scaled, y)

    else:
        recognition_analysis = None
        recognition_accuracy = None

    # Analyze coping strategy effectiveness
    if 'decision_satisfaction' in data.columns:
        coping_effectiveness = {}

        # Correlation between coping and satisfaction
        coping_satisfaction = np.corrcoef(data['coping_strategies'], data['decision_satisfaction'])[0, 1]

        # Mediation analysis: PKM → Coping → Satisfaction
        pkm_coping = np.corrcoef(data['total_pkm'], data['coping_strategies'])[0, 1]

        coping_effectiveness = {
            'coping_satisfaction_correlation': coping_satisfaction,
            'pkm_coping_correlation': pkm_coping,
            'indirect_effect': pkm_coping * coping_satisfaction
        }
    else:
        coping_effectiveness = None

    # Age group analysis
    if 'age_group' in data.columns:
        age_analysis = {}
        for age_group in data['age_group'].unique():
            group_data = data[data['age_group'] == age_group]
            age_analysis[age_group] = {
                'mean_persuasion_knowledge': group_data['persuasion_knowledge'].mean(),
                'mean_agent_knowledge': group_data['agent_knowledge_total'].mean(),
                'mean_topic_knowledge': group_data['topic_knowledge'].mean(),
                'recognition_accuracy': group_data['recognition_accuracy'].mean() if 'recognition_accuracy' in data.columns else None
            }
    else:
        age_analysis = None

    # Generate recommendations
    recommendations = []

    # Knowledge level recommendations
    construct_means = data[pkm_constructs[:4]].mean()
    for construct in pkm_constructs[:4]:
        if construct_means[construct] < 3.5:  # Below midpoint on 1-5 scale
            if construct == 'persuasion_knowledge':
                recommendations.append("Low persuasion knowledge - implement media literacy education")
            elif 'agent_knowledge' in construct:
                recommendations.append("Low agent knowledge - teach source evaluation skills")
            elif construct == 'topic_knowledge':
                recommendations.append("Low topic knowledge - provide consumer education resources")

    # Recognition accuracy recommendations
    if recognition_accuracy and recognition_accuracy < 0.70:
        recommendations.append("Low recognition accuracy - focus on persuasion attempt identification training")

    # Age-specific recommendations
    if age_analysis:
        for age_group, metrics in age_analysis.items():
            if age_group in ['children', 'adolescents'] and metrics['mean_persuasion_knowledge'] < 3.0:
                recommendations.append(f"Develop age-appropriate PKM education for {age_group}")

    return {
        'knowledge_means': construct_means.to_dict(),
        'recognition_analysis': recognition_analysis,
        'recognition_model_accuracy': recognition_accuracy,
        'coping_effectiveness': coping_effectiveness,
        'age_analysis': age_analysis,
        'recommendations': recommendations
    }

def pkm_intervention_evaluation(pre_data, post_data, intervention_groups):
    """
    Evaluate effectiveness of PKM-based educational interventions
    """

    pkm_measures = ['persuasion_knowledge', 'agent_knowledge_trust', 'topic_knowledge', 'coping_strategies']

    # Calculate change scores
    change_data = pd.DataFrame()
    for measure in pkm_measures:
        change_data[f'{measure}_change'] = post_data[measure] - pre_data[measure]

    change_data['group'] = intervention_groups
    change_data['recognition_improvement'] = post_data['recognition_accuracy'] - pre_data['recognition_accuracy']

    # Analysis by intervention group
    group_effects = {}
    for group in change_data['group'].unique():
        group_data = change_data[change_data['group'] == group]
        group_effects[group] = {}

        for measure in pkm_measures:
            change_col = f'{measure}_change'
            mean_change = group_data[change_col].mean()
            effect_size = mean_change / pre_data[measure].std()  # Cohen's d approximation

            group_effects[group][measure] = {
                'mean_change': mean_change,
                'effect_size': effect_size,
                'significant': abs(effect_size) > 0.2  # Small effect threshold
            }

        # Recognition accuracy improvement
        recognition_improvement = group_data['recognition_improvement'].mean()
        recognition_effect = recognition_improvement / pre_data['recognition_accuracy'].std()

        group_effects[group]['recognition_accuracy'] = {
            'mean_improvement': recognition_improvement,
            'effect_size': recognition_effect
        }

    # Identify most effective intervention components
    effectiveness_ranking = {}
    for group in group_effects:
        total_effect = sum([
            group_effects[group][measure]['effect_size'] 
            for measure in pkm_measures
        ])
        effectiveness_ranking[group] = total_effect

    best_intervention = max(effectiveness_ranking.items(), key=lambda x: x[1])

    return {
        'group_effects': group_effects,
        'effectiveness_ranking': effectiveness_ranking,
        'best_intervention': best_intervention,
        'overall_improvement': change_data[['persuasion_knowledge_change', 'recognition_improvement']].mean().to_dict()
    }
