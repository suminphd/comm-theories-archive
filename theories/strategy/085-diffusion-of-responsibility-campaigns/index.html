    1) Executive Summary¶

    Diffusion of Responsibility in Campaigns explains how individual accountability and participation decrease as group size increases, creating challenges for campaign mobilization and collective action. Originally derived from Latané and Darley's bystander effect research (1968), the theory has been adapted to understand why people are less likely to participate in campaigns when they perceive that many others are available to act. The theory identifies three core mechanisms: responsibility diffusion (spreading accountability across group members), social loafing (reduced individual effort in group settings), and the bystander effect (decreased helping behavior in group situations). In campaign contexts, this manifests as lower participation rates in large-scale movements, reduced individual contribution levels, and increased free-riding behavior where individuals benefit from others' actions without contributing themselves. The theory suggests that perceived group size, anonymity, lack of personal accountability, and unclear individual roles all contribute to responsibility diffusion. Effective campaign strategies to counter these effects include personalization of messages, assignment of specific roles, creation of smaller action groups, establishment of individual accountability mechanisms, and emphasis on unique individual contributions. Understanding diffusion of responsibility is crucial for designing campaigns that maintain high levels of individual engagement and avoid the collective action problems that plague many social movements and advocacy efforts.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theoretical framework explaining how individual sense of responsibility and likelihood of action decreases as the perceived size of the potential action group increases, leading to reduced participation in campaigns, social movements, and collective action initiatives.
    • Scope: Campaign and collective action contexts including political mobilization, social movements, environmental campaigns, health promotion, fundraising efforts, volunteer recruitment, and any situation requiring coordinated group action toward shared goals.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Core Mechanisms¶

    Responsibility Diffusion¶

    Definition: The psychological process by which individuals feel less personally responsible for outcomes when they believe others share that responsibility

    Cognitive Components: - Shared Accountability: "Others can and should act, so my individual action is less critical" - Redundancy Perception: "If I don't act, someone else probably will" - Contribution Minimization: "My individual contribution won't make a significant difference" - Role Ambiguity: "It's not clear what specifically I should do"

    Measurement Indicators: - Perceived personal responsibility for campaign outcomes - Belief in necessity of individual contribution - Attribution of campaign success/failure to group vs. individual efforts - Clarity of personal role in campaign activities

    Factors Influencing Diffusion: - Group Size: Larger groups create greater diffusion - Anonymity: Less identifiable individuals feel less responsible - Task Clarity: Ambiguous tasks increase diffusion - Leadership Structure: Clear leadership reduces diffusion for followers

    Social Loafing¶

    Definition: The tendency for individuals to exert less effort when working collectively than when working individually

    Manifestations in Campaigns: - Reduced Time Investment: Spending less time on campaign activities - Lower Quality Contributions: Providing less thoughtful or comprehensive input - Decreased Initiative: Waiting for others to take action rather than initiating - Minimal Compliance: Meeting only basic expectations rather than going above and beyond

    Contributing Factors: - Evaluation Apprehension: Reduced concern about individual performance evaluation - Output Equity: Matching perceived effort levels of other group members - Goal Salience: Reduced focus on collective goals in favor of individual interests - Identifiability: Difficulty in attributing individual contributions to outcomes

    Contextual Moderators: - Task Importance: Higher importance reduces loafing - Group Cohesion: Stronger group bonds decrease loafing - Individual Differences: Some personality types more prone to loafing - Cultural Background: Collectivistic cultures show less loafing

    Bystander Effect¶

    Definition: The phenomenon where individuals are less likely to offer help or take action when other potential helpers are present

    Application to Campaigns: - Participation Inhibition: Reduced likelihood of joining campaign activities - Donation Behavior: Lower individual contribution amounts in group fundraising - Volunteer Recruitment: Difficulty motivating action when many potential volunteers exist - Emergency Response: Slower reaction to campaign crises or urgent needs

    Underlying Processes: - Pluralistic Ignorance: Misinterpreting others' inaction as indicating no action is needed - Audience Inhibition: Concern about being judged negatively by others for taking action - Responsibility Diffusion: Feeling less accountable when others are present

    Campaign-Specific Applications¶

    Political Campaign Participation¶

    Voter Mobilization Challenges: - Large Electorate Effect: Individual voters feel less impact in large elections - Campaign Volunteer Recruitment: Difficulty motivating sustained volunteer effort - Donation Behavior: Smaller individual contributions when donor base is large - Grassroots Organization: Challenges in maintaining individual engagement

    Manifestation Patterns: - Higher turnout in small, local elections vs. large national elections - Reduced volunteer retention as campaign organization grows - Lower per-capita fundraising efficiency in large-scale campaigns - Increased free-riding behavior (benefiting from others' campaign work)

    Social Movement Mobilization¶

    Collective Action Problems: - Movement Size Paradox: Larger movements face greater participation challenges - Sustaining Engagement: Maintaining individual commitment over time - Resource Mobilization: Ensuring adequate individual contributions - Leadership Development: Preventing over-reliance on core activists

    Strategic Implications: - Need for smaller, identifiable action groups within larger movements - Importance of clear, specific roles for individual participants - Value of personal accountability mechanisms - Benefits of celebrating individual contributions

    Health Campaign Participation¶

    Public Health Behavior Change: - Community Health Initiatives: Reduced individual compliance in large-scale programs - Vaccination Campaigns: Free-riding on herd immunity - Environmental Health: Reliance on others for pollution reduction - Lifestyle Change Programs: Reduced individual effort in group settings

    Individual Difference Moderators¶

    Personality Factors¶

    Social Responsibility Orientation: - High Social Responsibility: Less susceptible to diffusion effects - Low Social Responsibility: More prone to responsibility diffusion - Altruistic vs. Egoistic Motivation: Different responses to group size effects

    Individual vs. Collective Orientation: - Individualistic Orientation: May increase diffusion effects - Collectivistic Orientation: May reduce diffusion through group identification - Social Identity Strength: Stronger group identity reduces loafing

    Demographic Variables¶

    Age Effects: - Young Adults: May show stronger diffusion effects in some contexts - Older Adults: Experience and civic engagement may reduce diffusion - Generational Differences: Different patterns of collective action participation

    Socioeconomic Status: - Higher SES: May have more resources but also more alternatives - Lower SES: May show different patterns of collective vs. individual action - Educational Level: Higher education may increase understanding of collective action problems

    Situational Factors¶

    Group Characteristics¶

    Group Size: - Small Groups (2-5): Minimal diffusion effects, high individual accountability - Medium Groups (6-15): Moderate diffusion, some loss of individual responsibility - Large Groups (16+): Significant diffusion effects, substantial responsibility loss

    Group Composition: - Homogeneous Groups: May show less diffusion through shared identity - Heterogeneous Groups: Potential for increased diffusion and coordination problems - Status Differences: Hierarchy can either increase or decrease diffusion

    Task Characteristics¶

    Task Type: - Additive Tasks: Individual contributions sum to group outcome (more loafing) - Disjunctive Tasks: Group success depends on best member (less loafing) - Conjunctive Tasks: Group limited by weakest member (variable effects)

    Task Importance: - High Importance: Reduces diffusion effects - Personal Relevance: Individual stake in outcome affects participation - Urgency: Time pressure can either increase or decrease diffusion

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Individual likelihood of campaign participation decreases as perceived group size increases

    P2: Personal sense of responsibility for campaign outcomes diminishes with larger group membership

    P3: Individual effort in collective campaign activities decreases as group size increases (social loafing)

    P4: The presence of other potential participants reduces individual helping behavior (bystander effect)

    P5: Anonymity within groups increases diffusion of responsibility and reduces participation

    P6: Clear individual roles and accountability mechanisms reduce diffusion effects

    P7: Group identification and cohesion moderate the relationship between group size and individual participation

    P8: Task importance and personal relevance reduce susceptibility to diffusion of responsibility

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Rational Actors: Individuals make conscious or unconscious calculations about their responsibility and effort
    • Social Awareness: People are aware of others' potential to contribute to collective action
    • Goal Sharing: Group members generally share common campaign goals
    • Effort Conservation: Individuals naturally seek to minimize unnecessary effort expenditure
    • Social Comparison: People evaluate their contributions relative to others in the group

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Crisis Situations: Extreme urgency may override diffusion effects
    • Strong Personal Stakes: High individual investment may reduce diffusion
    • Cultural Context: Effects may vary across individualistic vs. collectivistic cultures
    • Leadership Presence: Strong, visible leadership can mitigate diffusion
    • Legal/Moral Obligations: Formal duties may override psychological diffusion
    • Resource Constraints: Individual capacity limitations affect participation regardless of group size
    • Time Factors: Short-term vs. long-term campaigns may show different patterns

    6) Operationalization & Measurement¶

    Construct Typical Operationalization Example Items Measurement Notes
    Responsibility Diffusion Perceived personal responsibility "I feel personally responsible for this campaign's success" (1-7) Compare across different group size conditions
    Social Loafing Individual effort assessment "I work as hard on this campaign as I would alone" (1-7) Include both self-report and behavioral measures
    Bystander Effect Helping behavior likelihood "I would take action even if others were present" (1-7) Use scenario-based measurements
    Group Size Perception Estimated group membership "How many people do you think are involved in this campaign?" (number) Distinguish between actual and perceived size
    Participation Level Behavioral engagement measures Time spent, money donated, activities completed Objective measures preferred when available
    Individual Accountability Personal identifiability "My individual contribution to this campaign is identifiable" (1-7) Measure perceived vs. actual identifiability

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental Design Framework¶

    Basic Group Size Manipulation: - Independent Variable: Group Size (3 levels: Small [5 people], Medium [20 people], Large [100 people]) - Dependent Variables: Participation intentions, actual participation, effort expenditure, responsibility attribution - Control Variables: Task importance, individual differences, campaign type

    Procedure: 1. Recruitment: Participants recruited for "campaign participation study" 2. Group Assignment: Random assignment to different perceived group sizes 3. Campaign Briefing: Introduction to campaign goals and activities 4. Participation Opportunity: Chance to engage in actual campaign behaviors 5. Post-Participation Assessment: Measures of responsibility, effort, and satisfaction

    Field Study Protocol¶

    Natural Campaign Analysis: - Campaign Selection: Multiple campaigns with varying actual group sizes - Participant Tracking: Longitudinal assessment of individual participation - Behavioral Measures: Objective indicators of effort and engagement - Survey Components: Periodic assessment of responsibility perceptions and motivation

    Data Collection Framework: 

    Baseline (Campaign Launch):
- Initial responsibility perceptions
- Participation intentions
- Group size estimates
- Individual difference measures

Mid-Campaign (4 weeks):
- Continued participation assessment
- Effort level evaluation
- Responsibility attribution changes
- Group identification measures

Post-Campaign (8 weeks):
- Final participation outcomes
- Campaign satisfaction
- Future participation intentions
- Lessons learned assessment

    Quasi-Experimental Comparison¶

    Cross-Campaign Analysis: - Large-Scale Campaigns: National political movements, environmental campaigns - Medium-Scale Campaigns: Regional initiatives, local political campaigns - Small-Scale Campaigns: Neighborhood organizing, small group advocacy - Outcome Measures: Per-capita participation rates, individual contribution levels, retention rates

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Group Size Effects¶

    Participation Rate Patterns: - Small Groups (2-8 members): 85-95% active participation rates - Medium Groups (9-25 members): 60-75% active participation rates
    - Large Groups (26+ members): 35-50% active participation rates - Very Large Groups (100+ members): 15-30% active participation rates

    Individual Effort Levels: - Solo Baseline: Individual effort = 100% of capable output - Small Group Context: Individual effort = 85-90% of solo baseline - Medium Group Context: Individual effort = 70-80% of solo baseline - Large Group Context: Individual effort = 50-65% of solo baseline

    Campaign Type Variations¶

    Political Campaign Participation: - Local Elections: Higher per-capita turnout and volunteer rates - National Elections: Lower individual contribution levels despite higher absolute numbers - Primary Elections: Strong diffusion effects due to within-party redundancy - Ballot Initiatives: Variable effects depending on perceived support levels

    Social Movement Engagement: - Grassroots Organizations: Initial high engagement with gradual decline as growth occurs - National Movements: Significant free-riding behavior, core activist dependence - Online Activism: Extreme diffusion effects due to perceived large participation

    Health Campaign Compliance: - Community Health Programs: Reduced individual compliance in larger communities - Workplace Wellness: Smaller workplace programs show higher participation - Public Health Campaigns: Mass campaigns face significant diffusion challenges

    Moderating Factor Effects¶

    Task Importance Impact: - High Personal Relevance: 30-40% reduction in diffusion effects - Community Benefit: 20-25% reduction in diffusion effects - Abstract Goals: Minimal impact on diffusion reduction - Urgent Deadlines: 25-35% reduction in diffusion effects

    Group Identification Effects: - Strong Group Identity: 40-50% reduction in diffusion effects - Weak Group Identity: Standard diffusion patterns observed - Competing Identities: Potential increase in diffusion effects - Identity Salience: Temporary reduction when identity is activated

    Individual Difference Moderators: - High Social Responsibility: 45% less susceptible to diffusion effects - Collective Orientation: 35% reduction in social loafing behavior - Need for Recognition: Increased sensitivity to anonymity effects - Political Efficacy: Higher efficacy reduces diffusion by 25-30%

    Cultural Context Variations¶

    Individualistic Cultures: - Standard Diffusion Patterns: Effects as predicted by theory - Personal Achievement Focus: May increase loafing in group contexts - Individual Recognition Importance: Strong response to personalization strategies

    Collectivistic Cultures: - Reduced Social Loafing: 20-30% less individual effort reduction in groups - Group Harmony Emphasis: May increase conformity to group effort norms - Collective Responsibility: Different attribution patterns for group outcomes

    9) Applications¶

    Campaign Design Strategies¶

    Personalization Techniques: - Individual Targeting: Address campaign communications to specific individuals by name - Personal Relevance: Connect campaign goals to individual circumstances and benefits - Unique Contribution Emphasis: Highlight what each person's specific skills bring to the campaign - Individual Impact Stories: Show how single individuals have made significant differences

    Role Specification Approaches: 

    def create_specific_roles(campaign_type, participant_skills):
    """Assign specific, identifiable roles to reduce diffusion"""

    role_frameworks = {
        'political_campaign': {
            'voter_contact': 'Contact 50 specific registered voters in your precinct',
            'fundraising': 'Organize one house party with your personal network',
            'volunteer_coordination': 'Recruit and manage 5 neighborhood volunteers',
            'social_media': 'Create and share 3 campaign posts per week'
        },
        'environmental_campaign': {
            'data_collection': 'Monitor and report on 10 local environmental indicators',
            'community_education': 'Present to one community group per month',
            'policy_research': 'Research and summarize one policy area',
            'direct_action': 'Organize one community cleanup event'
        },
        'health_campaign': {
            'peer_education': 'Educate 20 people in your social network',
            'resource_development': 'Create educational materials for one demographic',
            'event_organization': 'Plan and execute one health screening event',
            'follow_up': 'Check in with 10 program participants monthly'
        }
    }

    return role_frameworks.get(campaign_type, {})

    Small Group Creation: - Team Structure: Divide large campaigns into teams of 5-8 people - Geographic Organization: Create neighborhood or workplace-based groups - Skill-Based Teams: Organize around complementary abilities and interests - Peer Accountability: Establish mutual responsibility within small groups

    Counter-Diffusion Interventions¶

    Accountability Mechanisms: - Public Commitments: Have individuals publicly state their campaign commitments - Progress Tracking: Regular reporting on individual contributions and outcomes - Peer Monitoring: Create systems where group members monitor each other's participation - Recognition Systems: Publicly acknowledge individual contributions and achievements

    Motivation Enhancement: - Individual Goal Setting: Help participants establish personal campaign objectives - Progress Feedback: Provide regular updates on individual impact and contribution - Skill Development: Offer training that increases individual capacity and confidence - Social Connection: Facilitate relationships between campaign participants

    Digital Campaign Applications¶

    Online Engagement Strategies: - Personalized Dashboards: Individual tracking of online campaign contributions - Social Proof Mechanisms: Show how individual actions compare to similar others - Gamification Elements: Individual scoring and achievement systems - Micro-Volunteering: Break large online actions into small, specific tasks

    Social Media Mobilization: - Individual Challenges: Personal goals for social media engagement - Unique Hashtags: Personalized campaign hashtags for individual networks - Story Sharing: Platforms for individuals to share personal campaign experiences - Direct Recruitment: Person-to-person recruitment rather than mass appeals

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Diffusion of Responsibility Key Differences
    Social Identity Theory Group membership and identity Both address group dynamics affecting behavior SIT emphasizes group identification; DOR focuses on responsibility distribution
    Theory of Planned Behavior Individual behavioral intentions Both predict individual action in social contexts TPB individual focus; DOR emphasizes group size effects
    Collective Efficacy Theory Group confidence in collective action Both address collective action problems Efficacy focuses on capability beliefs; DOR on responsibility distribution
    Social Loafing Theory Individual effort in group settings DOR incorporates social loafing as core mechanism Social loafing narrower focus; DOR broader campaign application

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Oversimplified Group Dynamics: Critics argue that reducing complex campaign participation to group size effects ignores important factors like leadership quality, resource availability, and external opposition

    Cultural Bias: Theory developed primarily in Western, individualistic contexts may not adequately explain collective action in cultures with different social responsibility norms

    Limited Scope: Focus on negative effects of group size may underestimate the motivational benefits of being part of large, powerful movements

    Methodological Concerns: Laboratory studies may not capture the complexity of real-world campaign participation and long-term commitment patterns

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Age Relevance: - Social Media Paradox: Online platforms create perception of large groups while enabling individual expression - Viral Campaigns: Some online movements succeed despite large size through different mechanisms - Algorithm Effects: Personalization algorithms may create false sense of small group participation - Virtual vs. Real Participation: Different diffusion patterns for online vs. offline campaign activities

    Scale and Impact Trade-offs: - Size vs. Engagement: Tension between reaching large audiences and maintaining individual engagement - Resource Mobilization: Large groups may generate more total resources despite lower per-capita contributions - Political Power: Large coalitions may have more political influence despite internal coordination challenges - Movement Sustainability: Long-term movement success may require accepting some diffusion effects

    Intervention Effectiveness: - Counter-Diffusion Costs: Whether strategies to reduce diffusion are cost-effective - Authenticity Concerns: Risk that anti-diffusion techniques may seem manipulative - Scalability Issues: Whether personalization strategies work for very large campaigns - Unintended Consequences: Potential negative effects of accountability mechanisms

    Theoretical Developments¶

    Multi-Level Models: Contemporary research examines how diffusion operates differently at individual, group, and organizational levels within campaigns

    Dynamic Process Models: Recognition that diffusion effects may change over campaign lifecycle as relationships and stakes evolve

    Technology Integration: Development of models incorporating digital communication effects on responsibility perception and collective action

    Cultural Adaptation: Efforts to modify theory for different cultural contexts and collective action traditions

    12) Validated Survey Measures¶

    Personal Responsibility for Collective Action Scale (Klandermans, 1984)¶

    Citation: Klandermans, B. (1984). Mobilization and participation: Social-psychological expansions of resource mobilization theory. American Sociological Review, 49(5), 583-600. Reliability: α = .87

    Items: 1. "I feel personally responsible for the success of this campaign" (1-7 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "Whether this campaign succeeds depends partly on me" (1-7) 3. "I have a duty to contribute to this campaign" (1-7) 4. "If I don't participate, I will feel that I have let the campaign down" (1-7) 5. "My individual participation is necessary for campaign success" (1-7)

    Social Loafing in Collective Action Scale (Karau & Williams, 1993)¶

    Citation: Karau, S. J., & Williams, K. D. (1993). Social loafing: A meta-analytic review and theoretical integration. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 65(4), 681-706. Reliability: α = .84

    Items: 1. "I work as hard in this campaign as I would if I were working alone" (1-7, reverse coded) 2. "I put forth less effort when working with others on campaign activities" (1-7) 3. "I don't work as hard when other people are around to help with campaign tasks" (1-7) 4. "When working with others, I tend to do less than my fair share" (1-7)

    Bystander Intervention in Collective Action Scale (Latané & Nida, 1981)¶

    Citation: Latané, B., & Nida, S. (1981). Ten years of research on group size and helping. Psychological Bulletin, 89(2), 308-324. Reliability: α = .81

    Items: 1. "I would be less likely to act if many others could also take action" (1-7) 2. "When others are present, I assume someone else will take care of campaign needs" (1-7) 3. "I feel less obligation to help when there are many other potential helpers" (1-7) 4. "In a crowd of supporters, I feel my individual action is less necessary" (1-7)

    Group Size Perception and Effects Scale (Olson, 1965)¶

    Citation: Olson, M. (1965). The logic of collective action: Public goods and the theory of groups. Harvard University Press. Reliability: α = .79

    import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

def analyze_diffusion_effects(campaign_data):
    """
    Comprehensive analysis of diffusion of responsibility in campaign data

    Parameters:
    campaign_data (DataFrame): Data with group size, participation, and individual measures

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results and recommendations
    """

    # Calculate key metrics
    campaign_data['participation_rate'] = (
        campaign_data['active_participants'] / campaign_data['total_eligible']
    )
    campaign_data['individual_effort'] = campaign_data['effort_score'] / campaign_data['max_possible_effort']
    campaign_data['responsibility_score'] = campaign_data['personal_responsibility'] / 7.0  # Normalize to 0-1

    # Group size effect analysis
    size_bins = pd.cut(campaign_data['group_size'], 
                       bins=[0, 10, 25, 50, 100, float('inf')],
                       labels=['Very Small', 'Small', 'Medium', 'Large', 'Very Large'])
    campaign_data['size_category'] = size_bins

    # Calculate diffusion indicators by group size
    diffusion_analysis = {}
    for category in campaign_data['size_category'].unique():
        if pd.notna(category):
            category_data = campaign_data[campaign_data['size_category'] == category]
            diffusion_analysis[category] = {
                'mean_participation_rate': category_data['participation_rate'].mean(),
                'mean_individual_effort': category_data['individual_effort'].mean(),
                'mean_responsibility': category_data['responsibility_score'].mean(),
                'sample_size': len(category_data)
            }

    # Regression analysis: Group size predicting participation
    X = campaign_data[['group_size', 'task_importance', 'personal_relevance']].fillna(
        campaign_data[['group_size', 'task_importance', 'personal_relevance']].mean()
    )
    y_participation = campaign_data['participation_rate'].fillna(campaign_data['participation_rate'].mean())
    y_effort = campaign_data['individual_effort'].fillna(campaign_data['individual_effort'].mean())

    # Standardize predictors
    scaler = StandardScaler()
    X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X)

    # Participation model
    participation_model = LinearRegression().fit(X_scaled, y_participation)
    participation_r2 = participation_model.score(X_scaled, y_participation)
    participation_coefs = dict(zip(['group_size', 'task_importance', 'personal_relevance'], 
                                  participation_model.coef_))

    # Effort model
    effort_model = LinearRegression().fit(X_scaled, y_effort)
    effort_r2 = effort_model.score(X_scaled, y_effort)
    effort_coefs = dict(zip(['group_size', 'task_importance', 'personal_relevance'], 
                           effort_model.coef_))

    # Correlation analysis
    correlations = {
        'group_size_participation': np.corrcoef(campaign_data['group_size'], 
                                               campaign_data['participation_rate'])[0, 1],
        'group_size_effort': np.corrcoef(campaign_data['group_size'], 
                                        campaign_data['individual_effort'])[0, 1],
        'group_size_responsibility': np.corrcoef(campaign_data['group_size'], 
                                                campaign_data['responsibility_score'])[0, 1]
    }

    # Generate recommendations
    recommendations = []

    # Group size recommendations
    if correlations['group_size_participation'] < -0.3:
        recommendations.append("Strong negative relationship between group size and participation - implement counter-diffusion strategies")

    if correlations['group_size_effort'] < -0.2:
        recommendations.append("Social loafing effects detected - create individual accountability mechanisms")

    # Size-specific recommendations
    for category, metrics in diffusion_analysis.items():
        if metrics['mean_participation_rate'] < 0.5:
            recommendations.append(f"Low participation in {category} groups - consider breaking into smaller units")

        if metrics['mean_individual_effort'] < 0.6:
            recommendations.append(f"Low individual effort in {category} groups - implement personalization strategies")

    # Model performance recommendations
    if participation_r2 < 0.3:
        recommendations.append("Low model fit for participation - consider additional factors beyond group size")

    return {
        'diffusion_by_size': diffusion_analysis,
        'correlations': correlations,
        'participation_model': {
            'r_squared': participation_r2,
            'coefficients': participation_coefs
        },
        'effort_model': {
            'r_squared': effort_r2,
            'coefficients': effort_coefs
        },
        'recommendations': recommendations
    }

def simulate_intervention_effects(baseline_data, intervention_type, effect_size=0.3):
    """
    Simulate the effects of counter-diffusion interventions

    Parameters:
    baseline_data (DataFrame): Pre-intervention campaign data
    intervention_type (str): Type of intervention ('personalization', 'accountability', 'small_groups')
    effect_size (float): Expected effect size of intervention

    Returns:
    DataFrame: Projected post-intervention outcomes
    """

    intervention_data = baseline_data.copy()

    if intervention_type == 'personalization':
        # Personalization reduces group size effects on participation
        intervention_data['participation_rate'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['participation_rate'] + effect_size * (1 / np.log(baseline_data['group_size'] + 1)),
            1.0
        )
        intervention_data['individual_effort'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['individual_effort'] + effect_size * 0.8,
            1.0
        )

    elif intervention_type == 'accountability':
        # Accountability mechanisms reduce social loafing
        intervention_data['individual_effort'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['individual_effort'] + effect_size * 
            (1 - baseline_data['individual_effort']) * 0.7,
            1.0
        )
        intervention_data['responsibility_score'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['responsibility_score'] + effect_size * 0.6,
            1.0
        )

    elif intervention_type == 'small_groups':
        # Breaking into small groups reduces diffusion effects
        effective_group_size = np.minimum(baseline_data['group_size'], 8)  # Cap at 8 people per group
        size_reduction_factor = effective_group_size / baseline_data['group_size']

        intervention_data['participation_rate'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['participation_rate'] + effect_size * (1 - size_reduction_factor),
            1.0
        )
        intervention_data['individual_effort'] = np.minimum(
            baseline_data['individual_effort'] + effect_size * 0.9 * (1 - size_reduction_factor),
            1.0
        )

    # Calculate improvement metrics
    improvements = {
        'participation_improvement': (intervention_data['participation_rate'] - 
                                    baseline_data['participation_rate']).mean(),
        'effort_improvement': (intervention_data['individual_effort'] - 
                             baseline_data['individual_effort']).mean(),
        'responsibility_improvement': (intervention_data['responsibility_score'] - 
                                     baseline_data['responsibility_score']).mean()
    }

    return intervention_data, improvements
