    Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)

    1) Executive Summary¶

    The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) provides a comprehensive framework for predicting and understanding human behavior by focusing on the psychological factors that drive behavioral intentions and subsequent actions. Developed by Icek Ajzen (1985) as an extension of the Theory of Reasoned Action, TPB posits that behavioral intentions are the primary determinant of actual behavior, with intentions themselves predicted by three key constructs: attitude toward the behavior (positive or negative evaluation of performing the behavior), subjective norms (perceived social pressure to perform or not perform the behavior), and perceived behavioral control (perceived ease or difficulty of performing the behavior). The theory operates on the principle that human behavior is largely planned and rational, with individuals considering the likely consequences of their actions, social expectations, and their ability to perform behaviors before forming intentions. Perceived behavioral control serves a dual function, directly influencing both intentions and actual behavior, particularly when it accurately reflects actual control over the behavior. TPB has demonstrated strong predictive validity across diverse domains including health behavior change, consumer decision-making, environmental actions, technology adoption, and organizational behavior. Meta-analyses show that TPB typically explains 40-50% of variance in behavioral intentions and 25-35% of variance in actual behavior. The framework is particularly valuable for designing behavior change interventions by identifying which psychological factors most strongly influence specific behaviors, allowing practitioners to target attitudes, social norms, or control perceptions as appropriate for maximum intervention effectiveness.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A social psychological theory that predicts human behavior through behavioral intentions, which are determined by attitudes toward the behavior, subjective norms about social expectations, and perceived behavioral control over performing the action.
    • Scope: All volitional behaviors where individuals have some degree of conscious control, including health behaviors, consumer choices, environmental actions, technology use, workplace behaviors, and any domain where behavioral prediction and change are relevant.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Behavioral Intention¶

    Definition and Role¶

    Behavioral Intention: The subjective probability that a person will perform a particular behavior, serving as the immediate antecedent and strongest predictor of actual behavior

    Characteristics of Intentions: - Temporal Proximity: Stronger predictor when measured closer in time to behavior performance - Specificity Match: More predictive when intention and behavior match in action, target, context, and time - Stability: More stable intentions lead to stronger intention-behavior relationships - Accessibility: Easily recalled intentions better predict behavior than those requiring prompting

    Intention-Behavior Gap: - Implementation Problems: Difficulty translating good intentions into actual behavior - Competing Priorities: Other behaviors or goals interfering with intended action - Contextual Barriers: Environmental factors preventing behavior despite strong intentions - Temporal Decay: Weakening of intentions over time before behavior opportunity arises

    Moderating Factors¶

    Individual Differences: - Self-Efficacy: Higher self-efficacy strengthens intention-behavior relationship - Planning Tendency: Individuals who naturally plan show stronger intention-behavior correspondence - Self-Control: Better self-regulation improves intention implementation - Past Behavior: Previous experience affects both intention formation and behavior prediction

    Situational Factors: - Behavioral Control: Greater actual control strengthens intention-behavior link - Social Support: Supportive environments facilitate intention implementation - Resource Availability: Adequate resources improve behavior performance likelihood - Competing Demands: Fewer alternative behaviors strengthen focal behavior prediction

    Attitude Toward the Behavior¶

    Definition and Components¶

    Attitude Toward Behavior: Overall positive or negative evaluation of performing the specific behavior, based on beliefs about behavior outcomes and evaluation of those outcomes

    Expectancy-Value Model: 

    Attitude = Σ (Behavioral Belief × Outcome Evaluation)
    Where behavioral beliefs represent the perceived likelihood that behavior leads to specific outcomes, and outcome evaluations reflect the desirability of those outcomes.

    Attitude Formation Process: - Outcome Identification: Recognition of possible consequences of behavior performance - Probability Assessment: Evaluation of likelihood that behavior will produce each outcome - Valence Evaluation: Determination of whether each outcome is positive or negative - Integration: Combination of outcome probabilities and evaluations into overall attitude

    Types of Behavioral Beliefs¶

    Instrumental Beliefs: - Functional Outcomes: Practical benefits or costs of behavior performance - Performance Consequences: Effects on goal achievement or task completion - Economic Implications: Financial costs or benefits associated with behavior - Efficiency Considerations: Time, effort, and resource requirements

    Experiential Beliefs: - Affective Outcomes: Emotional consequences of behavior performance - Hedonic Aspects: Pleasure or displeasure derived from behavior - Sensory Experience: Physical sensations associated with behavior - Aesthetic Considerations: Beauty, elegance, or appeal of behavior or outcomes

    Symbolic Beliefs: - Identity Expression: How behavior reflects or shapes personal identity - Value Demonstration: Behavior as expression of personal values or principles - Social Signaling: Messages behavior sends to others about the person - Status Implications: Effects on social standing or reputation

    Subjective Norms¶

    Definition and Structure¶

    Subjective Norms: Perceived social pressure to perform or not perform the behavior, reflecting beliefs about what important others think one should do and motivation to comply with those expectations

    Normative Pressure Components: 

    Subjective Norm = Σ (Normative Belief × Motivation to Comply)
    Where normative beliefs represent perceptions of important others' expectations, and motivation to comply reflects the desire to meet those expectations.

    Types of Social Influence¶

    Injunctive Norms: - Prescriptive Expectations: What important others think one should do - Moral Obligations: Ethical or moral expectations from social groups - Role Requirements: Behavioral expectations based on social roles or positions - Authority Directives: Expectations from legitimate authorities or institutions

    Descriptive Norms: - Behavioral Modeling: What important others actually do in similar situations - Social Proof: Evidence of common or typical behavior in relevant groups - Peer Behavior: Actions of similar others as behavioral guides - Cultural Patterns: Broader societal behavioral norms and practices

    Personal Norms: - Moral Standards: Individual ethical principles and moral obligations - Self-Expectations: Personal standards and self-imposed behavioral requirements - Identity Consistency: Behavior aligned with self-concept and personal values - Internalized Values: Deeply held beliefs about appropriate behavior

    Important Referent Groups¶

    Primary Groups: - Family Members: Parents, spouses, children, and close relatives - Close Friends: Intimate personal relationships and close companions - Romantic Partners: Significant others and intimate relationship partners - Household Members: People sharing living space and daily interactions

    Secondary Groups: - Professional Colleagues: Workplace peers, supervisors, and professional networks - Social Groups: Community organizations, clubs, and social associations - Educational Peers: Classmates, study groups, and academic communities - Religious Communities: Faith-based groups and spiritual communities

    Perceived Behavioral Control¶

    Definition and Dual Role¶

    Perceived Behavioral Control: Perceived ease or difficulty of performing the behavior, reflecting beliefs about control factors and their perceived power to facilitate or impede behavior

    Dual Function: 1. Indirect Effect through Intentions: PBC influences behavioral intentions alongside attitudes and norms 2. Direct Effect on Behavior: PBC directly predicts behavior when it accurately reflects actual control

    Control Belief Structure¶

    Perceived Behavioral Control = Σ (Control Belief × Perceived Power)
    Where control beliefs represent the presence or absence of facilitating or inhibiting factors, and perceived power reflects the impact of each factor on behavior performance.

    Internal Control Factors: - Knowledge and Skills: Competencies required for behavior performance - Self-Efficacy: Confidence in ability to perform behavior successfully - Emotional Regulation: Ability to manage emotions that might interfere with behavior - Physical Capabilities: Physical strength, health, or abilities needed for behavior

    External Control Factors: - Resource Availability: Money, time, equipment, or materials needed for behavior - Environmental Conditions: Physical or social environmental factors affecting behavior - Institutional Support: Organizational policies, systems, or structures facilitating behavior - Social Cooperation: Need for others' cooperation or assistance in behavior performance

    Self-Efficacy vs. Controllability¶

    Self-Efficacy Dimension: - Confidence Focus: Belief in personal ability to perform behavior - Internal Attribution: Emphasis on personal capabilities and skills - Performance Expectation: Likelihood of successful behavior execution - Competence Perception: Assessment of personal adequacy for behavior requirements

    Controllability Dimension: - External Focus: Emphasis on external factors affecting behavior performance - Resource Dependence: Recognition of dependence on external resources or conditions - Situation Assessment: Evaluation of environmental factors enabling or constraining behavior - Opportunity Perception: Availability of suitable circumstances for behavior performance

    Background Factors¶

    Individual Differences¶

    Demographic Variables: - Age and Life Stage: Developmental factors affecting behavior patterns and priorities - Gender and Identity: Social roles and expectations associated with gender identity - Education Level: Knowledge, skills, and cognitive resources affecting behavior - Socioeconomic Status: Economic resources and social position influences

    Personality Traits: - Conscientiousness: Tendency toward self-discipline and goal pursuit - Openness to Experience: Willingness to try new behaviors and approaches - Risk Tolerance: Comfort with uncertainty and potential negative outcomes - Need for Control: Desire to influence and control environmental factors

    Environmental Factors¶

    Social Environment: - Cultural Context: Broader cultural norms and values affecting behavior - Community Characteristics: Local social norms and behavioral patterns - Network Composition: Types and characteristics of social relationships - Social Capital: Resources available through social connections

    Physical Environment: - Geographic Location: Urban vs. rural, climate, and regional characteristics - Built Environment: Infrastructure, facilities, and physical resources available - Accessibility: Physical accessibility and convenience of behavior performance - Safety Considerations: Security and safety factors affecting behavior decisions

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Behavioral intentions are the immediate antecedent and best predictor of volitional behavior

    P2: Behavioral intentions are determined by attitudes toward the behavior, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control

    P3: The relative importance of the three predictors varies across behaviors, populations, and situations

    P4: Perceived behavioral control has both indirect effects (through intentions) and direct effects on behavior

    P5: Attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control are based on underlying belief structures

    P6: Behavior change interventions should target the most influential predictor(s) for the specific behavior and population

    P7: The intention-behavior relationship is stronger when behavioral control is high and accurately perceived

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Rational Decision Making: Individuals systematically consider available information when forming intentions
    • Volitional Control: Theory applies to behaviors under conscious, voluntary control
    • Conscious Processing: People are aware of their attitudes, norms, and control perceptions
    • Temporal Stability: Underlying beliefs and constructs remain relatively stable over relevant time periods
    • Individual Agency: People have capacity to form intentions and translate them into behavior

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Habitual Behaviors: Theory less applicable to automatic, habitual responses
    • Emergency Situations: Limited applicability when immediate response required without deliberation
    • Severe Resource Constraints: Theory assumes minimum level of behavioral control and choice
    • Unconscious Behaviors: Limited relevance for behaviors driven primarily by unconscious processes
    • Impulsive Actions: Reduced applicability for spontaneous, emotion-driven behaviors
    • Addictive Behaviors: Complicated application when behavior involves addiction or compulsion
    • Cultural Specificity: Constructs may have different meanings or importance across cultures

    6) Operationalization & Measurement¶

    Construct Typical Operationalization Example Items Measurement Notes
    Behavioral Intention Likelihood of behavior performance "I intend to exercise regularly next month" (1-7); "I plan to exercise regularly" (likely-unlikely) Include multiple intention indicators
    Attitude Toward Behavior Semantic differential scales "Exercising regularly is: harmful-beneficial, unpleasant-pleasant, bad-good" (1-7) Use bipolar adjective pairs
    Subjective Norms Perceived expectations of others "People important to me think I should exercise regularly" (1-7) Include both injunctive and descriptive norms
    Perceived Behavioral Control Perceived ease/difficulty "For me to exercise regularly would be: impossible-possible, difficult-easy" (1-7) Distinguish self-efficacy from controllability
    Behavioral Beliefs Outcome likelihood × evaluation "Exercise improves health" (likely-unlikely) × "Health improvement is" (bad-good) Multiply belief strength by evaluation
    Control Beliefs Factor presence × power "Having time constraints" (present-absent) × "Time constraints make exercise" (much harder-much easier) Assess barriers and facilitators

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Cross-Sectional Survey Design¶

    Basic TPB Study Structure: - Participants: Representative sample of target population for behavior of interest - Measures: Full TPB questionnaire measuring all constructs with multiple items - Analysis: Structural equation modeling or multiple regression to test model relationships - Outcome: Variance explained in intentions and behavior by TPB constructs

    Procedure: 1. Instrument Development: Create behavior-specific TPB measures following Ajzen's guidelines 2. Pilot Testing: Validate measures and identify salient beliefs through qualitative research 3. Data Collection: Administer questionnaire to adequate sample size for statistical analysis 4. Statistical Analysis: Test measurement model, then structural relationships between constructs 5. Interpretation: Identify strongest predictors and theoretical model fit for behavior

    Longitudinal Predictive Design¶

    Two-Wave Longitudinal Study: - Time 1: Measure TPB constructs (attitudes, norms, PBC) and behavioral intentions - Time 2: Measure actual behavior performance after appropriate time interval - Analysis: Examine intention-behavior relationship and direct PBC effects on behavior - Outcome: Predictive validity of intentions and direct control effects

    Time Interval Considerations: 

    Behavior Type → Optimal Time Interval
- One-time behaviors: 2-4 weeks
- Repeated behaviors: 1-3 months  
- Long-term behaviors: 3-6 months
- Habit formation: 6-12 months

    Intervention Evaluation Design¶

    Randomized Controlled Trial: - Pre-test: Baseline TPB measures and behavior assessment - Intervention: Theory-based behavior change program targeting specific TPB constructs - Post-test: Follow-up TPB measures and behavior assessment - Control Group: No intervention or alternative intervention comparison

    Mediation Analysis: - Path Analysis: Test whether intervention effects on behavior are mediated through TPB constructs - Construct Targeting: Evaluate whether intervention successfully changed targeted constructs - Mechanism Identification: Determine which TPB pathways account for behavior change

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Meta-Analytic Evidence¶

    Overall Predictive Performance: - Intention Prediction: TPB explains 40-50% of variance in behavioral intentions across studies - Behavior Prediction: Intentions alone predict 25-30% of variance in behavior - Full Model: Complete TPB model explains 25-35% of behavioral variance - Construct Contributions: Attitudes (β = .43), PBC (β = .43), subjective norms (β = .34) average effects

    Domain-Specific Performance: - Health Behaviors: Strong performance (R² = .35-.45 for intentions) - Consumer Behavior: Moderate to strong performance (R² = .30-.40 for intentions) - Environmental Behavior: Good performance (R² = .35-.50 for intentions) - Technology Adoption: Strong performance (R² = .40-.55 for intentions) - Organizational Behavior: Moderate performance (R² = .25-.35 for intentions)

    Construct-Specific Patterns¶

    Attitude Effects: - Consistency: Most stable predictor across behaviors and populations - Strength: Typically strongest or second-strongest predictor of intentions - Mediating Role: Often mediates effects of background factors on intentions - Cultural Variation: Less cultural variation than normative influences

    Subjective Norm Effects: - Variability: Most variable predictor, ranging from weak to strong across behaviors - Cultural Sensitivity: Stronger effects in collectivistic cultures - Behavior Type: Stronger for behaviors with clear social implications - Age Effects: Generally stronger for younger populations

    Perceived Behavioral Control Effects: - Dual Role: Consistent evidence for both indirect (intention) and direct (behavior) effects - Control Accuracy: Direct effects stronger when PBC accurately reflects actual control - Behavior Complexity: More important for complex, resource-dependent behaviors - Self-Efficacy Component: Self-efficacy typically stronger predictor than controllability

    Moderating Factors¶

    Individual Differences: - Self-Monitoring: High self-monitors show stronger attitude-intention relationships - Need for Cognition: Higher need for cognition strengthens attitude effects - Collectivistic Orientation: Increases importance of subjective norms - Experience Level: More experience strengthens PBC-behavior relationships

    Behavioral Characteristics: - Behavior Complexity: More complex behaviors increase importance of PBC - Social Visibility: Publicly performed behaviors show stronger normative effects - Resource Requirements: Resource-intensive behaviors increase PBC importance - Habit Strength: Habitual behaviors show weaker TPB effects

    Temporal Factors: - Intention-Behavior Interval: Shorter intervals show stronger intention-behavior correlations - Intention Stability: More stable intentions better predict behavior - Implementation Planning: Planning strengthens intention-behavior relationship - Opportunity Frequency: More opportunities strengthen TPB predictions

    Cultural and Demographic Variations¶

    Cultural Differences: - Individualism-Collectivism: Collectivistic cultures show stronger normative effects - Power Distance: High power distance cultures show stronger normative effects - Uncertainty Avoidance: High uncertainty avoidance increases importance of control - Long-term Orientation: Affects temporal aspects of intention formation and stability

    Demographic Moderators: - Age Effects: Older adults show stronger attitude-intention relationships - Gender Differences: Women sometimes show stronger normative influences - Education Level: Higher education associated with stronger attitude effects - Socioeconomic Status: Higher SES strengthens perceived control effects

    9) Applications¶

    Health Behavior Change¶

    Smoking Cessation Programs: - Attitude Targeting: Education about health benefits and risks to shape outcome beliefs - Normative Interventions: Social support programs and peer modeling - Control Enhancement: Skill training, resources provision, and environmental modifications - Implementation Support: Planning assistance and relapse prevention strategies

    Physical Activity Promotion: - Benefit Awareness: Information about physical, mental, and social benefits of exercise - Social Environment: Group exercise programs and social support networks - Barrier Reduction: Facility access, time management, and skill development - Self-Efficacy Building: Graduated exposure and mastery experience programs

    Consumer Behavior and Marketing¶

    Product Adoption: - Attitude Formation: Highlighting product benefits and addressing negative perceptions - Social Proof: Testimonials, reviews, and social media influence - Ease of Use: Simplifying purchase process and reducing barriers - Brand Positioning: Creating favorable attitudes through brand associations

    Sustainable Consumption: - Environmental Awareness: Education about environmental benefits of sustainable choices - Social Norms: Communicating descriptive norms about sustainable behavior - Convenience Factors: Making sustainable options more accessible and convenient - Identity Connection: Linking sustainable behavior to personal values and identity

    Technology Adoption¶

    Digital Technology Implementation: - Usefulness Perceptions: Demonstrating functional benefits and productivity gains - Social Influence: Peer adoption and organizational support - Ease of Use: User-friendly interfaces and training programs - Support Systems: Technical assistance and troubleshooting resources

    Innovation Diffusion: - Early Adopter Targeting: Focusing on individuals with favorable innovation attitudes - Network Effects: Leveraging social connections for adoption spread - Compatibility Emphasis: Highlighting fit with existing systems and practices - Risk Reduction: Providing trial opportunities and guarantees

    Environmental Behavior¶

    Pro-Environmental Action: - Environmental Education: Information about environmental consequences and benefits - Community Norms: Establishing and communicating pro-environmental community standards - Structural Changes: Policy interventions and infrastructure improvements - Collective Efficacy: Emphasizing collective impact of individual actions

    Recycling Programs: - Outcome Awareness: Education about environmental and economic benefits - Social Expectations: Clear communication of community recycling norms - Convenience Factors: Accessible recycling facilities and simple procedures - Feedback Systems: Information about program effectiveness and personal impact

    Organizational Behavior¶

    Employee Behavior Change: - Job Attitudes: Improving job satisfaction and organizational commitment - Workplace Norms: Establishing clear performance expectations and cultural values - Resource Provision: Ensuring adequate tools, training, and support - Goal Setting: Clear objectives and performance feedback systems

    Safety Behavior: - Risk Awareness: Education about safety risks and prevention benefits - Safety Culture: Establishing strong organizational norms supporting safe behavior - Training Programs: Skill development and self-efficacy building - Environmental Controls: Engineering solutions and safety equipment provision

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to TPB Key Differences
    Theory of Reasoned Action Attitudes and norms predicting behavior TPB extends TRA by adding perceived behavioral control TRA assumes full volitional control; TPB recognizes control limitations
    Social Cognitive Theory Reciprocal determinism and self-efficacy Self-efficacy overlaps with PBC; both predict behavior SCT broader scope; TPB focused on intention formation process
    Health Belief Model Health-specific behavior prediction Both predict health behaviors through belief structures HBM health-specific; TPB general behavior theory
    Transtheoretical Model Stages of behavior change Both address behavior change but different mechanisms TTM stage-based; TPB focuses on intention formation

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Limited Behavioral Variance Explained: Critics note that TPB typically explains only 25-35% of behavioral variance, leaving substantial unexplained variation in behavior prediction

    Assumption of Rationality: Theory assumes systematic, rational decision-making, potentially overlooking emotional, spontaneous, or unconscious influences on behavior

    Cultural Bias: Framework developed in Western, individualistic contexts may not adequately capture behavior patterns in collectivistic or non-Western cultures

    Temporal Stability: Theory assumes relatively stable constructs but may not account for dynamic changes in attitudes, norms, and control perceptions

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Intention-Behavior Gap: - Implementation Intentions: Role of specific action plans in bridging intention-behavior gap - Habit Formation: How repeated behavior performance becomes automatic and bypasses intentional control - Competing Goals: Impact of multiple, conflicting behavioral goals on intention implementation - Contextual Factors: Environmental influences that facilitate or inhibit intention enactment

    Additional Constructs: - Past Behavior: Debate over whether past behavior should be included as predictor - Moral Norms: Role of personal moral obligations in behavior prediction - Anticipated Regret: Influence of anticipated emotional consequences on behavior - Self-Identity: Impact of behavior-relevant identity on intention formation

    Measurement Issues: - Correspondence Principle: Importance of matching specificity between predictors and behavior - Social Desirability: Potential bias in self-reported attitudes and intentions - Belief Accessibility: Whether all relevant beliefs are accessible and measurable - Cross-Cultural Validity: Applicability of measures across different cultural contexts

    Theoretical Extensions¶

    Integrated Models: Development of comprehensive frameworks combining TPB with other theories like Social Cognitive Theory or Transtheoretical Model

    Dynamic TPB: Recognition that TPB constructs change over time and across situations, requiring more flexible theoretical approaches

    Unconscious Influences: Integration of implicit attitudes, automatic processes, and unconscious influences on behavior

    Collective Behavior: Extension to group-level phenomena and collective action contexts

    12) Validated Survey Measures¶

    Theory of Planned Behavior Questionnaire (Ajzen, 2006)¶

    Citation: Ajzen, I. (2006). Constructing a theory of planned behavior questionnaire. Available at: http://people.umass.edu/aizen/pdf/tpb.measurement.pdf Reliability: Variable by construct (α = .70-.95)

    Behavioral Intention (α = .85): 1. "I intend to [behavior] in the next [time period]" (1-7 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "I will try to [behavior] in the next [time period]" (1-7) 3. "I plan to [behavior] in the next [time period]" (1-7)

    Attitude Toward Behavior (α = .89): 1. "My [behavior] in the next [time period] would be:" (1-7 semantic differential) - Harmful - Beneficial - Pleasant - Unpleasant (reverse) - Good - Bad (reverse) - Worthless - Valuable

    Subjective Norms (α = .82): 1. "Most people who are important to me think that I should [behavior]" (1-7) 2. "It is expected of me that I [behavior]" (1-7) 3. "The people in my life whose opinions I value would approve of my [behavior]" (1-7)

    Perceived Behavioral Control Scale (Ajzen, 2006)¶

    Citation: Same as above Reliability: α = .81

    Self-Efficacy Component: 1. "I am confident that I am able to [behavior]" (1-7) 2. "For me to [behavior] is:" (1-7 easy-difficult, reverse coded) 3. "If I wanted to I could [behavior]" (1-7)

    Controllability Component: 1. "The number of events outside my control which could prevent me from [behavior] are:" (1-7 numerous-few) 2. "Whether or not I [behavior] is completely up to me" (1-7)

    Behavior-Specific Belief Measures¶

    Behavioral Beliefs (α = .79): 1. Belief strength: "[Behavior] will lead to [outcome]" (1-7 unlikely-likely) 2. Outcome evaluation: "[Outcome] is:" (1-7 bad-good) 3. Composite score: Σ (belief strength × outcome evaluation)

    Normative Beliefs (α = .76): 1. Injunctive: "[Referent group] thinks I should [behavior]" (1-7 unlikely-likely) 2. Descriptive: "[Referent group] [behaviors themselves]" (1-7 unlikely-likely) 3. Motivation to comply: "Generally speaking, how much do you want to do what [referent group] thinks you should do?" (1-7)

    Control Beliefs (α = .78): 1. Belief strength: "[Control factor] will be present when I try to [behavior]" (1-7 unlikely-likely) 2. Perceived power: "[Control factor] would make [behavior]:" (1-7 much more difficult-much easier)

def analyze_tpb_data(study_data):
    """
    Comprehensive analysis of Theory of Planned Behavior data

    Parameters:
    study_data (DataFrame): TPB data with all construct measures

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results including model fit and construct effects
    """

    # Calculate composite scores for TPB constructs
    attitude_items = ['att1', 'att2', 'att3', 'att4']
    norm_items = ['norm1', 'norm2', 'norm3']
    pbc_items = ['pbc1', 'pbc2', 'pbc3', 'pbc4']
    intention_items = ['int1', 'int2', 'int3']

    # Create composite scores (handling missing values)
    available_att = [col for col in attitude_items if col in study_data.columns]
    available_norm = [col for col in norm_items if col in study_data.columns]
    available_pbc = [col for col in pbc_items if col in study_data.columns]
    available_int = [col for col in intention_items if col in study_data.columns]

    if available_att:
        study_data['attitude'] = study_data[available_att].mean(axis=1)
    if available_norm:
        study_data['subjective_norms'] = study_data[available_norm].mean(axis=1)
    if available_pbc:
        study_data['perceived_behavioral_control'] = study_data[available_pbc].mean(axis=1)
    if available_int:
        study_data['behavioral_intention'] = study_data[available_int].mean(axis=1)

    analysis_results = {
        'sample_size': len(study_data),
        'descriptive_stats': {
            'attitude_mean': study_data.get('attitude', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean(),
            'norms_mean': study_data.get('subjective_norms', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean(),
            'pbc_mean': study_data.get('perceived_behavioral_control', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean(),
            'intention_mean': study_data.get('behavioral_intention', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean()
        }
    }

    # Reliability analysis (Cronbach's alpha approximation)
    def calculate_alpha(items_data):
        """Calculate Cronbach's alpha for scale reliability"""
        if len(items_data.columns) < 2:
            return np.nan

        # Calculate inter-item correlations
        corr_matrix = items_data.corr()
        n_items = len(items_data.columns)

        # Average inter-item correlation
        sum_corr = 0
        count_corr = 0
        for i in range(n_items):
            for j in range(i+1, n_items):
                if not np.isnan(corr_matrix.iloc[i, j]):
                    sum_corr += corr_matrix.iloc[i, j]
                    count_corr += 1

        if count_corr == 0:
            return np.nan

        avg_corr = sum_corr / count_corr

        # Spearman-Brown formula approximation
        alpha = (n_items * avg_corr) / (1 + (n_items - 1) * avg_corr)
        return alpha

    if available_att:
        analysis_results['reliability'] = {
            'attitude_alpha': calculate_alpha(study_data[available_att])
        }
        if available_norm:
            analysis_results['reliability']['norms_alpha'] = calculate_alpha(study_data[available_norm])
        if available_pbc:
            analysis_results['reliability']['pbc_alpha'] = calculate_alpha(study_data[available_pbc])
        if available_int:
            analysis_results['reliability']['intention_alpha'] = calculate_alpha(study_data[available_int])

    # TPB model analysis - Predicting intentions
    tpb_predictors = ['attitude', 'subjective_norms', 'perceived_behavioral_control']
    available_predictors = [var for var in tpb_predictors if var in study_data.columns]

    if len(available_predictors) >= 2 and 'behavioral_intention' in study_data.columns:
        # Prepare data for regression
        X = study_data[available_predictors].fillna(study_data[available_predictors].mean())
        y = study_data['behavioral_intention'].fillna(study_data['behavioral_intention'].mean())

        # Standardize predictors for comparison
        scaler = StandardScaler()
        X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X)
        X_scaled_df = pd.DataFrame(X_scaled, columns=available_predictors)

        # Multiple regression predicting intentions
        intention_model = LinearRegression().fit(X_scaled_df, y)
        intention_r2 = intention_model.score(X_scaled_df, y)
        intention_coefs = dict(zip(available_predictors, intention_model.coef_))

        analysis_results['intention_model'] = {
            'r_squared': intention_r2,
            'standardized_coefficients': intention_coefs,
            'intercept': intention_model.intercept_,
            'predictors_used': available_predictors
        }

        # Predicting behavior (if available)
        if 'behavior' in study_data.columns:
            # Model 1: Intentions predicting behavior
            behavior_model1 = LinearRegression().fit(
                study_data[['behavioral_intention']].fillna(study_data[['behavioral_intention']].mean()),
                study_data['behavior'].fillna(study_data['behavior'].mean())
            )

            # Model 2: Intentions + PBC predicting behavior
            if 'perceived_behavioral_control' in available_predictors:
                behavior_predictors = ['behavioral_intention', 'perceived_behavioral_control']
                X_behavior = study_data[behavior_predictors].fillna(study_data[behavior_predictors].mean())
                y_behavior = study_data['behavior'].fillna(study_data['behavior'].mean())

                behavior_model2 = LinearRegression().fit(X_behavior, y_behavior)

                analysis_results['behavior_model'] = {
                    'intention_only_r2': behavior_model1.score(
                        study_data[['behavioral_intention']].fillna(study_data[['behavioral_intention']].mean()),
                        study_data['behavior'].fillna(study_data['behavior'].mean())
                    ),
                    'intention_pbc_r2': behavior_model2.score(X_behavior, y_behavior),
                    'intention_coefficient': behavior_model2.coef_[0],
                    'pbc_direct_coefficient': behavior_model2.coef_[1] if len(behavior_model2.coef_) > 1 else None
                }

    # Correlation analysis between all TPB constructs
    tpb_constructs = ['attitude', 'subjective_norms', 'perceived_behavioral_control', 'behavioral_intention']
    if 'behavior' in study_data.columns:
        tpb_constructs.append('behavior')

    available_constructs = [var for var in tpb_constructs if var in study_data.columns]

    if len(available_constructs) >= 2:
        corr_matrix = study_data[available_constructs].corr()
        analysis_results['correlations'] = corr_matrix.to_dict()

    # Belief-based analysis (if belief measures available)
    belief_measures = [col for col in study_data.columns if 'belief' in col.lower()]
    if belief_measures:
        # Calculate belief-based attitude (example for behavioral beliefs)
        behavioral_beliefs = [col for col in belief_measures if 'behavioral' in col.lower()]
        if len(behavioral_beliefs) >= 2:  # Assume belief*evaluation pairs
            belief_attitude = 0
            for i in range(0, len(behavioral_beliefs), 2):
                if i+1 < len(behavioral_beliefs):
                    belief_strength = study_data[behavioral_beliefs[i]].fillna(4)  # Neutral default
                    evaluation = study_data[behavioral_beliefs[i+1]].fillna(4)
                    belief_attitude += belief_strength * evaluation

            if 'attitude' in study_data.columns:
                belief_attitude_corr = np.corrcoef(
                    belief_attitude.dropna(),
                    study_data['attitude'].dropna()
                )[0, 1] if len(belief_attitude.dropna()) > 0 else np.nan

                analysis_results['belief_analysis'] = {
                    'belief_attitude_correlation': belief_attitude_corr,
                    'belief_measures_found': len(belief_measures)
                }

    # Generate practical recommendations
    recommendations = []

    # Model performance recommendations
    if 'intention_model' in analysis_results:
        r2 = analysis_results['intention_model']['r_squared']
        if r2 > 0.5:
            recommendations.append("Strong TPB model fit - all constructs provide good prediction")
        elif r2 > 0.3:
            recommendations.append("Moderate TPB model fit - consider additional predictors")
        else:
            recommendations.append("Weak TPB model fit - may need behavior-specific modifications")

        # Strongest predictor identification
        coefs = analysis_results['intention_model']['standardized_coefficients']
        strongest_predictor = max(coefs.keys(), key=lambda x: abs(coefs[x]))
        recommendations.append(f"Focus interventions on {strongest_predictor} - strongest intention predictor")

    # Intention-behavior gap analysis
    if 'behavior_model' in analysis_results:
        int_only_r2 = analysis_results['behavior_model']['intention_only_r2']
        if int_only_r2 < 0.25:
            recommendations.append("Large intention-behavior gap - focus on implementation support")

        pbc_coef = analysis_results['behavior_model'].get('pbc_direct_coefficient')
        if pbc_coef and abs(pbc_coef) > 0.2:
            recommendations.append("Strong PBC direct effect - address control barriers and resources")

    # Reliability recommendations
    if 'reliability' in analysis_results:
        for construct, alpha in analysis_results['reliability'].items():
            if alpha < 0.7:
                recommendations.append(f"Low reliability for {construct} - consider scale improvement")

    analysis_results['recommendations'] = recommendations
    return analysis_results

def simulate_tpb_intervention(baseline_data, intervention_targets, effect_sizes):
    """
    Simulate TPB-based intervention effects on behavior change

    Parameters:
    baseline_data (DataFrame): Pre-intervention TPB measures
    intervention_targets (list): TPB constructs targeted by intervention
    effect_sizes (dict): Expected effect sizes for each targeted construct

    Returns:
    dict: Predicted intervention outcomes and behavior change estimates
    """

    np.random.seed(42)
    intervention_data = baseline_data.copy()

    # Apply intervention effects to targeted constructs
    for target in intervention_targets:
        if target in intervention_data.columns:
            effect_size = effect_sizes.get(target, 0.5)  # Default moderate effect

            # Add intervention effect with some random variation
            intervention_effect = np.random.normal(effect_size, 0.1, len(intervention_data))
            intervention_data[target] += intervention_effect

            # Ensure values stay within reasonable bounds (1-7 scale)
            intervention_data[target] = np.clip(intervention_data[target], 1, 7)

    # Recalculate intentions based on TPB model with typical weights
    tpb_weights = {
        'attitude': 0.43,
        'subjective_norms': 0.34,
        'perceived_behavioral_control': 0.43
    }

    if all(construct in intervention_data.columns for construct in tpb_weights.keys()):
        baseline_intention = intervention_data['behavioral_intention'].mean()

        # Calculate new intentions based on changed constructs
        intention_change = 0
        for construct, weight in tpb_weights.items():
            if construct in intervention_targets:
                construct_change = (intervention_data[construct] - 
                                  baseline_data[construct]).mean()
                intention_change += weight * construct_change * 0.3  # Scaling factor

        intervention_data['behavioral_intention'] += np.random.normal(
            intention_change, 0.2, len(intervention_data)
        )
        intervention_data['behavioral_intention'] = np.clip(
            intervention_data['behavioral_intention'], 1, 7
        )

    # Calculate behavior change based on intention change and PBC
    if 'behavior' in intervention_data.columns:
        intention_behavior_effect = 0.6  # Typical intention-behavior correlation
        pbc_behavior_effect = 0.3  # Direct PBC effect

        intention_change = (intervention_data['behavioral_intention'] - 
                          baseline_data['behavioral_intention']).mean()

        behavior_change = intention_change * intention_behavior_effect

        if 'perceived_behavioral_control' in intervention_targets:
            pbc_change = (intervention_data['perceived_behavioral_control'] - 
                         baseline_data['perceived_behavioral_control']).mean()
            behavior_change += pbc_change * pbc_behavior_effect

        intervention_data['behavior'] += np.random.normal(
            behavior_change, 0.15, len(intervention_data)
        )
        intervention_data['behavior'] = np.clip(intervention_data['behavior'], 1, 7)

    # Calculate intervention effectiveness metrics
    effectiveness_results = {
        'sample_size': len(intervention_data),
        'intervention_targets': intervention_targets,
        'construct_changes': {},
        'behavior_outcomes': {}
    }

    # Construct-level changes
    for construct in ['attitude', 'subjective_norms', 'perceived_behavioral_control', 'behavioral_intention']:
        if construct in intervention_data.columns:
            baseline_mean = baseline_data[construct].mean()
            post_mean = intervention_data[construct].mean()
            change = post_mean - baseline_mean
            effect_size = change / baseline_data[construct].std()

            effectiveness_results['construct_changes'][construct] = {
                'baseline_mean': baseline_mean,
                'post_mean': post_mean,
                'absolute_change': change,
                'effect_size': effect_size,
                'targeted': construct in intervention_targets
            }

    # Behavior change outcomes
    if 'behavior' in intervention_data.columns:
        baseline_behavior = baseline_data['behavior'].mean()
        post_behavior = intervention_data['behavior'].mean()
        behavior_change = post_behavior - baseline_behavior
        behavior_effect_size = behavior_change / baseline_data['behavior'].std()

        effectiveness_results['behavior_outcomes'] = {
            'baseline_behavior': baseline_behavior,
            'post_behavior': post_behavior,
            'behavior_change': behavior_change,
            'behavior_effect_size': behavior_effect_size,
            'improvement_rate': (intervention_data['behavior'] > baseline_data['behavior']).mean()
        }

    return effectiveness_results, intervention_data
