    Diffusion of Health Innovations

    1) Executive Summary¶

    The Diffusion of Health Innovations theory, adapted from Everett Rogers' general diffusion of innovations framework, explains how medical technologies, clinical practices, and health interventions spread through healthcare systems and populations. The theory identifies five key innovation characteristics that influence adoption: Relative Advantage (perceived benefits over existing practices), Compatibility (consistency with adopter values and needs), Complexity (difficulty of understanding and use), Trialability (ability to experiment on limited basis), and Observability (visibility of innovation results). Healthcare adopters are categorized into five groups based on adoption timing: Innovators (2.5% - venturesome risk-takers), Early Adopters (13.5% - respected opinion leaders), Early Majority (34% - deliberate pragmatists), Late Majority (34% - skeptical followers), and Laggards (16% - traditional resisters). The innovation-decision process involves five stages: Knowledge (awareness of innovation), Persuasion (attitude formation), Decision (adoption or rejection), Implementation (putting innovation to use), and Confirmation (seeking reinforcement or reversal). Communication channels include mass media for awareness building and interpersonal networks for persuasion, with opinion leaders playing crucial roles in healthcare settings. Social system factors such as organizational culture, professional norms, regulatory requirements, and resource availability significantly influence diffusion patterns. In healthcare contexts, the theory addresses unique challenges including evidence requirements, patient safety concerns, professional autonomy, regulatory oversight, and reimbursement policies. Applications include diffusion of clinical guidelines, medical devices, health information technologies, prevention programs, and treatment protocols. The theory has evolved to incorporate implementation science concepts, addressing not just adoption decisions but sustained implementation and integration into routine practice. Healthcare-specific modifications emphasize the role of professional networks, academic medical centers, regulatory agencies, and quality improvement initiatives in innovation spread.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: The process by which health-related innovations (medical technologies, clinical practices, treatment protocols, prevention programs) are communicated through certain channels over time among members of healthcare systems and populations, including factors that facilitate or impede adoption and implementation.
    • Scope: Healthcare innovation adoption contexts including clinical practice adoption, medical technology diffusion, health intervention implementation, public health program spread, and organizational health innovation adoption within hospitals, clinics, and health systems.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Innovation Characteristics in Healthcare¶

    Relative Advantage¶

    Definition: The degree to which a health innovation is perceived as better than existing medical practices, treatments, or technologies

    Healthcare-Specific Dimensions: - Clinical Effectiveness: Improved patient outcomes, reduced complications, enhanced treatment success rates - Cost-Effectiveness: Reduced healthcare costs, improved resource utilization, better value for money - Efficiency Gains: Time savings for providers, streamlined workflows, reduced administrative burden - Quality of Care: Enhanced patient safety, improved care coordination, better patient experience - Professional Benefits: Increased provider satisfaction, enhanced professional reputation, career advancement

    Measurement in Healthcare: - Clinical Outcomes: Mortality rates, morbidity measures, functional status improvements - Economic Outcomes: Cost per quality-adjusted life year (QALY), return on investment, budget impact - Process Measures: Time to diagnosis, length of stay, readmission rates, medication errors - Patient-Reported Outcomes: Patient satisfaction, quality of life, symptom relief - Provider Outcomes: Job satisfaction, professional efficacy, work-life balance

    Examples of Relative Advantage: - Electronic Health Records: Improved care coordination vs. paper records - Minimally Invasive Surgery: Reduced recovery time vs. traditional surgical approaches - Telemedicine: Increased access to care vs. in-person visits only - Clinical Decision Support: Enhanced diagnostic accuracy vs. unaided clinical judgment

    Compatibility¶

    Definition: The degree to which a health innovation fits with existing healthcare values, practices, organizational culture, and patient needs

    Healthcare Compatibility Factors: - Professional Values: Alignment with medical ethics, evidence-based practice principles, patient-centered care - Organizational Culture: Fit with institutional mission, quality improvement philosophy, innovation climate - Workflow Integration: Compatibility with existing clinical processes, documentation systems, care pathways - Patient Preferences: Consistency with patient values, cultural beliefs, treatment preferences - Regulatory Compliance: Adherence to healthcare regulations, accreditation standards, legal requirements

    Assessment Dimensions: - Clinical Workflow: How innovation integrates with existing patient care processes - Technology Infrastructure: Compatibility with current information systems and equipment - Training Requirements: Fit with existing staff skills and educational programs - Financial Systems: Alignment with reimbursement mechanisms and budgeting processes - Quality Measures: Consistency with quality indicators and performance metrics

    Complexity¶

    Definition: The degree to which a health innovation is perceived as difficult to understand, learn, or use in healthcare settings

    Healthcare Complexity Factors: - Technical Complexity: Sophistication of medical technology, equipment requirements, maintenance needs - Clinical Complexity: Number of steps in protocols, decision-making requirements, skill demands - Organizational Complexity: Implementation requirements, training needs, system changes - Cognitive Complexity: Information processing demands, learning curve, expertise requirements - Coordination Complexity: Need for multi-disciplinary collaboration, care team coordination

    Complexity Reduction Strategies: - Training Programs: Comprehensive education and skill development initiatives - User Interface Design: Intuitive technology interfaces and workflow optimization - Support Systems: Technical support, clinical champions, peer mentoring - Phased Implementation: Gradual rollout with increasing complexity over time - Decision Aids: Clinical guidelines, protocols, and decision support tools

    Trialability¶

    Definition: The degree to which a health innovation can be experimented with on a limited basis before full adoption

    Healthcare Trialability Approaches: - Pilot Programs: Small-scale implementation in selected units or departments - Simulation Training: Practice environments for skill development and testing - Vendor Demonstrations: Trial periods with equipment or software systems - Research Studies: Participation in clinical trials or quality improvement projects - Peer Observation: Visiting other institutions using the innovation

    Factors Affecting Trialability: - Resource Requirements: Cost and time needed for trial implementation - Safety Considerations: Patient safety requirements and risk management - Regulatory Constraints: FDA approval, institutional review board requirements - Organizational Policies: Institutional procedures for innovation testing - Professional Liability: Legal and ethical considerations for experimental practices

    Observability¶

    Definition: The degree to which the results of a health innovation are visible and communicable to others in healthcare settings

    Healthcare Observability Elements: - Clinical Outcomes: Visible improvements in patient health and treatment results - Process Improvements: Observable changes in workflow efficiency and care delivery - Quality Metrics: Measurable improvements in quality indicators and performance measures - Cost Savings: Demonstrable financial benefits and resource optimization - Professional Recognition: Awards, publications, and peer acknowledgment

    Enhancing Observability: - Data Dashboard: Real-time performance monitoring and outcome tracking - Case Studies: Documentation of successful implementation and results - Peer Presentations: Sharing experiences at conferences and professional meetings - Publications: Research articles and quality improvement reports - Site Visits: Opportunities for peers to observe innovation in practice

    Adopter Categories in Healthcare¶

    Innovators (2.5%)¶

    Characteristics in Healthcare: - Venturesome: Willing to take risks with new medical technologies and practices - Cosmopolitan: Connected to external professional networks and research communities - Risk-Taking: Comfortable with uncertainty and potential failure - Resource-Rich: Access to funding, time, and organizational support for innovation

    Healthcare Examples: - Academic Physicians: Researchers developing new treatment protocols - Early Technology Adopters: Physicians using cutting-edge medical devices - Innovation Champions: Healthcare leaders promoting organizational change - Research Institutions: Academic medical centers testing new interventions

    Early Adopters (13.5%)¶

    Characteristics in Healthcare: - Opinion Leaders: Respected professionals who influence colleague adoption decisions - Judicious: Careful evaluation of innovations before adoption - Well-Integrated: Strong connections within professional and organizational networks - Role Models: Others look to them for guidance on innovation adoption

    Healthcare Examples: - Department Chiefs: Medical directors implementing new clinical protocols - Professional Society Leaders: Physicians promoting evidence-based guidelines - Quality Champions: Healthcare professionals leading improvement initiatives - Thought Leaders: Recognized experts in specialized medical fields

    Early Majority (34%)¶

    Characteristics in Healthcare: - Deliberate: Careful consideration before adopting innovations - Pragmatic: Focus on proven benefits and practical implementation - Connected: Part of professional networks but not opinion leaders - Evidence-Oriented: Require demonstration of effectiveness before adoption

    Late Majority (34%)¶

    Characteristics in Healthcare: - Skeptical: Cautious about new innovations and prefer established practices - Peer-Influenced: Adopt after majority of colleagues have already adopted - Resource-Conscious: Concerned about costs and implementation challenges - Risk-Averse: Prefer proven technologies and well-established practices

    Laggards (16%)¶

    Characteristics in Healthcare: - Traditional: Strong preference for established medical practices - Isolated: Limited connections to professional networks and innovation sources - Suspicious: Skeptical of new technologies and external change agents - Resource-Limited: May lack access to training, support, or implementation resources

    Communication Channels in Healthcare¶

    Mass Media Channels¶

    Function: Create awareness and knowledge about health innovations - Medical Journals: Peer-reviewed publications reporting research findings - Professional Conferences: Medical meetings and symposiums showcasing innovations - Online Resources: Medical websites, databases, and digital platforms - Traditional Media: News coverage of medical breakthroughs and health innovations

    Interpersonal Channels¶

    Function: Influence attitudes and persuade adoption decisions - Colleague Networks: Informal communication between healthcare professionals - Mentoring Relationships: Senior professionals guiding junior colleagues - Professional Societies: Formal and informal interactions within medical organizations - Multidisciplinary Teams: Communication within healthcare teams and committees

    Opinion Leaders in Healthcare¶

    Role: Influential professionals who shape adoption decisions of others - Clinical Experts: Physicians with specialized knowledge and reputation - Research Leaders: Scientists and academics conducting innovation research - Administrative Leaders: Healthcare executives and department heads - Professional Advocates: Individuals promoting specific innovations or practices

    Social System Factors in Healthcare¶

    Organizational Structure¶

    Impact on Diffusion: - Hierarchical Systems: Clear authority structures that can facilitate or impede innovation - Decentralized Networks: Distributed decision-making that may slow or accelerate adoption - Professional Autonomy: Individual practitioner freedom to adopt innovations - Committee Structures: Formal groups evaluating and approving innovations

    Healthcare Culture and Norms¶

    Influence on Innovation Adoption: - Evidence-Based Practice: Cultural emphasis on scientific evidence and research - Patient Safety Focus: Risk aversion and careful evaluation of new practices - Quality Improvement: Organizational commitment to continuous improvement - Professional Standards: Adherence to medical ethics and professional guidelines

    Resource Availability¶

    Factors Affecting Adoption: - Financial Resources: Funding for equipment, training, and implementation - Human Resources: Availability of skilled staff and training capacity - Technical Infrastructure: Information systems and equipment support - Time Resources: Staff availability for learning and implementation

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Health innovations with greater perceived relative advantage, compatibility, trialability, and observability, and lower complexity are adopted more rapidly

    P2: Healthcare adopter categories differ in their innovation adoption timing, risk tolerance, and communication patterns

    P3: Mass media channels are most effective for creating awareness, while interpersonal channels are most effective for persuasion in healthcare settings

    P4: Opinion leaders play crucial roles in healthcare innovation diffusion by influencing colleague adoption decisions

    P5: Social system characteristics including organizational structure, culture, and resources significantly influence innovation diffusion rates

    P6: The innovation-decision process in healthcare involves unique considerations including evidence requirements, safety concerns, and regulatory compliance

    P7: Healthcare innovations diffuse through professional networks with patterns influenced by specialty, geography, and institutional affiliations

    P8: Successful diffusion requires adaptation of innovations to local healthcare contexts and organizational factors

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Rational Decision-Making: Healthcare professionals make informed decisions based on available evidence and professional judgment
    • Innovation Benefits: Health innovations generally offer potential improvements over existing practices
    • Communication Networks: Healthcare professionals are connected through formal and informal communication channels
    • Professional Autonomy: Healthcare providers have some degree of freedom in adoption decisions
    • Organizational Influence: Healthcare organizations can facilitate or impede innovation adoption through policies and resources

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Evidence Requirements: Healthcare innovations require more rigorous evidence than innovations in other fields
    • Safety Imperatives: Patient safety concerns may override other adoption considerations
    • Regulatory Oversight: Government and professional body regulations significantly influence adoption decisions
    • Professional Liability: Legal and ethical responsibilities may limit willingness to adopt unproven innovations
    • Resource Constraints: Healthcare organizations often face significant financial and human resource limitations
    • Institutional Inertia: Large healthcare systems may resist change due to complexity and risk aversion
    • Specialty Differences: Diffusion patterns vary significantly across medical specialties and practice settings
    • Technology Dependence: Some innovations require substantial technological infrastructure and support
    • Reimbursement Policies: Insurance coverage and payment mechanisms strongly influence adoption decisions
    • Cultural Variation: Diffusion patterns may differ across healthcare systems in different countries and regions

    6) Operationalization & Measurement¶

    Construct Typical Operationalization Example Items Measurement Notes
    Relative Advantage 5-7 item scale "This innovation is more effective than current practice" (1-7) Healthcare-specific benefits should be emphasized
    Compatibility 4-6 item scale "This innovation fits well with our current workflow" (1-7) Include professional values and organizational culture
    Complexity 3-5 item scale "This innovation is difficult to use" (1-7, reverse coded) Focus on learning curve and implementation challenges
    Trialability 3-4 item scale "I can try this innovation on a limited basis" (1-7) Consider regulatory and safety constraints
    Observability 4-5 item scale "Results of this innovation are clearly visible" (1-7) Include both clinical and process outcomes
    Adoption Rate Time-to-adoption analysis Months/years from awareness to implementation Track through multiple measurement points

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Longitudinal Diffusion Study Design¶

    Multi-Wave Panel Study: - Wave 1 (Baseline): Pre-innovation awareness assessment, organizational characteristics, provider attitudes - Wave 2 (Awareness): Initial exposure measurement, early perceptions, intention formation - Wave 3 (Early Adoption): Pilot implementation, early adopter experiences, opinion leader influence - Wave 4 (Mainstream Adoption): Broader implementation patterns, organizational changes, outcome measurement - Wave 5 (Sustainability): Long-term adoption patterns, innovation adaptation, discontinuation analysis

    Cross-Institutional Comparison¶

    Multi-Site Diffusion Analysis: - Site Selection: Diverse healthcare organizations (academic medical centers, community hospitals, clinics) - Standardized Measures: Common instruments across all sites for comparison - Organizational Assessment: Structure, culture, resources, and innovation climate - Environmental Factors: Regulatory context, competitive landscape, patient population

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Innovation Characteristics Effect Sizes¶

    Meta-Analytic Findings from Healthcare Studies: - Relative Advantage → Adoption: r = .65 (95% CI: .58-.71, k = 34 studies) - Compatibility → Adoption: r = .52 (95% CI: .46-.58, k = 28 studies) - Complexity → Adoption: r = -.48 (95% CI: -.55--.41, k = 31 studies) - Trialability → Adoption: r = .38 (95% CI: .31-.45, k = 19 studies) - Observability → Adoption: r = .44 (95% CI: .37-.51, k = 22 studies)

    Healthcare-Specific Patterns: - Evidence Requirements: Relative advantage must be supported by rigorous clinical evidence - Safety Concerns: Complexity has stronger negative effects in high-risk clinical contexts - Professional Networks: Observability effects amplified through professional communication channels - Regulatory Factors: Trialability limited by FDA requirements and institutional policies

    Adopter Category Characteristics in Healthcare¶

    Temporal Adoption Patterns: - Innovators: Adopt within 0-6 months of availability - Early Adopters: Adopt within 6-18 months - Early Majority: Adopt within 18-36 months - Late Majority: Adopt within 36-60 months - Laggards: Adopt after 60 months or never adopt

    Communication Channel Effectiveness¶

    Channel Usage by Adoption Stage: - Knowledge Stage: Medical journals (45%), conferences (32%), online resources (28%), colleagues (25%) - Persuasion Stage: Colleagues (68%), opinion leaders (52%), site visits (38%), training programs (35%) - Decision Stage: Direct experience (78%), peer recommendations (65%), organizational policy (48%) - Implementation Stage: Training programs (82%), technical support (71%), peer mentoring (59%)

    Professional Network Influence: - Specialty Networks: Stronger influence within medical specialties than across specialties - Geographic Proximity: Local networks more influential for implementation support - Institutional Ties: Shared affiliations increase communication and influence - Hierarchical Effects: Senior physicians influence junior colleagues more than peers

    Organizational Factors¶

    Healthcare System Characteristics: - Size Effects: Larger organizations adopt earlier but implement more slowly - Academic Affiliation: Academic medical centers are faster early adopters - Resource Availability: Well-funded organizations show higher adoption rates - Innovation Climate: Organizations with strong improvement culture adopt more readily

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Health Innovation Diffusion Key Differences
    Technology Acceptance Model Individual technology acceptance Both address healthcare technology adoption TAM focuses on individual beliefs; Diffusion theory emphasizes social processes and innovation characteristics
    Implementation Science Frameworks Evidence-based practice implementation Complementary approaches to healthcare innovation Diffusion theory provides adoption process; Implementation science focuses on sustained integration
    Social Network Theory Social structure and influence Professional networks crucial for diffusion Diffusion theory incorporates network effects; Social network theory provides detailed network analysis
    Organizational Learning Theory Organizational knowledge acquisition Both address organizational change and innovation Diffusion focuses on innovation spread; Learning theory emphasizes knowledge creation and retention

    Diffusion Theory vs. Technology Acceptance Model¶

    Scope and Level of Analysis: - Diffusion Theory: Multi-level analysis including individual, organizational, and system factors - TAM: Primarily individual-level cognitive decision-making process - Social Processes: Diffusion emphasizes social influence and communication; TAM focuses on individual beliefs - Innovation Characteristics: Diffusion theory provides detailed innovation attribute framework; TAM focuses on usefulness and ease of use

    Healthcare Applications: - Diffusion Strength: Better for understanding innovation spread across healthcare systems - TAM Strength: More detailed individual adoption prediction for specific technologies - Complementary Use: Diffusion for macro-level planning; TAM for micro-level design and implementation - Time Orientation: Diffusion theory more longitudinal; TAM more cross-sectional

    Diffusion Theory vs. Implementation Science¶

    Theoretical Foundation: - Diffusion Theory: Communication and social psychology roots - Implementation Science: Health services research and organizational psychology - Process Focus: Diffusion emphasizes adoption decision; Implementation science focuses on sustained use - Evidence Integration: Implementation science more explicit about evidence-practice gaps

    Practical Applications: - Diffusion Use: Understanding innovation spread patterns and adoption barriers - Implementation Use: Developing strategies for sustained evidence-based practice integration - Research Design: Diffusion uses longitudinal adoption studies; Implementation uses hybrid effectiveness-implementation trials - Outcome Measures: Diffusion tracks adoption rates; Implementation measures fidelity and sustainability

    Integration Opportunities: - Combined Frameworks: Using diffusion theory for adoption prediction and implementation science for sustainability - Multi-Phase Studies: Diffusion analysis during spread phase; Implementation focus during integration phase - Stakeholder Engagement: Diffusion theory for early adopter identification; Implementation science for barrier assessment

    Evidence Requirements: - Clinical Trials: Rigorous evidence standards for healthcare innovations - Safety Monitoring: Post-market surveillance and adverse event reporting - Comparative Effectiveness: Need for comparative studies with existing treatments - Real-World Evidence: Growing emphasis on effectiveness in routine practice settings

    Professional Culture: - Evidence-Based Practice: Strong cultural emphasis on scientific evidence - Patient Safety: Risk aversion and careful evaluation of new practices - Professional Autonomy: Individual practitioner decision-making authority - Continuing Education: Ongoing learning requirements for healthcare professionals

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Individual-Level Bias: - Limited Organizational Focus: Critics argue the theory overemphasizes individual adoption decisions while underestimating organizational and systemic factors - Context Neglect: Insufficient attention to healthcare-specific contexts like regulatory requirements, reimbursement policies, and safety imperatives - Power Dynamics: Theory doesn't adequately address how organizational hierarchies and professional power structures influence innovation diffusion

    Linear Process Assumption: - Non-Linear Reality: Healthcare innovation adoption often involves complex, iterative processes rather than linear stage progression - Reinvention Ignored: Theory underestimates how innovations are modified and adapted during implementation - Discontinuation Patterns: Limited attention to why healthcare innovations are abandoned after initial adoption

    Innovation Characteristics Limitations: - Oversimplification: Reducing complex healthcare innovations to five characteristics may miss important factors - Relative Weighting: Theory doesn't specify how to weight different characteristics when they conflict - Measurement Challenges: Difficulty in objectively measuring innovation characteristics in healthcare contexts

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Implementation Science Integration: - Adoption vs. Implementation: Debate over whether diffusion theory adequately addresses sustained implementation versus initial adoption - Evidence-Practice Gaps: Discussion about how diffusion theory explains persistent gaps between research evidence and clinical practice - Fidelity vs. Adaptation: Tension between maintaining innovation fidelity and allowing local adaptation - Sustainability Challenges: Questions about theory's ability to predict long-term sustainability of healthcare innovations

    Digital Health Disruption: - Technology Convergence: How diffusion patterns change when multiple technologies converge (AI, mobile health, telemedicine) - Platform Ecosystems: Traditional diffusion models may not apply to interconnected digital health platforms - Consumer-Driven Adoption: Changing dynamics when patients drive technology adoption rather than healthcare providers - Regulatory Lag: How rapid technological change outpaces regulatory frameworks designed for traditional diffusion patterns

    Equity and Access Considerations: - Innovation Equity: Debate over whether diffusion theory adequately addresses health equity and disparities - Digital Divide: How socioeconomic factors influence diffusion patterns in digital health innovations - Resource Requirements: Whether theory adequately considers resource constraints in low-resource settings - Cultural Appropriateness: Questions about applying Western-developed diffusion models in diverse cultural contexts

    Methodological Challenges¶

    Network Complexity: - Multi-Level Networks: Healthcare operates through complex, overlapping professional, organizational, and policy networks - Network Boundaries: Difficulty defining relevant network boundaries for diffusion analysis - Temporal Dynamics: Networks change over time, affecting diffusion patterns - Virtual Networks: How digital communication changes traditional network-based diffusion

    Outcome Measurement: - Multiple Outcomes: Healthcare innovations may have clinical, economic, and process outcomes that diffuse differently - Long-term Effects: Difficulty measuring long-term impacts of healthcare innovations - Unintended Consequences: Theory may not predict negative or unexpected outcomes of innovation adoption - Attribution Challenges: Difficulty attributing outcomes specifically to innovation adoption versus other factors

def classify_adopter_categories(professionals_df):
    """Classify healthcare professionals into adopter categories"""

    # Calculate adoption propensity based on multiple factors
    adoption_score = (
        0.3 * professionals_df['innovation_orientation'] +
        0.2 * professionals_df['network_centrality'] * 7 +  # Scale to 1-7
        0.2 * professionals_df['resource_availability'] +
        0.1 * (professionals_df['practice_setting'] == 'Academic').astype(int) * 2 +
        0.1 * np.where(professionals_df['years_experience'] < 10, 1, 
                      np.where(professionals_df['years_experience'] > 25, -1, 0)) +
        0.1 * np.random.normal(0, 0.5, len(professionals_df))
    )

    # Classify into adopter categories based on percentiles
    percentiles = np.percentile(adoption_score, [2.5, 16, 50, 84, 97.5])

    conditions = [
        adoption_score >= percentiles[4],  # Top 2.5%
        adoption_score >= percentiles[3],  # Next 13.5%
        adoption_score >= percentiles[2],  # Next 34%
        adoption_score >= percentiles[1],  # Next 34%
        adoption_score >= percentiles[0]   # Bottom 16%

    choices = ['Innovator', 'Early Adopter', 'Early Majority', 'Late Majority', 'Laggard']

    professionals_df['adopter_category'] = np.select(conditions, choices)
    professionals_df['adoption_score'] = adoption_score

    return professionals_df

def simulate_diffusion_over_time(professionals_df, innovation_name, time_periods=24):
    """Simulate adoption over time with network effects"""

    innovations = simulate_innovation_characteristics()
    innovation_chars = innovations[innovation_name]

    # Initialize adoption tracking
    adoption_history = pd.DataFrame(index=professionals_df.index)
    adoption_history['professional_id'] = professionals_df['professional_id']
    adoption_history['adopter_category'] = professionals_df['adopter_category']

    # Calculate base adoption probabilities
    base_probabilities = calculate_adoption_probability(professionals_df, innovation_chars)

    # Simulate adoption over time periods (months)
    adopted = np.zeros(len(professionals_df), dtype=bool)
    adoption_times = np.full(len(professionals_df), np.nan)

    for period in range(time_periods):
        # Calculate current adoption probability
        current_prob = base_probabilities.copy()

        # Network effect: increase probability based on peer adoption
        peer_adoption_rate = adopted.mean()
        network_effect = peer_adoption_rate * 0.3  # Peer influence factor
        current_prob += network_effect

        # Time-based effects for different adopter categories
        time_factors = {
            'Innovator': 1.0 if period < 3 else 0.5,
            'Early Adopter': 0.3 if period < 3 else 1.0 if period < 8 else 0.6,
            'Early Majority': 0.1 if period < 6 else 1.0 if period < 16 else 0.7,
            'Late Majority': 0.05 if period < 12 else 1.0 if period < 20 else 0.8,
            'Laggard': 0.02 if period < 18 else 0.5
        }

        for category, factor in time_factors.items():
            mask = professionals_df['adopter_category'] == category
            current_prob[mask] *= factor

        # Determine new adopters this period
        not_adopted = ~adopted
        new_adopters = (np.random.random(len(professionals_df)) < current_prob) & not_adopted

        # Update adoption status
        adopted[new_adopters] = True
        adoption_times[new_adopters] = period

        # Store period data
        adoption_history[f'period_{period}'] = adopted.copy()

    professionals_df['adoption_time'] = adoption_times
    professionals_df['adopted'] = adopted

    return professionals_df, adoption_history

def calculate_adoption_probability(professional_data, innovation_characteristics):
    """Calculate adoption probability based on professional and innovation characteristics"""

    # Base probability from adopter category
    category_probabilities = {
        'Innovator': 0.85,
        'Early Adopter': 0.72,
        'Early Majority': 0.58,
        'Late Majority': 0.35,
        'Laggard': 0.15
    }

    base_prob = professional_data['adopter_category'].map(category_probabilities)

    # Adjust based on innovation characteristics
    innovation_effect = (
        0.25 * (innovation_characteristics['relative_advantage'] - 4) / 3 +
        0.20 * (innovation_characteristics['compatibility'] - 4) / 3 +
        0.20 * (7 - innovation_characteristics['complexity'] - 4) / 3 +  # Invert complexity
        0.15 * (innovation_characteristics['trialability'] - 4) / 3 +
        0.20 * (innovation_characteristics['observability'] - 4) / 3
    )

    # Professional characteristics modifier
    professional_modifier = (
        0.3 * (professional_data['innovation_orientation'] - 4) / 3 +
        0.2 * (professional_data['resource_availability'] - 4) / 3 +
        0.1 * (professional_data['practice_setting'] == 'Academic').astype(int) +
        0.1 * (professional_data['network_centrality'] - 0.5) * 2
    )

    # Combine effects
    final_probability = np.clip(
        base_prob + 0.3 * innovation_effect + 0.2 * professional_modifier,
        0.05, 0.95
    )

    return final_probability

    # Adoption rate by category
    category_adoption = professionals_df.groupby('adopter_category').agg({
        'adopted': ['count', 'sum', 'mean'],
        'adoption_time': 'mean'
    }).round(2)

    analysis_results['category_analysis'] = category_adoption

    # Specialty differences
    specialty_adoption = professionals_df.groupby('specialty').agg({
        'adopted': 'mean',
        'adoption_time': 'mean'
    }).round(2)

    analysis_results['specialty_analysis'] = specialty_adoption

    # Time series analysis
    time_series = []
    for col in adoption_history.columns:
        if col.startswith('period_'):
            period = int(col.split('_')[1])
            adoption_rate = adoption_history[col].mean()
            time_series.append({'period': period, 'cumulative_adoption': adoption_rate})

    time_series_df = pd.DataFrame(time_series)
    analysis_results['time_series'] = time_series_df

    return analysis_results

def generate_diffusion_report(professionals_df, adoption_history, innovation_name):
    """Generate comprehensive diffusion analysis report"""

    analysis = analyze_diffusion_patterns(professionals_df, adoption_history)

    print(f"=== HEALTHCARE INNOVATION DIFFUSION ANALYSIS: {innovation_name.upper()} ===\n")

    # Overall adoption statistics
    total_adoption_rate = professionals_df['adopted'].mean()
    mean_adoption_time = professionals_df['adoption_time'].mean()

    print("1. OVERALL ADOPTION METRICS")
    print("-" * 40)
    print(f"Total Adoption Rate: {total_adoption_rate:.1%}")
    print(f"Average Time to Adoption: {mean_adoption_time:.1f} months")
    print(f"Number of Professionals: {len(professionals_df)}")
    print()

    # Adopter category analysis
    print("2. ADOPTER CATEGORY PERFORMANCE")
    print("-" * 40)
    category_stats = analysis['category_analysis']
    for category in category_stats.index:
        count = category_stats.loc[category, ('adopted', 'count')]
        adopted = category_stats.loc[category, ('adopted', 'sum')]
        rate = category_stats.loc[category, ('adopted', 'mean')]
        avg_time = category_stats.loc[category, ('adoption_time', 'mean')]

        print(f"{category}:")
        print(f"  Count: {count} professionals")
        print(f"  Adopted: {adopted} ({rate:.1%})")
        print(f"  Average Time: {avg_time:.1f} months")
    print()

    return analysis

# Network analysis for healthcare innovation diffusion
def create_professional_network(professionals_df):
    """Create professional network based on similarity and connections"""

    # Create network based on professional characteristics
    G = nx.Graph()

    # Add nodes with attributes
    for idx, prof in professionals_df.iterrows():
        G.add_node(idx, 
                  specialty=prof['specialty'],
                  adopter_category=prof['adopter_category'],
                  practice_setting=prof['practice_setting'])

    # Add edges based on professional similarity
    for i in range(len(professionals_df)):
        for j in range(i+1, len(professionals_df)):
            prof_i = professionals_df.iloc[i]
            prof_j = professionals_df.iloc[j]

            # Connection probability based on similarity
            similarity_score = 0
            if prof_i['specialty'] == prof_j['specialty']:
                similarity_score += 0.4
            if prof_i['practice_setting'] == prof_j['practice_setting']:
                similarity_score += 0.3

            # Random connection probability
            if np.random.random() < similarity_score * 0.2:
                G.add_edge(i, j)

    return G
