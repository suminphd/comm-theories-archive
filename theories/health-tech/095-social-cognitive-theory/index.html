    Social Cognitive Theory

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) provides a comprehensive framework for understanding human behavior through the dynamic interaction of personal, environmental, and behavioral factors. Developed by Albert Bandura (1986), SCT emphasizes human agency and the capacity for self-direction in behavior change and maintenance. The theory's core principle is reciprocal determinism, proposing that behavior, personal factors (cognitive, affective, biological), and environmental influences operate as interacting determinants that influence each other bidirectionally. Self-efficacy serves as the central mechanism, defined as beliefs in one's capability to organize and execute actions required to produce given attainments. Observational learning explains how individuals acquire new behaviors by watching others, involving attention, retention, reproduction, and motivation processes. The theory emphasizes self-regulation through goal-setting, self-monitoring, and self-evaluation. Outcome expectations represent beliefs about the consequences of performing behaviors, while self-efficacy expectations focus on confidence in one's ability to perform the behavior itself. SCT has demonstrated strong empirical support across diverse domains including health behavior change, education, therapy, organizational behavior, and technology adoption. The theory's practical applications emphasize building self-efficacy through mastery experiences, vicarious learning, verbal persuasion, and emotional/physiological states. Modern applications include digital health interventions, social media behavior change, and technology-mediated learning environments.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theory explaining human behavior through reciprocal interactions between personal factors, environmental influences, and behavioral patterns, emphasizing self-efficacy, observational learning, and human agency in behavior acquisition, change, and maintenance.
    • Scope: All domains of human behavior where learning, self-regulation, and behavior change occur, including health behaviors, educational achievement, therapeutic interventions, organizational performance, social behaviors, and technology adoption.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Reciprocal Determinism¶

    Triadic Model¶

    Definition: The dynamic interaction between three sets of factors that influence each other bidirectionally: personal factors, environmental factors, and behavioral factors

    Personal Factors: - Cognitive Factors: Knowledge, beliefs, attitudes, expectations, self-perceptions - Affective Factors: Emotions, mood states, emotional reactions, affective responses - Biological Factors: Physical capabilities, health status, genetic predispositions, neurological factors

    Environmental Factors: - Physical Environment: Built environment, resources, barriers, physical context - Social Environment: Social support, cultural norms, peer influences, social modeling - Institutional Environment: Policies, rules, organizational culture, formal structures

    Behavioral Factors: - Past Behavior: Previous experience with similar behaviors and their outcomes - Current Behavior: Ongoing behavioral patterns and their immediate consequences - Behavioral Skills: Specific competencies and abilities required for behavior performance

    Bidirectional Influences¶

    Person ↔ Environment: - Personal characteristics influence environmental selection and modification - Environmental factors shape personal development and cognitive processes - Individual agency allows active environment construction and navigation

    Person ↔ Behavior: - Personal factors (beliefs, skills, emotions) influence behavioral choices and performance - Behavioral experiences modify personal factors through learning and feedback - Self-reflection and self-evaluation create ongoing person-behavior interaction

    Environment ↔ Behavior: - Environmental conditions facilitate or constrain behavioral options - Behaviors modify environmental conditions through consequences and social reactions - Environmental feedback shapes future behavioral patterns and choices

    Self-Efficacy¶

    Definition and Characteristics¶

    Self-Efficacy: Beliefs in one's capabilities to organize and execute the courses of action required to produce given attainments

    Key Features: - Task-Specific: Self-efficacy is behavior and context-specific, not a global trait - Future-Oriented: Focuses on prospective capability rather than past performance - Capability Beliefs: Concerns what one can do, not what one will do - Gradated: Varies in strength, generality, and level of challenge

    Dimensions of Self-Efficacy: - Level: The difficulty level of tasks one believes they can perform - Strength: The degree of confidence in performing tasks at different levels - Generality: The breadth of situations across which self-efficacy beliefs apply

    Sources of Self-Efficacy¶

    Mastery Experiences: - Performance Accomplishments: Successful task completion builds efficacy beliefs - Skill Development: Gradual improvement through practice and training - Challenge Management: Overcoming obstacles strengthens efficacy perceptions - Failure Integration: Learning from setbacks without undermining confidence

    Vicarious Experiences: - Social Modeling: Observing similar others succeed increases efficacy beliefs - Comparative Assessment: Evaluating one's capabilities relative to observed models - Identification Factors: Greater similarity to models enhances vicarious efficacy - Diverse Models: Exposure to multiple successful models strengthens beliefs

    Verbal Persuasion: - Encouragement: Positive feedback and encouragement from credible sources - Instructional Guidance: Expert advice and coaching to build confidence - Social Support: Expressions of confidence from important others - Realistic Appraisal: Honest but supportive evaluation of capabilities

    Emotional and Physiological States: - Arousal Interpretation: How physical sensations are interpreted (anxiety vs. excitement) - Mood Effects: Positive emotional states enhance efficacy perceptions - Stress Management: Learning to manage stress and anxiety improves confidence - Physical Condition: Health and fitness levels affecting capability perceptions

    Effects of Self-Efficacy¶

    Behavioral Outcomes: - Choice: Higher self-efficacy leads to choosing more challenging tasks - Effort: Greater effort expenditure when facing difficulties - Persistence: Longer persistence when encountering obstacles or setbacks - Performance: Generally positive relationship between self-efficacy and performance

    Cognitive and Emotional Outcomes: - Goal Setting: Higher efficacy associated with more ambitious goal setting - Problem-Solving: Enhanced problem-solving under high efficacy conditions - Stress and Anxiety: Lower stress and anxiety when facing challenges - Causal Attribution: More adaptive attributions for success and failure

    Observational Learning¶

    Modeling Process¶

    Definition: Learning that occurs through observing others' behaviors and their consequences, without direct personal experience

    Four Component Processes:

    Attention: - Selective Observation: Focusing on relevant aspects of modeled behavior - Model Characteristics: Attractiveness, competence, status, similarity affecting attention - Observer Characteristics: Cognitive capacity, motivation, arousal level - Situational Factors: Environmental conditions affecting attention allocation

    Retention: - Symbolic Coding: Converting observed behaviors into cognitive representations - Mental Rehearsal: Cognitive practice of observed behaviors - Memory Organization: Structuring information for later retrieval - Cognitive Processing: Active processing and integration of observed information

    Reproduction: - Motor Skill: Physical capability to perform observed behaviors - Cognitive Representation: Clear mental model of the behavior sequence - Feedback Integration: Using performance feedback to refine reproduction - Practice Opportunities: Chances to attempt and improve behavioral reproduction

    Motivation: - Outcome Expectations: Expected consequences of performing the behavior - Vicarious Reinforcement: Observing others being rewarded for behavior - Self-Reinforcement: Internal satisfaction from successful performance - External Incentives: Environmental rewards and punishments

    Types of Modeling¶

    Live Modeling: - Direct Observation: Watching real people perform behaviors in person - Interactive Learning: Opportunities for questions and immediate feedback - Contextual Authenticity: Realistic environmental conditions and social interactions - Relationship Factors: Personal connection with model affecting learning

    Symbolic Modeling: - Media Representations: Learning from television, film, books, digital media - Virtual Models: Computer-generated characters and avatars - Narrative Learning: Stories and case studies providing behavioral examples - Instructional Materials: Educational content presenting behavioral models

    Self-Modeling: - Performance Review: Observing recordings of one's own successful performance - Mental Rehearsal: Visualizing oneself performing desired behaviors - Self-Observation: Monitoring and reflecting on one's own behavioral patterns - Identity Development: Using self-modeling to strengthen behavioral identity

    Outcome Expectations¶

    Types of Outcome Expectations¶

    Physical Outcomes: - Health Consequences: Expected physical health effects of behavior - Sensory Effects: Anticipated physical sensations and experiences - Functional Outcomes: Impact on physical functioning and capabilities - Appearance Changes: Expected effects on physical appearance

    Social Outcomes: - Approval/Disapproval: Anticipated reactions from others - Relationship Effects: Impact on social relationships and connections - Status Changes: Effects on social standing and recognition - Social Integration: Influence on group membership and belonging

    Self-Evaluative Outcomes: - Self-Satisfaction: Personal satisfaction and pride from behavior performance - Self-Concept: Effects on self-image and identity - Goal Achievement: Progress toward personal objectives and values - Meaning and Purpose: Contribution to life meaning and purpose

    Outcome Expectation Formation¶

    Direct Experience: - Personal History: Past consequences of similar behaviors - Immediate Feedback: Short-term outcomes from behavior performance - Long-term Results: Extended consequences observed over time - Pattern Recognition: Identifying consistent outcome patterns

    Vicarious Learning: - Observed Consequences: Watching outcomes experienced by others - Social Comparison: Comparing one's likely outcomes to others' experiences - Cultural Learning: Understanding typical outcomes within cultural context - Media Influence: Learning about outcomes through media representations

    Social Communication: - Expert Information: Professional advice about likely outcomes - Peer Sharing: Information from others with similar experiences - Cultural Transmission: Traditional knowledge about behavior consequences - Educational Content: Formal instruction about behavior outcomes

    Self-Regulation¶

    Self-Regulatory Process¶

    Self-Observation: - Behavioral Monitoring: Systematic tracking of one's own behaviors - Performance Assessment: Evaluating quality and quantity of performance - Context Awareness: Understanding situational factors affecting behavior - Pattern Recognition: Identifying trends and cycles in behavioral patterns

    Judgmental Process: - Standard Setting: Establishing criteria for acceptable performance - Comparative Evaluation: Assessing performance against standards and goals - Causal Attribution: Understanding reasons for success or failure - Value Assessment: Evaluating importance and priority of different outcomes

    Self-Reaction: - Self-Reinforcement: Rewarding oneself for meeting standards - Self-Punishment: Consequences for failing to meet standards - Goal Adjustment: Modifying goals based on performance evaluation - Strategy Revision: Changing approaches based on self-evaluation results

    Goal Setting in Self-Regulation¶

    Goal Characteristics: - Specificity: Clear, precise definitions of desired outcomes - Challenge Level: Appropriately difficult but achievable objectives - Proximity: Short-term goals providing immediate direction - Measurability: Quantifiable criteria for assessing progress

    Goal Functions: - Direction: Focusing attention and effort on relevant activities - Energizing: Mobilizing effort proportional to goal difficulty - Persistence: Sustaining effort over time toward goal achievement - Strategy Development: Promoting development of task-appropriate strategies

    Human Agency¶

    Core Features of Agency¶

    Intentionality: - Purposeful Action: Behavior directed toward specific intended outcomes - Planning: Developing strategies and action plans for goal achievement - Decision-Making: Conscious choices about behavioral alternatives - Commitment: Sustained dedication to chosen courses of action

    Forethought: - Future Orientation: Considering long-term consequences of current actions - Anticipatory Self-Regulation: Adjusting behavior based on expected outcomes - Scenario Planning: Mentally rehearsing different possible futures - Prevention Focus: Anticipating and avoiding potential problems

    Self-Reactiveness: - Self-Direction: Guiding behavior according to personal standards and goals - Self-Motivation: Maintaining motivation through self-regulatory processes - Adaptive Response: Adjusting behavior based on ongoing feedback - Performance Optimization: Continuously improving behavioral effectiveness

    Self-Reflectiveness: - Metacognition: Thinking about one's own thinking and behavioral processes - Self-Evaluation: Assessing the adequacy of one's knowledge and capabilities - Meaning-Making: Understanding the significance and implications of experiences - Identity Development: Forming and refining self-concept through reflection

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Human behavior results from reciprocal interactions between personal, environmental, and behavioral factors

    P2: Self-efficacy beliefs are the primary determinant of behavioral choices, effort, and persistence

    P3: People learn behaviors through observing others without requiring direct experience

    P4: Outcome expectations and self-efficacy expectations jointly influence behavior

    P5: Self-regulation enables individuals to control their behavior through goal-setting, self-monitoring, and self-evaluation

    P6: Human agency allows individuals to exercise control over their lives through intentional action

    P7: Behavior change requires addressing multiple factors including self-efficacy, outcome expectations, and environmental conditions

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Human Agency: Individuals have capacity for self-direction and intentional action
    • Cognitive Capability: People can process information, form beliefs, and make decisions
    • Learning Ability: Individuals can acquire new behaviors through observation and experience
    • Self-Awareness: People can observe and evaluate their own behavior and mental processes
    • Goal-Directed Behavior: Human behavior is largely purposeful and directed toward objectives

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Cognitive Impairment: Theory applicability limited when severe cognitive dysfunction present
    • Involuntary Behaviors: Less relevant for reflexive, automatic, or compulsive behaviors
    • Extreme Environmental Constraints: Limited applicability under conditions of severely restricted choice
    • Developmental Limitations: Age-related constraints on cognitive and self-regulatory capabilities
    • Cultural Specificity: Some aspects may vary across cultures with different agency and control concepts
    • Crisis Situations: Emergency contexts may override normal self-regulatory processes

    6) Operationalization & Measurement¶

    Construct Typical Operationalization Example Items Measurement Notes
    Self-Efficacy Task-specific confidence scales "I can [specific behavior] even when it is difficult" (1-10) Use behavior-specific measures
    Outcome Expectations Expected consequence ratings "If I [behavior], I expect [outcome]" (1-7 likely-unlikely) Include physical, social, self-evaluative outcomes
    Self-Regulation Goal-setting and monitoring measures "I set specific goals for [behavior]" (1-7); "I monitor my progress" (1-7) Assess planning, monitoring, evaluation
    Observational Learning Modeling exposure and retention assessment "I have seen others successfully [behavior]" (yes/no); recall accuracy tests Measure attention, retention, reproduction
    Environmental Factors Social support and barrier assessments "People important to me support my [behavior]" (1-7); barrier frequency ratings Include both facilitators and obstacles
    Behavioral Performance Objective behavior measurement Frequency, duration, quality ratings, performance indicators Use multiple measurement methods when possible

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental Self-Efficacy Manipulation¶

    Self-Efficacy Enhancement Study: - Participants: Individuals with low baseline self-efficacy for target behavior - Manipulation: Random assignment to self-efficacy building interventions vs. control - Interventions: Mastery experiences, modeling, verbal persuasion, arousal management - Measures: Pre-post self-efficacy, outcome expectations, behavioral performance - Analysis: ANOVA examining intervention effects on self-efficacy and subsequent behavior

    Procedure: 1. Baseline Assessment: Self-efficacy, outcome expectations, past behavior, individual characteristics 2. Randomization: Assignment to specific self-efficacy enhancement condition 3. Intervention Delivery: Structured program targeting assigned self-efficacy source 4. Immediate Post-test: Self-efficacy and outcome expectation measurement 5. Behavioral Assessment: Performance measurement under standardized conditions 6. Follow-up: Delayed assessment of self-efficacy maintenance and behavior persistence

    Longitudinal Behavior Change Study¶

    Natural History Design: - Timeline: Multiple assessments over behavior change process (6-24 months) - Participants: Individuals attempting specific behavior change - Measures: SCT constructs, environmental factors, behavioral outcomes at each timepoint - Analysis: Growth curve modeling and cross-lagged panel analysis

    Assessment Schedule: 

    Baseline: Pre-change SCT constructs and environmental assessment
Month 1: Early change experiences and self-efficacy adjustment
Month 3: Behavior establishment and self-regulation development
Month 6: Maintenance phase and environmental integration
Month 12: Long-term sustainability and mastery
Month 24: Stable behavior patterns and outcome achievement

    Intervention Effectiveness Trial¶

    Multi-Component SCT Intervention: - Design: Randomized controlled trial comparing SCT-based intervention to control condition - Components: Self-efficacy building, modeling, goal-setting, self-monitoring, environmental modification - Population: Specific behavior change target (health, educational, organizational) - Outcomes: Primary behavioral outcomes and mediating SCT construct changes

    Intervention Components: - Self-Efficacy Building: Graduated task assignment, successful experience facilitation - Modeling Programs: Peer models, video demonstrations, self-modeling techniques - Goal Setting Training: SMART goal development, progress tracking systems - Environmental Modification: Barrier reduction, social support enhancement - Self-Regulation Skills: Monitoring tools, self-evaluation training, problem-solving

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Self-Efficacy Effects¶

    Behavioral Performance Relationships: - Performance Correlation: Self-efficacy typically correlates r = .30-.60 with behavioral performance - Goal Setting: Higher self-efficacy associated with more challenging goal selection - Persistence: Strong self-efficacy predicts longer persistence when facing obstacles - Effort Allocation: Positive relationship between self-efficacy and effort expenditure

    Mediating Role: - Intervention Effects: Self-efficacy often mediates 40-60% of intervention effects on behavior - Skill-Performance: Self-efficacy partially mediates relationship between skills and performance - Experience-Future: Self-efficacy mediates effects of past experience on future behavior - Environmental-Individual: Self-efficacy mediates environmental influences on individual behavior

    Domain-Specific Patterns: - Health Behaviors: Consistently strong self-efficacy effects across diverse health behaviors - Academic Performance: Self-efficacy predicts academic achievement beyond ability measures - Therapeutic Outcomes: Self-efficacy central to psychotherapy and behavior change success - Organizational Performance: Work-related self-efficacy predicts job performance and satisfaction

    Observational Learning Effectiveness¶

    Modeling Characteristics: - Model Similarity: Greater behavioral change when models similar to observers - Model Competence: Expert models more effective for skill acquisition - Model Status: Higher status models increase attention and motivation - Multiple Models: Diverse models enhance learning generalization

    Process Components: - Attention Effects: Environmental distractions reduce modeling effectiveness - Retention Enhancement: Active cognitive processing improves behavioral reproduction - Motor Reproduction: Physical practice necessary for motor skill acquisition - Motivational Factors: Incentives and outcome expectations drive model imitation

    Media Modeling: - Video Effectiveness: Video models comparable to live models for many behaviors - Interactive Media: Computer-based modeling programs show promising results - Virtual Reality: Immersive modeling environments enhance engagement and learning - Social Media: Peer modeling through social platforms influences behavior

    Self-Regulation Patterns¶

    Goal Setting Effects: - Specific Goals: Specific goals produce 12-15% better performance than "do your best" goals - Challenging Goals: Difficult but attainable goals enhance performance more than easy goals - Proximal Goals: Short-term goals more effective than distant goals for maintaining motivation - Goal Commitment: Goal effects depend on commitment level and goal acceptance

    Self-Monitoring Impact: - Behavior Awareness: Self-monitoring increases awareness of behavioral patterns - Performance Improvement: Monitoring alone often produces 10-20% behavior improvement - Feedback Integration: Combining monitoring with feedback enhances effectiveness - Technology Tools: Digital monitoring tools facilitate sustained self-observation

    Self-Evaluation and Reinforcement: - Standard Setting: Personal standards influence self-evaluation and motivation - Self-Reward: Self-administered rewards maintain behavior when external rewards absent - Causal Attribution: Adaptive attributions enhance self-regulation effectiveness - Identity Integration: Behaviors consistent with identity receive stronger self-reinforcement

    Reciprocal Determinism Evidence¶

    Person-Environment Interactions: - Environmental Selection: People actively choose environments matching their characteristics - Environmental Modification: Individuals change environments to suit their needs and goals - Bi-directional Influence: Environmental changes reciprocally influence personal characteristics - Dynamic Processes: Person-environment interactions change over time and development

    Person-Behavior Interactions: - Behavioral Impact: Performance experiences modify self-efficacy and outcome expectations - Skill Development: Behavioral practice enhances personal capabilities and confidence - Identity Formation: Repeated behaviors contribute to self-concept and identity development - Cognitive Change: Behavioral experiences update knowledge and belief systems

    Environment-Behavior Interactions: - Situational Influence: Environmental conditions facilitate or constrain behavioral options - Behavioral Consequences: Behaviors modify environmental conditions through their effects - Social Reactions: Behavioral performance elicits social responses that shape future behavior - Cultural Evolution: Collective behaviors contribute to cultural and environmental change

    Intervention Effectiveness¶

    Single Component Interventions: - Self-Efficacy Only: Modest behavior change effects (d = .30-.50) - Modeling Only: Moderate effects particularly for skill acquisition (d = .40-.60) - Goal Setting Only: Small to moderate effects (d = .25-.45) - Environmental Only: Variable effects depending on barrier/facilitator modification

    Multi-Component SCT Interventions: - Combined Approaches: Larger effect sizes (d = .50-.80) when multiple components included - Self-Efficacy + Skills: Particularly effective combination for behavior change - Modeling + Practice: Strong effects for complex behavior acquisition - Comprehensive Programs: Best outcomes when addressing personal, environmental, and behavioral factors

    Maintenance and Generalization: - Long-term Effects: SCT interventions show good maintenance at 6-12 month follow-up - Generalization: Transfer to related behaviors when self-efficacy beliefs generalize - Booster Sessions: Periodic reinforcement enhances long-term maintenance - Environmental Support: Sustained environmental changes predict better maintenance

    9) Applications¶

    Health Behavior Change¶

    Chronic Disease Management: - Self-Efficacy Building: Graduated task assignments to build confidence in disease management - Modeling Programs: Peer mentors and video models demonstrating successful management - Goal Setting: Collaborative development of specific, achievable health goals - Self-Monitoring: Technology-supported tracking of symptoms, medications, behaviors

    Prevention Programs: - Risk Reduction: Building self-efficacy for healthy behaviors (exercise, nutrition, safety) - Skill Development: Teaching specific behavioral skills through modeling and practice - Environmental Modification: Creating supportive environments for healthy choices - Social Support: Enhancing social networks to support behavior change

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Social Cognitive Theory Key Differences
    Theory of Planned Behavior Intentions and behavioral prediction Both emphasize cognitive factors but SCT includes observational learning TPB focuses on intention formation; SCT on learning and self-regulation
    Health Belief Model Health-specific behavior prediction Both address self-efficacy but SCT has broader scope HBM health-specific; SCT general behavioral theory
    Self-Determination Theory Intrinsic motivation and autonomy Both emphasize personal agency but different motivational mechanisms SDT focuses on intrinsic motivation; SCT on self-efficacy and learning
    Transtheoretical Model Stages of behavior change SCT principles apply across TTM stages TTM stage-focused; SCT addresses mechanisms of change

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Complexity and Breadth: Critics argue that SCT is too broad and complex, making it difficult to test comprehensively and apply systematically in practical interventions

    Measurement Challenges: The theory's emphasis on specific self-efficacy measures requires developing new instruments for each behavior and context, creating measurement burden

    Reciprocal Determinism Complexity: The bidirectional relationships between person, environment, and behavior are difficult to disentangle and test empirically

    Cultural Generalizability: Theory developed primarily in Western, individualistic contexts may not adequately address collectivistic cultures' different views of agency and control

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Technology and Digital Behavior: - Virtual Self-Efficacy: How do virtual achievements and online mastery experiences translate to real-world confidence? - Digital Modeling: Effectiveness of AI-generated models versus human models for behavioral learning - Screen Time Effects: Impact of digital media consumption on observational learning and self-regulation - Social Media Influence: How do social media platforms alter traditional modeling and social comparison processes?

    Neuroscience Integration: - Brain Mechanisms: Neurological basis of self-efficacy beliefs and observational learning - Developmental Neuroscience: How brain development affects self-regulatory capabilities across lifespan - Individual Differences: Genetic and neurological factors influencing self-efficacy and modeling susceptibility - Intervention Mechanisms: Neural changes accompanying SCT-based behavioral interventions

    Cultural and Contextual Considerations: - Collective Efficacy: How do collective cultures modify individual self-efficacy concepts? - Cultural Modeling: Different cultural models and their effectiveness across populations - Contextual Self-Efficacy: How do specific contexts (work, family, community) shape efficacy beliefs? - Global Applications: Adapting SCT interventions for diverse cultural and economic contexts

    Methodological Issues¶

    Self-Efficacy Measurement: - Task Specificity: Balancing specificity with practical measurement constraints - Temporal Stability: How quickly self-efficacy beliefs change and implications for measurement timing - Social Desirability: Potential bias in self-reported confidence measures - Cultural Equivalence: Ensuring measures work across different cultural populations

    Reciprocal Determinism Testing: - Causal Inference: Establishing causal relationships in bidirectional influence models - Temporal Dynamics: Capturing real-time interactions between person, environment, and behavior - Complexity Management: Studying all three factors simultaneously without oversimplification - Longitudinal Challenges: Maintaining participants and measures over extended time periods

    Theoretical Extensions¶

    Collective Social Cognitive Theory: Extension to group-level phenomena and collective efficacy

    Digital Social Cognitive Theory: Adaptations for virtual environments and digital behavior change

    Developmental Social Cognitive Theory: Life-span approaches to self-efficacy and observational learning

    Cultural Social Cognitive Theory: Frameworks incorporating diverse cultural concepts of agency and learning

    Sample Items: 1. "I can always manage to solve difficult problems if I try hard enough" (1-4 not at all true to exactly true) 2. "I can solve most problems if I invest the necessary effort" (1-4)

    Self-Efficacy for Exercise Scale (Resnick & Jenkins, 2000)¶

    Citation: Resnick, B., & Jenkins, L. S. (2000). Testing the reliability and validity of the self-efficacy for exercise scale. Nursing Research, 49(3), 154-159. Reliability: α = .92

    Sample Items: "How confident are you that you can exercise when:" 1. "You are feeling tired" (0-10 not confident to very confident) 2. "You are feeling stressed" (0-10) 3. "You have to exercise alone" (0-10) 4. "It is raining or snowing" (0-10)

    Teacher Self-Efficacy Scale (Tschannen-Moran & Hoy, 2001)¶

    Citation: Tschannen-Moran, M., & Hoy, A. W. (2001). Teacher efficacy: Capturing an elusive construct. Teaching and Teacher Education, 17(7), 783-805. Reliability: α = .94

    Sample Items: "How much can you do to:" 1. "Get through to the most difficult students?" (1-9 nothing to a great deal) 2. "Help your students think critically?" (1-9) 3. "Motivate students who show low interest in school work?" (1-9) 4. "Get students to believe they can do well in school work?" (1-9)

    Self-Regulation Questionnaire (Brown et al., 1999)¶

    Citation: Brown, J. M., Miller, W. R., & Lawendowski, L. A. (1999). The self-regulation questionnaire. In L. VandeCreek & T. L. Jackson (Eds.), Innovations in clinical practice (Vol. 17, pp. 281-289). Professional Resource Press. Reliability: α = .91

    Sample Items: 1. "I set goals for myself and keep track of my progress" (1-5 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "I have trouble making up my mind about things" (1-5, reverse coded) 3. "I get easily distracted from my plans" (1-5, reverse coded) 4. "I notice changes in my moods" (1-5)

import numpy as np

def analyze_social_cognitive_theory_data(study_data):
    """
    Comprehensive analysis of Social Cognitive Theory constructs and relationships

    Parameters:
    study_data (DataFrame): Data with SCT measures and behavioral outcomes

    Returns:
    dict: Complete analysis results including construct relationships and predictions
    """

    # Calculate composite scores for SCT constructs
    self_efficacy_items = ['se1', 'se2', 'se3', 'se4', 'se5']
    outcome_exp_items = ['oe_physical1', 'oe_physical2', 'oe_social1', 'oe_social2', 'oe_self1', 'oe_self2']
    self_reg_items = ['sr_goals', 'sr_monitor', 'sr_evaluate', 'sr_adjust']

    available_se = [col for col in self_efficacy_items if col in study_data.columns]
    available_oe = [col for col in outcome_exp_items if col in study_data.columns]
    available_sr = [col for col in self_reg_items if col in study_data.columns]

    if available_se:
        study_data['self_efficacy'] = study_data[available_se].mean(axis=1)
    if available_oe:
        study_data['outcome_expectations'] = study_data[available_oe].mean(axis=1)
    if available_sr:
        study_data['self_regulation'] = study_data[available_sr].mean(axis=1)

    analysis_results = {
        'sample_size': len(study_data),
        'descriptive_stats': {
            'self_efficacy_mean': study_data.get('self_efficacy', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean(),
            'outcome_expectations_mean': study_data.get('outcome_expectations', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean(),
            'self_regulation_mean': study_data.get('self_regulation', pd.Series([np.nan])).mean()
        }
    }

    # Self-efficacy behavior relationship (core SCT prediction)
    if 'self_efficacy' in study_data.columns and 'behavior_performance' in study_data.columns:
        se_behavior_corr, se_p_value = stats.pearsonr(
            study_data['self_efficacy'].dropna(),
            study_data['behavior_performance'].dropna()
        )

        analysis_results['self_efficacy_effects'] = {
            'behavior_correlation': se_behavior_corr,
            'p_value': se_p_value,
            'effect_size': 'small' if abs(se_behavior_corr) < 0.3 else 'medium' if abs(se_behavior_corr) < 0.5 else 'large'
        }

    # Multiple regression: SCT constructs predicting behavior
    sct_predictors = ['self_efficacy', 'outcome_expectations', 'self_regulation']
    available_predictors = [var for var in sct_predictors if var in study_data.columns]

    if len(available_predictors) >= 2 and 'behavior_performance' in study_data.columns:
        X = study_data[available_predictors].fillna(study_data[available_predictors].mean())
        y = study_data['behavior_performance'].fillna(study_data['behavior_performance'].mean())

        # Standardize predictors for coefficient comparison
        scaler = StandardScaler()
        X_scaled = scaler.fit_transform(X)
        X_scaled_df = pd.DataFrame(X_scaled, columns=available_predictors)

        model = LinearRegression().fit(X_scaled_df, y)
        r_squared = model.score(X_scaled_df, y)
        coefficients = dict(zip(available_predictors, model.coef_))

        analysis_results['sct_model'] = {
            'r_squared': r_squared,
            'standardized_coefficients': coefficients,
            'intercept': model.intercept_,
            'strongest_predictor': max(coefficients.keys(), key=lambda x: abs(coefficients[x]))
        }

    return analysis_results
