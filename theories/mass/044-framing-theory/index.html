    1) Executive Summary¶

    Framing Theory explains how the presentation of information influences audience interpretation by emphasizing certain aspects of reality while de-emphasizing others, fundamentally shaping how people understand and respond to issues. Originally developed by sociologist Erving Goffman (1974) and refined for communication research by Robert Entman (1993), the theory focuses on selective emphasis and interpretation frameworks that guide cognitive processing. Frames are organizing principles that define problems, diagnose causes, make moral evaluations, and suggest remedies through the strategic selection and salience of information elements. Frame construction involves journalists, politicians, and communicators making choices about what information to include, exclude, and emphasize when presenting stories or arguments. Frame effects occur when these presentation choices systematically influence audience interpretation, causal attribution, and opinion formation. The theory distinguishes between episodic frames that focus on individual cases and events, and thematic frames that emphasize broader patterns and systemic issues. Equivalency framing studies how mathematically equivalent information presented differently affects decisions, while emphasis framing examines how highlighting different aspects changes interpretation. Key mechanisms include accessibility (making certain considerations more mentally available), applicability (influencing which considerations are relevant), and interpretation (shaping how information is understood). Framing effects are strongest when audiences lack strong prior attitudes, have limited knowledge, or face ambiguous situations. The theory has demonstrated robust empirical support across diverse domains including political communication, health messaging, crisis communication, and social movement mobilization. Modern applications address digital framing, algorithmic presentation, visual framing, and interactive media effects where user engagement shapes frame exposure and processing.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: A theory explaining how the presentation and organization of information influences audience interpretation and understanding by selectively emphasizing certain aspects of reality while de-emphasizing others, shaping cognitive processing through interpretive frameworks.
    • Scope: All communication contexts where message presentation affects interpretation, including news media, political discourse, health communication, advertising, organizational communication, social media, and interpersonal conversation.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Frame Definition and Components¶

    Entman's Frame Functions¶

    Problem Definition: - Issue Identification: Determining what counts as a problem requiring attention - Boundary Setting: Defining the scope and limits of the issue - Stakeholder Identification: Specifying who is affected by the problem - Severity Assessment: Indicating the magnitude and urgency of the issue

    Causal Interpretation: - Cause Attribution: Identifying factors responsible for the problem - Causal Chains: Explaining how causes lead to effects - Multiple Causation: Acknowledging complex, interconnected causes - Temporal Sequencing: Ordering events to establish causal relationships

    Moral Evaluation: - Value Judgments: Applying moral standards to evaluate the situation - Responsibility Assignment: Determining who bears moral or legal responsibility - Victim/Perpetrator Roles: Defining moral characters in the narrative - Ethical Implications: Highlighting moral dimensions and consequences

    Treatment Recommendation: - Solution Proposals: Suggesting specific remedies or responses - Action Justification: Providing rationales for recommended treatments - Implementation Guidelines: Specifying how solutions should be enacted - Outcome Predictions: Forecasting results of proposed treatments

    Frame Elements and Devices¶

    Textual Elements: - Keywords and Phrases: Specific language choices that carry interpretive weight - Metaphors and Analogies: Comparative devices that shape understanding - Catchphrases and Slogans: Memorable expressions that encapsulate frame meaning - Exemplars and Cases: Specific examples that represent broader patterns

    Visual Elements: - Images and Graphics: Visual representations that support frame interpretation - Charts and Data Visualization: Statistical presentations that emphasize certain patterns - Symbolic Imagery: Visual symbols that evoke emotional and cognitive responses - Layout and Design: Spatial organization that guides attention and emphasis

    Narrative Elements: - Story Structure: Beginning, middle, and end that organize information - Character Development: Portrayal of actors and their motivations - Plot Progression: Sequence of events that creates meaning - Dramatic Elements: Tension, conflict, and resolution that engage audiences

    Types of Frames¶

    Generic vs. Issue-Specific Frames¶

    Generic Frames: - Conflict Frame: Emphasizing disagreement, opposition, and winners/losers - Economic Consequences Frame: Focusing on financial costs and benefits - Human Interest Frame: Highlighting personal stories and emotional impacts - Morality Frame: Emphasizing ethical principles and moral considerations - Responsibility Frame: Focusing on accountability and causal attribution

    Issue-Specific Frames: - Policy Frames: Specific to particular policy domains and debates - Event Frames: Tailored to specific incidents or occurrences - Campaign Frames: Designed for particular political campaigns or candidates - Crisis Frames: Specialized for emergency and crisis situations - Health Frames: Adapted for health-related issues and behaviors

    Episodic vs. Thematic Framing¶

    Episodic Framing: - Individual Focus: Concentration on specific people and particular incidents - Concrete Examples: Use of detailed, specific cases to illustrate issues - Personal Narrative: Emphasis on individual experiences and stories - Event-Centered: Organization around specific occurrences and their details - Contextual Limitation: Reduced attention to broader patterns and systemic factors

    Thematic Framing: - Systemic Focus: Emphasis on patterns, trends, and institutional factors - Abstract Concepts: Use of general principles and statistical evidence - Structural Analysis: Attention to underlying causes and systemic issues - Policy Context: Connection to broader policy debates and solutions - Causal Complexity: Recognition of multiple, interconnected factors

    Equivalency vs. Emphasis Framing¶

    Equivalency Framing: - Logically Equivalent Information: Same facts presented in different ways - Gain vs. Loss Framing: Highlighting positive outcomes vs. negative consequences - Percentage vs. Frequency: Statistical information in different formats - Absolute vs. Relative Risk: Different presentations of risk information

    Emphasis Framing: - Selective Attention: Highlighting some aspects while downplaying others - Aspect Selection: Choosing which dimensions of an issue to emphasize - Contextual Variation: Different situational factors made salient - Perspective Differences: Alternative viewpoints and considerations

    Frame Effects Mechanisms¶

    Cognitive Processing Mechanisms¶

    Accessibility: - Mental Availability: Making certain thoughts and considerations more readily available - Priming Effects: Activating related concepts in memory through exposure - Retrieval Enhancement: Improving ability to recall relevant information - Cognitive Shortcuts: Providing mental heuristics for quick decision-making

    Applicability: - Relevance Judgments: Influencing which considerations are deemed relevant - Contextual Matching: Connecting frame elements to personal experience - Situational Appropriateness: Determining which frames fit specific circumstances - Knowledge Integration: Incorporating frame content into existing understanding

    Interpretation: - Meaning Construction: Shaping how information is understood and evaluated - Causal Attribution: Influencing explanations for events and outcomes - Value Application: Connecting information to personal and social values - Evaluative Judgments: Affecting positive or negative assessments

    Psychological Mechanisms¶

    Schema Activation: - Knowledge Structures: Activating organized sets of beliefs and expectations - Pattern Recognition: Helping audiences identify familiar interpretive patterns - Gap Filling: Providing information to complete incomplete mental models - Consistency Seeking: Encouraging interpretation consistent with activated schemas

    Emotional Responses: - Affective Priming: Triggering emotional reactions that influence judgment - Empathy Activation: Encouraging identification with particular characters or groups - Fear Appeals: Using threat perception to motivate attention and response - Hope and Efficacy: Creating positive emotions that encourage engagement

    Motivation and Engagement: - Personal Relevance: Connecting issues to individual concerns and interests - Efficacy Beliefs: Affecting perceptions of ability to understand or act - Issue Importance: Influencing how much attention people pay to issues - Response Intentions: Shaping likelihood of behavioral and attitudinal responses

    Frame Building and Setting¶

    Frame Building Process¶

    Source Influences: - Official Sources: Government officials, institutional representatives - Expert Sources: Academics, professionals, technical specialists - Interest Groups: Advocacy organizations, lobbyists, activists - Opposition Sources: Critics, alternative viewpoints, competing interests

    Journalist Factors: - Professional Norms: Journalistic values like objectivity, balance, newsworthiness - Organizational Constraints: Editorial policies, resource limitations, deadline pressures - Personal Characteristics: Individual backgrounds, beliefs, and experiences - Competitive Pressures: Need to differentiate from other news organizations

    Contextual Factors: - Cultural Context: Broader cultural values and interpretive frameworks - Political Environment: Current political climate and partisan considerations - Economic Conditions: Financial pressures and market considerations - Historical Precedents: Past events and established interpretive patterns

    Frame Setting Process¶

    Media Presentation: - Story Placement: Position and prominence in media outlet - Repetition Frequency: How often frame appears across stories and time - Source Credibility: Perceived trustworthiness of frame sponsors - Multimedia Integration: Combination of text, images, and audio elements

    Audience Reception: - Attention Allocation: How much cognitive resources audiences devote - Processing Depth: Level of careful consideration versus superficial processing - Prior Knowledge: Existing information and beliefs about the issue - Motivational Factors: Personal interest, involvement, and relevance

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Frames organize experience by selecting and highlighting certain aspects of reality while downplaying others

    P2: Frame effects occur through accessibility, applicability, and interpretation mechanisms that shape cognitive processing

    P3: Episodic frames encourage individual attribution while thematic frames promote systemic attribution

    P4: Frame effects are strongest when audiences have weak prior attitudes and limited issue knowledge

    P5: Competing frames can neutralize each other's effects when presented simultaneously

    P6: Visual and narrative elements enhance frame power beyond textual content alone

    P7: Frame effects persist over time but can be modified by subsequent exposure to alternative frames

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Selective Attention: People cannot process all available information simultaneously and must rely on selective attention
    • Cognitive Limitations: Human information processing capacity is limited, making interpretive shortcuts necessary
    • Schema Dependence: Understanding requires connecting new information to existing knowledge structures
    • Multiple Interpretations: Most issues can be framed in multiple, potentially contradictory ways
    • Frame Ubiquity: All communication involves some form of framing; neutral presentation is impossible

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Strong Prior Attitudes: Frame effects reduced when audiences have well-developed, strongly held opinions
    • High Knowledge Levels: Expert audiences less susceptible to frame effects due to extensive domain knowledge
    • Motivated Reasoning: Strong motivational factors can override frame effects through biased processing
    • Multiple Frame Exposure: Simultaneous exposure to competing frames can neutralize individual frame effects
    • Cultural Misalignment: Frames that conflict with deep cultural values may be rejected or reinterpreted
    • Extreme Positions: Frames promoting highly controversial or extreme positions may trigger counterargument

    Construct Typical Operationalization Example Items Measurement Notes

    Frame Acceptance Survey measures of frame agreement "This story accurately represents the issue" (1-7) Measure both explicit and implicit acceptance
    Problem Attribution Causal responsibility ratings "Rate responsibility: individual vs. societal factors" (1-10) Distinguish between causal and treatment responsibility
    Issue Interpretation Open-ended and closed-ended responses "What is the main problem described?" Analyze both structured and spontaneous responses
    Policy Preferences Support ratings for policy alternatives "Rate support for proposed solutions" (1-7) Include multiple policy options
    Emotional Responses Affect scales and physiological measures "How angry/sympathetic does this make you feel?" (1-10) Consider both self-report and objective measures

    7) Research Designs & Analysis¶

    Experimental Frame Effects Study¶

    Classic Framing Experiment: - Design: Between-subjects experimental design manipulating frame presentation - Participants: Representative sample stratified by demographics and political orientation - Manipulation: Random assignment to different frame conditions of same issue - Control: Identical factual content presented with different emphasis and organization - Measures: Pre-post attitude measures, attribution assessments, policy preferences

    Procedure: 1. Baseline Assessment: Pre-manipulation attitudes, knowledge, and demographic characteristics 2. Frame Exposure: Randomly assigned news story or message with specific frame emphasis 3. Attention Check: Verification that participants processed frame content 4. Immediate Effects: Post-exposure measures of attitudes, attributions, and preferences 5. Delayed Assessment: Follow-up measures after 1-2 weeks to assess persistence 6. Debriefing: Full disclosure of experimental purposes and frame variations

    Content Analysis of Frame Patterns¶

    Longitudinal Frame Analysis: - Sample: Systematic sampling of news coverage over extended time period - Units: Individual news stories, editorials, headlines, and visual elements - Coding Scheme: Standardized categories for frame identification and measurement - Reliability: Multiple coders with inter-coder reliability assessment (κ > .80) - Analysis: Temporal patterns, source influences, and frame competition dynamics

    Frame Coding Categories: 

    Primary Frame: Dominant organizing principle (conflict, economic, human interest, etc.)
Frame Elements: Problem definition, causation, evaluation, recommendation
Frame Devices: Keywords, metaphors, examples, images, sources cited
Frame Sponsors: Sources promoting specific interpretive frameworks
Frame Contest: Presence and interaction of competing frames

    Survey-Based Frame Reception Study¶

    Audience Frame Processing Research: - Design: Cross-sectional survey with embedded framing manipulations - Sample: Population-representative sample with oversampling of key demographic groups - Method: Online survey with randomized frame exposure and comprehensive outcome measures - Analysis: Structural equation modeling examining frame processing pathways

    Key Variables: - Individual Differences: Prior knowledge, political orientation, cognitive motivation - Frame Processing: Accessibility, applicability, interpretation mechanisms - Outcomes: Issue attitudes, causal attribution, policy support, behavioral intentions - Moderators: Education, partisan strength, issue involvement, media consumption patterns

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Core Frame Effects¶

    Episodic vs. Thematic Attribution: - Individual Attribution: Episodic frames increase attribution to personal characteristics by 40-60% - Systemic Attribution: Thematic frames increase attribution to social/political factors by 35-50% - Policy Implications: Episodic framing reduces support for social programs by 20-30% - Responsibility Assignment: Thematic frames increase government responsibility attributions by 45%

    Equivalency Framing Effects: - Gain-Loss Asymmetry: Loss frames typically 1.5-2x more influential than equivalent gain frames - Risk Perception: Frequency formats increase perceived risk compared to percentage formats - Decision Context: Risk-seeking increases under loss frames, risk aversion under gain frames - Temporal Persistence: Loss frame effects maintain strength longer than gain frame effects

    Emphasis Framing Patterns: - Selective Attention: Emphasized frame elements receive 2-3x more cognitive processing - Memory Enhancement: Framed information shows 25-40% better recall after one week - Attitude Polarization: Strong frames can increase attitude extremity by 15-25% - Issue Importance: Frame emphasis increases perceived issue importance by 30-50%

    Individual Difference Moderators¶

    Prior Knowledge Effects: - Low Knowledge: Strong frame effects (d = .60-.80) across most outcome measures - Moderate Knowledge: Moderate frame effects (d = .30-.50) with selective influence - High Knowledge: Weak frame effects (d = .10-.30) limited to novel frame aspects - Expert Knowledge: Minimal frame effects (d < .20) except for value-laden interpretations

    Political Orientation Interactions: - Partisan Congruence: Frames matching political orientation show enhanced effects - Cross-Cutting Frames: Frames challenging partisan views often rejected or reinterpreted - Independent/Moderate: Greatest susceptibility to frame effects across all conditions - Strong Partisans: Frame effects primarily limited to non-political outcome domains

    Cognitive Motivation Patterns: - High Elaboration: Reduced frame effects due to systematic processing and counterargument - Low Elaboration: Maximum frame effects through peripheral processing routes - Motivated Reasoning: Frame effects moderated by directional processing goals - Accuracy Motivation: Frame effects reduced when accuracy goals are prominent

    Media and Message Characteristics¶

    Source Credibility Effects: - High Credibility: Frame effects enhanced by 25-40% compared to low credibility sources - Expert Sources: Particularly effective for technical and scientific frame content - In-group Sources: Stronger frame effects when sources share audience characteristics - Multiple Sources: Convergent framing across sources increases effect sizes significantly

    Visual Framing Impact: - Image-Text Congruence: Aligned visual and textual frames show additive effects - Emotional Imagery: Visual frames with emotional content increase effect sizes by 30-50% - Symbolic Visuals: Abstract visual symbols can carry powerful framing effects - Attention Direction: Visual elements guide attention to specific textual frame content

    Repetition and Timing: - Frequency Effects: Frame effects increase with repetition up to saturation point - Timing Intervals: Spaced repetition more effective than massed exposure - Recency Effects: Recent frame exposure typically dominates when multiple frames present - Decay Patterns: Frame effects decline exponentially without reinforcement

    Digital Media Frame Effects¶

    Social Media Framing: - Algorithmic Amplification: Platform algorithms can amplify certain frames while suppressing others - User-Generated Frames: Peer-created frames often more persuasive than institutional frames - Viral Frame Dynamics: Emotionally charged frames spread faster and wider - Echo Chamber Effects: Personalized feeds can reinforce frame bubbles

    Interactive Frame Processing: - User Engagement: Interactive elements increase frame processing depth and effects - Personalization: Customized frame presentation increases relevance and impact - Real-time Feedback: Immediate response metrics influence frame evolution - Multimodal Integration: Combining text, audio, video, and interactive elements enhances framing

    9) Applications¶

    Political Communication¶

    Campaign Framing Strategies: - Candidate Positioning: Framing candidate characteristics and qualifications advantageously - Issue Ownership: Establishing frame dominance on key political issues - Opposition Research: Counter-framing opponent positions and characteristics - Debate Preparation: Anticipating and responding to alternative frame attacks

    Policy Advocacy: - Problem Construction: Framing social issues to highlight need for policy intervention - Solution Framing: Presenting policy proposals in most persuasive light - Coalition Building: Creating frames that unite diverse stakeholder groups - Public Support: Generating citizen support through strategic frame presentation

    Health Communication¶

    Risk Communication: - Risk Magnitude: Presenting health risks in ways that promote appropriate response - Behavioral Motivation: Framing health behaviors to encourage adoption and maintenance - Treatment Compliance: Presenting medical recommendations to maximize adherence - Prevention Campaigns: Framing prevention messages for maximum public health impact

    Crisis Health Communication: - Emergency Response: Rapid frame deployment during health emergencies - Trust Building: Framing official communications to maintain public confidence - Behavior Change: Emergency behavior modification through strategic framing - Misinformation Counter: Competing with false frames through effective counter-framing

    Organizational Communication¶

    Corporate Communications: - Reputation Management: Framing organizational actions and characteristics positively - Crisis Response: Strategic framing during organizational crises and scandals - Change Management: Framing organizational changes to reduce resistance - Stakeholder Relations: Tailored framing for different stakeholder groups

    Internal Communications: - Employee Engagement: Framing work and organizational goals to motivate employees - Performance Management: Constructive framing of feedback and evaluation - Culture Change: Framing cultural transformation initiatives effectively - Leadership Communication: Executive framing of vision, strategy, and direction

    Journalism and Media¶

    News Framing Practices: - Story Development: Constructing news narratives with appropriate frame emphasis - Source Integration: Balancing multiple source frames in news reporting - Audience Consideration: Adapting frames for target audience understanding - Ethical Framing: Maintaining journalistic integrity while acknowledging frame choices

    Editorial Decision-Making: - Frame Diversity: Ensuring multiple perspectives in coverage decisions - Frame Transparency: Acknowledging framing choices in editorial content - Frame Accountability: Responsibility for frame effects on public understanding - Frame Innovation: Developing new frames for emerging issues and topics

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Framing Theory Key Differences
    Agenda-Setting Theory Issue salience and importance Framing determines how agenda items are interpreted Agenda-setting: what to think about; Framing: how to think about it
    Priming Theory Cognitive accessibility of considerations Framing can prime specific considerations for evaluation Priming emphasizes activation; Framing emphasizes interpretation
    Schema Theory Organized knowledge structures Frames activate and modify cognitive schemas Schema theory focuses on structure; Framing on strategic activation
    Cognitive Dissonance Theory Consistency motivation Frame effects can create or resolve cognitive dissonance Dissonance focuses on consistency; Framing on interpretation

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Conceptual Fragmentation: Critics argue that framing research lacks theoretical coherence, with different researchers using "frame" to mean fundamentally different concepts, making cumulative knowledge building difficult

    Methodological Inconsistencies: Substantial variation in operational definitions and measurement approaches across studies makes comparison and meta-analysis challenging

    Effect Size Concerns: Some researchers question whether frame effects are large enough to matter practically, especially given competing influences on public opinion

    Ecological Validity: Laboratory experiments may not capture real-world frame processing where people encounter multiple, competing frames simultaneously

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Era Transformations: - Algorithmic Mediation: How do recommendation algorithms affect frame exposure and processing? - User Agency: Greater user control over information consumption may reduce frame effects - Micro-Targeting: Personalized frame delivery may increase effectiveness but raise ethical concerns - Real-time Adaptation: Dynamic frame adjustment based on user response data

    Frame Neutrality and Bias: - Journalist Objectivity: Whether neutral framing is possible or if all framing involves bias - Democratic Implications: Concern that strategic framing manipulates public opinion - Frame Responsibility: Who bears responsibility for frame effects on public understanding - Counter-Frame Rights: Whether audiences deserve exposure to alternative frames

    Cognitive vs. Cultural Approaches: - Individual Processing: Emphasis on psychological mechanisms vs. cultural meaning systems - Universal vs. Cultural: Whether frame effects operate similarly across cultures - Power Relations: Role of social power in determining which frames become dominant - Ideological Functions: How framing serves to maintain or challenge existing power structures

    Methodological Issues¶

    Frame Identification: - Researcher Subjectivity: Potential bias in identifying and categorizing frames - Cultural Specificity: Frames may not translate across cultural contexts - Temporal Stability: Whether frame categories remain consistent over time - Audience Interpretation: Mismatch between intended and received frames

    Effect Measurement: - Demand Characteristics: Experimental subjects may respond to perceived expectations - Short-term vs. Long-term: Most studies measure immediate effects rather than persistence - Behavioral Outcomes: Limited evidence for frame effects on actual behavior vs. attitudes - Context Dependence: Frame effects may vary significantly across real-world contexts

    Theoretical Extensions¶

    Multi-Level Framing: Integrating individual, organizational, and societal levels of frame analysis

    Dynamic Framing Theory: Accounting for frame evolution and adaptation over time

    Intersectional Framing: Considering how multiple identity categories affect frame reception

    Computational Framing: Using artificial intelligence to identify and analyze frame patterns

    Sample Items: 1. "This story emphasizes individual responsibility for the problem" (1-7 strongly disagree to strongly agree) 2. "This story focuses on system-level causes" (1-7) 3. "This story suggests personal solutions are most important" (1-7) 4. "This story highlights broader social patterns" (1-7)

    Issue Interpretation Scale (Iyengar, 1991)¶

    Citation: Iyengar, S. (1991). Is anyone responsible? How television frames political issues. University of Chicago Press. Reliability: α = .85

    Sample Items: "After reading this story, how much responsibility do you assign to:" 1. "The individuals involved in the situation" (1-10 no responsibility to complete responsibility) 2. "Government policies and programs" (1-10) 3. "Social and economic conditions" (1-10) 4. "Cultural factors and values" (1-10)

    Frame Acceptance Scale (Chong & Druckman, 2007)¶

    Citation: Chong, D., & Druckman, J. N. (2007). Framing public opinion in competitive democracies. American Political Science Review, 101(4), 637-655. Reliability: α = .91

    Sample Items: 1. "This story presents a fair and accurate view of the issue" (1-7) 2. "I agree with how this story defines the problem" (1-7) 3. "This story's interpretation makes sense to me" (1-7) 4. "The perspective in this story is convincing" (1-7)

    Causal Attribution Assessment (Nelson et al., 1997)¶

    Citation: Nelson, T. E., Oxley, Z. M., & Clawson, R. A. (1997). Toward a psychology of framing effects. Political Behavior, 19(3), 221-246. Reliability: α = .88

    Sample Items: "What factors are most responsible for this problem?" 1. "Individual choices and behaviors" (1-9 not at all responsible to completely responsible) 2. "Institutional policies and procedures" (1-9) 3. "Economic forces and market conditions" (1-9) 4. "Cultural and social norms" (1-9)

    Emotional Response to Frames Scale (Gross, 2008)¶

    Citation: Gross, K. (2008). Framing persuasive appeals: Episodic and thematic framing, emotional response, and policy opinion. Political Psychology, 29(2), 169-192. Reliability: α = .93

    Sample Items: "How does this story make you feel?" 1. "Angry" (1-7 not at all to extremely) 2. "Sympathetic" (1-7) 3. "Hopeful" (1-7) 4. "Frustrated" (1-7)

    def analyze_frame_effects_data(experiment_data): """ Comprehensive analysis of framing experiment results

    Parameters:
experiment_data (DataFrame): Data with frame conditions and outcome measures

Returns:
dict: Analysis results including frame effects and moderators
"""

# Basic descriptive statistics
analysis_results = {
    'sample_size': len(experiment_data),
    'frame_conditions': experiment_data['frame_condition'].value_counts().to_dict()
}

# Main frame effects analysis
if 'frame_condition' in experiment_data.columns and 'attitude_change' in experiment_data.columns:
    frame_groups = experiment_data.groupby('frame_condition')['attitude_change']

    # ANOVA for frame condition effects
    frame_conditions = experiment_data['frame_condition'].unique()
    group_data = [experiment_data[experiment_data['frame_condition'] == condition]['attitude_change'].dropna() 
                 for condition in frame_conditions]

    f_stat, p_value = stats.f_oneway(*group_data)

    analysis_results['main_frame_effects'] = {
        'f_statistic': f_stat,
        'p_value': p_value,
        'group_means': frame_groups.mean().to_dict(),
        'group_sizes': frame_groups.count().to_dict()
    }

# Attribution analysis (episodic vs. thematic)
if 'individual_attribution' in experiment_data.columns and 'systemic_attribution' in experiment_data.columns:
    # Compare attribution patterns across frame conditions
    attribution_analysis = experiment_data.groupby('frame_condition').agg({
        'individual_attribution': ['mean', 'std'],
        'systemic_attribution': ['mean', 'std']
    })

    analysis_results['attribution_patterns'] = attribution_analysis.to_dict()

# Moderator analysis - prior knowledge
if 'prior_knowledge' in experiment_data.columns:
    # Split by knowledge level
    experiment_data['knowledge_level'] = pd.cut(experiment_data['prior_knowledge'], 
                                               bins=3, labels=['Low', 'Medium', 'High'])

    knowledge_interaction = experiment_data.groupby(['frame_condition', 'knowledge_level'])['attitude_change'].mean()
    analysis_results['knowledge_moderation'] = knowledge_interaction.to_dict()

# Emotional response analysis
emotion_cols = ['anger', 'sympathy', 'hope', 'frustration']
available_emotions = [col for col in emotion_cols if col in experiment_data.columns]

if available_emotions:
    emotion_effects = experiment_data.groupby('frame_condition')[available_emotions].mean()
    analysis_results['emotional_responses'] = emotion_effects.to_dict()

# Frame acceptance and processing
if 'frame_acceptance' in experiment_data.columns:
    acceptance_by_frame = experiment_data.groupby('frame_condition')['frame_acceptance'].agg(['mean', 'std'])
    analysis_results['frame_acceptance'] = acceptance_by_frame.to_dict()

return analysis_results
