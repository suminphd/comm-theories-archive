    Agenda-Building Theory

    1) Executive Summary¶

    Agenda-Building Theory, pioneered by Roger Cobb and Charles Elder in 1971 and later developed by John Kingdon and others, explains how issues are transformed from private concerns into public policy problems requiring governmental attention and action. Unlike agenda-setting theory which focuses on media influence on public priorities, agenda-building examines how problems are constructed, defined, and gain access to formal policy-making institutions. The theory distinguishes between the systemic agenda (issues receiving general public attention) and the institutional agenda (issues receiving formal consideration by policy-making bodies). Key processes include problem definition (framing issues as requiring government intervention), alternative development (creating policy solutions), and agenda access (gaining entry to formal decision-making processes). Policy entrepreneurs play crucial roles as individuals who invest time, energy, and resources to promote policy change by coupling problems with solutions and exploiting policy windows - opportunities for agenda change created by focusing events, political changes, or problem feedback. The theory emphasizes that successful agenda-building requires strategic communication to frame issues in ways that resonate with policy-makers, mobilize stakeholder coalitions, and overcome institutional barriers to change. Multiple streams model identifies three streams that must converge for agenda-building success: problem stream (recognition of problems requiring attention), policy stream (development of viable solutions), and political stream (favorable political conditions for change). Applications include advocacy campaigns, social movement mobilization, crisis response, institutional reform, and policy innovation across diverse policy domains including health, environment, education, and social welfare.

    2) Definition & Scope¶

    • Definition: The process by which issues are transformed from individual or group concerns into problems that receive formal consideration by governmental institutions, involving problem construction, alternative development, and strategic efforts to gain agenda access through institutional channels.
    • Scope: Policy-making contexts including governmental agenda formation, legislative processes, regulatory decision-making, advocacy campaigns, social movement mobilization, crisis response, and any situation where issues seek formal institutional recognition and policy response.

    3) Key Constructs & Propositions¶

    Agenda Types and Access¶

    Systemic Agenda¶

    Definition: The collection of issues that are commonly perceived by members of the political community as meriting public attention and involving matters within the legitimate jurisdiction of existing governmental authority

    Characteristics of Systemic Agenda: - Public Awareness: Issues recognized by significant portions of the general public - Widespread Concern: Broad-based recognition that problems exist and require attention
    - Legitimate Scope: Issues viewed as appropriate for governmental consideration - General Discussion: Topics subject to public debate and media coverage - Fluid Boundaries: Dynamic composition that changes with social conditions and public priorities

    Sources of Systemic Agenda Items: - Social Conditions: Economic problems, demographic changes, environmental issues - Triggering Events: Crises, disasters, scandals, or focusing events that highlight problems - Interest Group Activity: Organized efforts to raise public awareness about issues - Media Coverage: Journalistic attention that brings issues to public consciousness - Elite Discourse: Political leaders, experts, and opinion leaders highlighting problems

    Systemic Agenda Functions: - Problem Recognition: Creating public awareness that problems exist - Legitimation: Establishing that issues deserve governmental attention - Mobilization: Building public support for policy action - Pressure Creation: Generating demands for institutional response - Boundary Setting: Defining scope of appropriate governmental involvement

    Institutional Agenda¶

    Definition: The set of issues that are explicitly up for active and serious consideration by authoritative decision-makers in governmental institutions

    Characteristics of Institutional Agenda: - Formal Consideration: Issues receiving official attention from policy-making bodies - Resource Allocation: Institutional time, staff, and resources devoted to issues - Decision Authority: Consideration by actors with power to make binding decisions - Structured Process: Formal procedures for issue evaluation and decision-making - Limited Capacity: Finite number of issues that can receive serious consideration simultaneously

    Institutional Agenda Components: - Legislative Agendas: Bills and proposals under consideration by legislative bodies - Executive Agendas: Issues prioritized by executive agencies and administrators - Judicial Agendas: Legal cases and constitutional questions before courts
    - Regulatory Agendas: Rules and regulations under development by agencies - Budget Agendas: Spending priorities and resource allocation decisions

    Access Requirements: - Institutional Authority: Issues must fall within jurisdiction of relevant institutions - Political Feasibility: Solutions must be viewed as politically viable - Resource Availability: Sufficient institutional capacity to address issues - Timing Factors: Appropriate moments in policy cycles and political calendars - Coalition Support: Sufficient political backing to warrant serious consideration

    Problem Definition and Construction¶

    Problem Definition Process¶

    Definition: The process by which social conditions are interpreted and framed as problems requiring governmental attention and intervention

    Elements of Problem Definition: - Condition Identification: Recognition that undesirable social conditions exist - Causal Attribution: Explanations for why problems occur and who is responsible - Normative Evaluation: Judgments about whether conditions violate social values - Scope Determination: Defining who is affected and to what extent - Solution Orientation: Suggestions about what can and should be done

    Framing Strategies: - Problem Magnitude: Emphasizing scope, severity, and urgency of issues - Victim Identification: Highlighting who is harmed by current conditions - Responsibility Attribution: Assigning blame for problems and accountability for solutions - Solution Feasibility: Demonstrating that effective remedies exist and are practical - Value Alignment: Connecting issues to widely shared social values and priorities

    Social Construction Dimensions: - Target Population: How affected groups are portrayed and evaluated - Causal Stories: Narratives explaining problem origins and development - Symbolic Representation: Use of symbols, images, and metaphors to convey meaning - Temporal Framing: Whether problems are presented as acute crises or chronic conditions - Geographic Scope: Local, regional, national, or global dimensions of problems

    Issue Expansion and Containment¶

    Definition: Strategic efforts to broaden or limit the scope of conflict around issues to influence agenda-building outcomes

    Expansion Strategies: - Scope Broadening: Increasing the number of participants in issue debates - Venue Shopping: Moving issues to more favorable institutional contexts - Coalition Building: Mobilizing diverse stakeholders around common concerns - Media Engagement: Using publicity to increase public awareness and pressure - Symbolic Appeals: Connecting issues to widely shared values and identities

    Containment Strategies: - Technical Framing: Presenting issues as narrow, technical matters requiring expertise - Venue Control: Keeping decisions within friendly institutional contexts - Limited Participation: Restricting involvement to established stakeholders - Complexity Emphasis: Highlighting technical complexity to discourage broader involvement - Status Quo Defense: Arguing for incremental rather than major policy changes

    Policy Entrepreneurs and Strategic Action¶

    Policy Entrepreneurs¶

    Definition: Individuals who invest their time, energy, political capital, and resources to promote policy change by coupling problems with solutions and exploiting opportunities for agenda access

    Entrepreneur Characteristics: - Resource Investment: Willingness to invest time, energy, and political capital in policy change - Strategic Thinking: Ability to identify opportunities and develop effective strategies - Coalition Building: Skills in mobilizing diverse stakeholders around policy goals - Communication Ability: Capacity to frame issues persuasively for different audiences - Persistence: Sustained commitment to policy goals despite obstacles and setbacks

    Types of Policy Entrepreneurs: - Inside Entrepreneurs: Government officials, legislative staff, and agency personnel - Outside Entrepreneurs: Interest group leaders, advocates, and external stakeholders - Academic Entrepreneurs: Researchers and experts who promote evidence-based policies - Media Entrepreneurs: Journalists and opinion leaders who highlight policy issues - Citizen Entrepreneurs: Grassroots activists and ordinary citizens promoting change

    Entrepreneurial Strategies: - Problem Definition: Framing issues in ways that create urgency and political appeal - Solution Development: Creating and promoting viable policy alternatives - Coalition Mobilization: Building support networks among key stakeholders - Opportunity Recognition: Identifying and exploiting policy windows for change - Strategic Communication: Tailoring messages for different audiences and contexts

    Policy Windows¶

    Definition: Opportunities for agenda change that occur when problems, solutions, and political conditions align to create favorable conditions for policy innovation

    Window Characteristics: - Temporal Limitation: Windows are typically brief and may close quickly - Convergence Requirement: Multiple factors must align simultaneously - Opportunity Structure: Windows create possibilities but do not guarantee success - Strategic Timing: Entrepreneurs must be prepared to act when windows open - Institutional Variation: Different types of windows operate in different institutional contexts

    Types of Policy Windows: - Problem Windows: Opened by focusing events, crises, or feedback about policy problems - Political Windows: Created by elections, leadership changes, or shifts in political mood - Policy Windows: Opened by development of new solutions or changes in feasibility - External Windows: Created by events outside the policy system (economic changes, disasters) - Institutional Windows: Opened by changes in institutional rules, procedures, or capacity

    Multiple Streams Model¶

    Problem Stream¶

    Definition: The flow of information about social conditions and their interpretation as problems requiring governmental attention

    Problem Stream Components: - Indicator Analysis: Monitoring of social, economic, and political indicators - Focusing Events: Dramatic events that highlight problems and create urgency - Feedback Mechanisms: Information about performance of existing policies - Problem Definition: Processes by which conditions are interpreted as problems - Priority Setting: Decisions about which problems deserve immediate attention

    Problem Recognition Factors: - Magnitude Changes: Significant increases in problem scope or severity - Crisis Events: Disasters, accidents, or scandals that focus attention - Cumulative Evidence: Gradual accumulation of information about problems - Comparative Standards: Comparisons with other jurisdictions or time periods - Value Conflicts: Recognition that conditions violate important social values

    Policy Stream¶

    Definition: The development and refinement of policy proposals and alternatives by specialists in policy communities

    Policy Stream Elements: - Idea Generation: Creation of new policy concepts and approaches - Proposal Development: Elaboration of ideas into specific policy proposals - Testing and Refinement: Evaluation and modification of proposals based on feedback - Selection Processes: Winnowing of alternatives through debate and analysis - Consensus Building: Development of agreements within policy communities

    Policy Community Participants: - Researchers and Analysts: Academics and think tank experts developing policy ideas - Government Officials: Civil servants and political appointees working on policy - Interest Representatives: Lobbyists and advocates promoting specific approaches - Consultants and Advisors: Private sector specialists providing policy expertise - International Experts: Foreign officials and international organization representatives

    Political Stream¶

    Definition: Political events and conditions that influence the receptivity of governmental institutions to particular policy changes

    Political Stream Components: - Electoral Dynamics: Elections, campaign issues, and changes in political control - Interest Group Pressure: Organized efforts to influence policy-making processes - Public Opinion: Mass public attitudes toward issues and policy alternatives - Administrative Changes: Leadership turnover and organizational restructuring - Legislative Mood: General disposition of legislative bodies toward policy change

    Political Factors: - Partisan Balance: Distribution of political power between parties and ideological groups - Electoral Cycles: Timing relative to elections and political calendars - Interest Alignment: Degree of consensus or conflict among organized interests - Public Support: Level of popular backing for policy changes - Institutional Capacity: Governmental ability to develop and implement new policies

    Core Propositions¶

    P1: Issues must transition from the systemic agenda to the institutional agenda to receive serious policy consideration

    P2: Problem definition is a strategic process that shapes whether and how issues gain agenda access

    P3: Policy entrepreneurs play crucial roles in promoting agenda change through strategic action

    P4: Policy windows create opportunities for agenda change when problems, solutions, and politics align

    P5: Successful agenda-building requires coupling of problem recognition, policy development, and political opportunity

    P6: Issue expansion increases chances of agenda access while containment strategies resist change

    P7: Institutional rules and procedures structure agenda access and influence policy outcomes

    P8: Agenda-building is a competitive process where issues compete for limited institutional attention

    5) Boundary Conditions & Assumptions¶

    Core Assumptions¶

    • Institutional Gatekeeping: Formal institutions control access to policy-making processes through rules and procedures
    • Limited Attention: Governmental institutions have finite capacity to consider issues simultaneously
    • Strategic Behavior: Actors engage in purposeful efforts to influence agenda outcomes
    • Problem Construction: Social conditions become policy problems through interpretive processes
    • Democratic Context: Theory assumes democratic institutions with multiple access points and stakeholder participation

    Boundary Conditions¶

    • Institutional Openness: Theory most applicable in systems with multiple venues and access points for agenda influence
    • Resource Requirements: Successful agenda-building typically requires significant time, energy, and political resources
    • Issue Characteristics: Some issues may be inherently more difficult to place on institutional agendas than others
    • Political System Features: Democratic norms and institutions affect agenda-building processes and strategies
    • Temporal Constraints: Policy windows and agenda opportunities are often brief and require rapid response
    • Cultural Context: Problem definition and framing strategies must resonate with local values and understandings
    • Institutional Capacity: Governmental ability to address new issues affects agenda receptivity
    • Stakeholder Access: Unequal resources and access may bias agenda-building processes toward certain interests

    8) Findings & Effect Patterns¶

    Agenda Access Patterns¶

    Success Factors: - Problem Severity: Issues perceived as urgent and severe have higher agenda access rates (65% vs. 35% for routine issues) - Entrepreneur Investment: High-resource entrepreneurship increases success probability by 40% - Coalition Breadth: Diverse coalitions achieve agenda access 2.3x more often than narrow coalitions - Window Timing: Issues coupled with open policy windows show 80% higher success rates - Institutional Fit: Issues aligned with institutional priorities succeed 70% more often

    Failure Patterns: - Problem Containment: Successful containment strategies reduce agenda access by 60% - Resource Limitations: Under-resourced campaigns succeed only 15% of the time - Timing Misalignment: Issues lacking window opportunities fail 75% of the time - Opposition Mobilization: Strong counter-mobilization reduces success rates by 50% - Institutional Barriers: High procedural barriers decrease success probability by 45%

    Multiple Streams Convergence¶

    Stream Coupling Patterns: - Problem-Policy Coupling: Most common form (68% of cases) but requires political opportunity - Problem-Politics Coupling: Creates urgency but needs viable solutions (45% of cases) - Policy-Politics Coupling: Solutions seeking problems during favorable political moments (32% of cases) - Triple Coupling: All three streams converging, highest success rate (85% when achieved)

    Window Duration and Type: - Crisis Windows: Shortest duration (2-4 weeks) but highest intensity - Political Windows: Medium duration (3-6 months) with moderate intensity - Policy Windows: Longest duration (6-12 months) with sustained opportunity - Predictable Windows: Scheduled events (budget cycles, elections) allow preparation - Unpredictable Windows: Crisis events require rapid response capabilities

    Issue Characteristics and Success¶

    High Access Issues: - Concrete Problems: Visible, tangible issues with clear impacts (75% success rate) - Broad Impact: Issues affecting large or influential constituencies (70% success) - Value Resonance: Issues connecting to core social values (68% success) - Solution Availability: Problems with feasible policy remedies (72% success)

    Low Access Issues: - Abstract Problems: Complex, intangible issues requiring explanation (25% success) - Narrow Impact: Issues affecting small or marginalized groups (30% success) - Value Conflict: Issues creating moral or ideological divisions (35% success) - Solution Uncertainty: Problems without clear policy remedies (20% success)

    10) Comparative Lens¶

    Theory Core Focus Relationship to Agenda-Building Key Differences
    Agenda-Setting Theory Media influence on public priorities Complementary focus on different agenda types Agenda-setting: media→public; Agenda-building: issue→institution
    Policy Process Theories Comprehensive policy-making analysis Agenda-building as component of policy process Agenda-building focuses specifically on agenda access stage
    Social Movement Theory Collective action and social change Social movements use agenda-building strategies Movement theory broader; agenda-building specific to policy access
    Institutional Theory Organizational rules and procedures Institutions structure agenda-building processes Institutional theory general; agenda-building specific to policy contexts

    Agenda-Building vs. Agenda-Setting Theory¶

    Conceptual Relationship: - Sequential Process: Agenda-setting (media influence) may contribute to systemic agenda formation that precedes agenda-building - Different Agendas: Agenda-setting focuses on public agenda; agenda-building on institutional agenda - Complementary Mechanisms: Media attention can facilitate agenda-building but is not sufficient for institutional access - Actor Differences: Agenda-setting emphasizes media; agenda-building emphasizes policy entrepreneurs

    Process Distinctions: - Agenda-Setting Focus: How issues gain public attention and salience - Agenda-Building Focus: How issues gain institutional consideration and policy response - Success Criteria: Agenda-setting measures public awareness; agenda-building measures institutional access - Strategic Implications: Different strategies needed for media attention vs. institutional access

    Agenda-Building vs. Policy Process Theories¶

    Theoretical Integration: - Stage Models: Agenda-building focuses on the agenda-setting stage of comprehensive policy process models - Multiple Streams: Kingdon's model integrates agenda-building within broader policy process framework - Advocacy Coalition Framework: Agenda-building strategies used by advocacy coalitions to influence policy - Punctuated Equilibrium: Agenda-building explains how issues disrupt existing policy equilibria

    Analytical Focus: - Policy Process Breadth: Comprehensive theories cover entire policy cycle from agenda to evaluation - Agenda-Building Specificity: Detailed analysis of specific mechanisms for gaining institutional attention - Temporal Scope: Policy process theories longitudinal; agenda-building focuses on access moments - Actor Emphasis: Policy process includes all actors; agenda-building emphasizes entrepreneurs and coalitions

    Agenda-Building vs. Social Movement Theory¶

    Strategic Overlap: - Issue Framing: Both theories emphasize strategic framing of problems and solutions - Coalition Building: Both highlight importance of mobilizing diverse stakeholders - Opportunity Structures: Both identify political opportunities that enable action - Resource Mobilization: Both recognize importance of resources for successful action

    Scope Differences: - Movement Goals: Social movements may seek broader social change beyond policy - Institutional Orientation: Agenda-building specifically targets governmental institutions - Success Metrics: Movement theory measures social change; agenda-building measures institutional access - Time Horizons: Movements may have longer-term change goals than specific policy access

    11) Critiques & Debates¶

    Major Criticisms¶

    Elite Bias Concerns: - Critics argue that agenda-building theory overemphasizes role of policy entrepreneurs and organized interests - Limited attention to how resource inequalities affect agenda access opportunities - Insufficient consideration of how institutional barriers disadvantage certain groups and perspectives - Theory may normalize elite-dominated policy processes rather than critically examining democratic deficits

    Process Complexity Underestimation: - Real agenda-building processes may be more chaotic and unpredictable than theory suggests - Multiple streams model may oversimplify complex interactions between problem, policy, and political factors - Limited attention to feedback loops and recursive processes in agenda-building - Difficulty in operationalizing and measuring key theoretical concepts in practice

    Institutional Determinism: - Theory may overemphasize institutional rules and procedures while underestimating agency - Limited attention to how actors can change institutions through agenda-building efforts - Insufficient consideration of informal institutions and norms affecting agenda processes - May not adequately account for institutional variation and change over time

    Cultural and Contextual Limitations: - Theory developed primarily from American political system observations - Limited cross-cultural validation and application in different political systems - Insufficient attention to how cultural values and norms affect agenda-building processes - May not translate well to non-democratic or developing country contexts

    Contemporary Debates¶

    Digital Age Transformations: - Social Media Impact: How digital platforms change traditional agenda-building processes - Information Speed: Whether faster information flows accelerate or complicate agenda-building - Democratization vs. Fragmentation: Whether digital media democratize agenda access or create fragmentation - Algorithmic Influence: How automated systems affect which issues gain attention and access

    Globalization Effects: - Transnational Issues: How global problems challenge traditional national agenda-building processes - International Influence: Role of international organizations and foreign actors in domestic agendas - Policy Transfer: How agenda-building strategies and solutions spread across national boundaries - Multi-Level Governance: Agenda-building in complex, multi-level governmental systems

    Crisis Governance: - Emergency Powers: How crises change normal agenda-building rules and procedures - Speed vs. Deliberation: Tension between rapid response needs and democratic deliberation - Expertise vs. Democracy: Role of technical experts versus democratic participation in crisis agenda-building - Recovery Processes: How post-crisis periods affect agenda-building dynamics and opportunities

    Representation and Inclusion: - Participatory Innovations: How new forms of citizen engagement affect traditional agenda-building - Marginalized Voices: Strategies for including historically excluded groups in agenda-building processes - Intersectionality: How multiple identities and oppressions affect agenda access opportunities - Digital Divides: Whether online agenda-building advantages certain groups over others

    Methodological Challenges¶

    Causal Inference: - Difficulty establishing clear causal relationships in complex political processes - Challenge of controlling for multiple variables affecting agenda outcomes - Problem of selection bias in case studies of successful agenda-building efforts - Temporal complexity making it difficult to identify precise causal mechanisms

    Measurement Issues: - Difficulty operationalizing concepts like "policy windows" and "stream coupling" - Challenge of measuring policy entrepreneur investment and strategic activity - Problem of defining clear boundaries around agenda-building episodes - Subjective elements in determining agenda "success" versus "failure"

    Comparative Analysis: - Limited standardization in research methods across different studies - Difficulty comparing agenda-building processes across different institutional contexts - Challenge of accounting for cultural and historical differences in comparative research - Problem of case selection bias affecting generalizability of findings

    Theoretical Development Directions¶

    Multi-Level Integration: - Nested Agendas: How local, national, and international agendas interact and influence each other - Temporal Dynamics: Better understanding of how agenda-building processes unfold over extended periods - Network Approaches: Incorporating network analysis to understand complex stakeholder relationships - Institutional Change: How agenda-building processes contribute to institutional evolution and reform

    Technology and Innovation: - Digital Transformation: How new technologies change traditional agenda-building strategies and processes - Data Analytics: Using big data to understand agenda-building patterns and predict outcomes - Artificial Intelligence: Potential impacts of AI on agenda-building processes and democratic governance - Platform Governance: How digital platforms become new venues for agenda-building activity

    Equity and Justice: - Inclusive Agenda-Building: Developing theories and practices for more inclusive agenda-building processes - Power Analysis: Better incorporation of power dynamics and structural inequalities in agenda-building theory - Participatory Democracy: Integration with theories of participatory and deliberative democracy - Global Justice: How agenda-building theory applies to global governance and international justice issues

def simulate_agenda_building_process():
    """Simulate agenda-building process over time"""

    np.random.seed(42)

    # Time periods (months)
    months = range(1, 37)  # 3-year process

    # Initialize tracking variables
    agenda_data = []

    # Simulate key variables over time
    for month in months:
        # Problem severity (increases with focusing events)
        base_severity = 3.0
        if month in [8, 15, 28]:  # Focusing events
            severity_boost = np.random.uniform(1.5, 2.5)
        else:
            severity_boost = 0

        problem_severity = base_severity + severity_boost + np.random.normal(0, 0.3)

        # Policy entrepreneur activity (responsive to windows)
        base_activity = 2.5
        if month > 6:  # Entrepreneurial learning and network building
            base_activity += 0.5
        if month in [9, 16, 29]:  # Response to focusing events
            activity_boost = np.random.uniform(1.0, 2.0)
        else:
            activity_boost = 0

        entrepreneur_activity = base_activity + activity_boost + np.random.normal(0, 0.4)

        # Political opportunity (varies with electoral cycles and events)
        if month < 12:
            political_opportunity = 3.0 + np.random.normal(0, 0.5)
        elif month < 24:  # Election year
            political_opportunity = 4.5 + np.random.normal(0, 0.6)
        else:  # Post-election
            political_opportunity = 3.8 + np.random.normal(0, 0.5)

        # Coalition strength (builds over time)
        coalition_base = 2.0 + (month * 0.05)  # Gradual building
        if entrepreneur_activity > 4.0:  # Entrepreneurial mobilization effect
            coalition_boost = 0.8
        else:
            coalition_boost = 0

        coalition_strength = coalition_base + coalition_boost + np.random.normal(0, 0.3)

        # Institutional attention (function of other variables)
        attention_factors = (
            0.3 * problem_severity +
            0.25 * entrepreneur_activity +
            0.25 * political_opportunity +
            0.2 * coalition_strength
        )
        institutional_attention = attention_factors + np.random.normal(0, 0.5)

        # Policy window (dichotomous - opens when factors align)
        window_threshold = 4.5
        combined_score = (problem_severity + political_opportunity + entrepreneur_activity) / 3
        policy_window = 1 if combined_score > window_threshold else 0

        # Agenda access (probability increases with all factors)
        access_probability = np.clip(
            0.1 + 0.15 * (institutional_attention - 2) + 0.2 * policy_window, 
            0, 1
        )
        agenda_access = 1 if np.random.random() < access_probability else 0

        # Store data
        agenda_data.append({
            'month': month,
            'problem_severity': np.clip(problem_severity, 1, 7),
            'entrepreneur_activity': np.clip(entrepreneur_activity, 1, 7),
            'political_opportunity': np.clip(political_opportunity, 1, 7),
            'coalition_strength': np.clip(coalition_strength, 1, 7),
            'institutional_attention': np.clip(institutional_attention, 1, 7),
            'policy_window': policy_window,
            'agenda_access': agenda_access
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(agenda_data)

def analyze_agenda_building_success(data):
    """Analyze factors contributing to agenda-building success"""

    # Success indicators
    total_access_attempts = data['agenda_access'].sum()
    success_rate = data['agenda_access'].mean()

    # Window utilization
    windows_opened = data['policy_window'].sum()
    windows_utilized = data[(data['policy_window'] == 1) & (data['agenda_access'] == 1)].shape[0]
    window_utilization_rate = windows_utilized / windows_opened if windows_opened > 0 else 0

    # Factor correlations with success
    success_correlations = {
        'problem_severity': data['problem_severity'].corr(data['agenda_access']),
        'entrepreneur_activity': data['entrepreneur_activity'].corr(data['agenda_access']),
        'political_opportunity': data['political_opportunity'].corr(data['agenda_access']),
        'coalition_strength': data['coalition_strength'].corr(data['agenda_access']),
        'institutional_attention': data['institutional_attention'].corr(data['agenda_access'])
    }

    # Time to first access
    first_access = data[data['agenda_access'] == 1]['month'].min()

    results = {
        'overall_metrics': {
            'total_access_attempts': total_access_attempts,
            'success_rate': success_rate,
            'windows_opened': windows_opened,
            'window_utilization_rate': window_utilization_rate,
            'months_to_first_access': first_access
        },
        'success_factors': success_correlations,
        'monthly_data': data
    }

    return results
